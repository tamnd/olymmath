On the branches of a large oak, several crows are sitting. At a signal, they start moving. Each minute, one of the crows is driven away by its neighbors sitting on the same branch, and this crow flies to the next higher branch; if there are no higher branches, the crow flies away. All branches are located at different heights. Prove that the time it takes for the process to end (i.e., there will be no more than one crow on each branch) does not depend on the order of the flights, but only on the initial arrangement of the crows.

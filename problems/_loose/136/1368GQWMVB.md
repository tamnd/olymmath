Two boys were given the following task: You need to figure out into which three different (integer) factors we factored 900. To do this, we wrote the middle factor of the factorization (in size) on a slip of paper, and we will give this to Béla, while on another slip of paper, we wrote how much smaller the smallest factor is compared to one of the other factors, which Dezső will receive. Of course, you must not reveal the numbers you received to each other. You will take turns speaking, but you must either state the factors of the factorization or only say: "I don't know yet."

They received the slips of paper, and Béla started with "I don't know yet," then after a brief thought, Dezső also stated the same. Then both of them engaged in a longer calculation, and after a few more "I don't know yet," Dezső said: "Now I know," and he stated the correct factorization.

Can the factorization be figured out from what was said, even without knowing what numbers were on the slips of paper?

The 76th question: Let the set $P=\left\{p_{1}, p_{2}, \ldots, p_{k}\right\}$, where $p_{1}, p_{2}, \ldots, p_{k}$ are distinct prime numbers. $\mathrm{f}(\mathrm{P})$ represents the maximum number of consecutive positive integers such that each of these integers is divisible by at least one prime in $\mathrm{P}$. Prove: $k \leq f(P)k$ when the left equality holds.

When the left equality holds, it means that the maximum number of consecutive positive integers, each divisible by at least one prime in $P$, is exactly $k$.

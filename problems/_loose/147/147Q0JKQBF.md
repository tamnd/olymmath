Let $\mathrm{n}$, $\mathrm{p}$, and $\mathrm{q}$ be positive integers, $\mathrm{n}>\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q}$. If $\mathrm{x}_{0}$, $\mathrm{x}_{1}$, $\cdots$, $\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{n}}$ are integers satisfying the following conditions: (1) $x_{0}=x_{n}=0$; (2) For each integer $i(1 \leq i \leq n)$, either $x_{i}-x_{i-1}=p$ or $x_{i}-$ $x_{i-1}=-q$. Prove: There exists a pair of indices $(i, j)$ such that $i<j$, $(i, j) \neq(0, n)$, and $x_{i}=x_{j}$.

Thomas presents Jürgen with the following problem:

(1) In my class, exactly 15 students are involved in extracurricular sports, and only the sports of football, swimming, gymnastics, or athletics are practiced.

(2) Each of the mentioned sports is practiced by at least one student.

(3) No student practices more than two of these sports.

(4) Every student who practices swimming or athletics also practices a second sport.

(5) Exactly 3 students practice both football and swimming, exactly 2 students practice both swimming and athletics; no student practices both football and gymnastics.

(6) The number of football players is greater than the number of swimmers, which in turn is greater than the number of gymnasts, and this is greater than the number of athletes.

(7) The number of football players is equal to the sum of the number of gymnasts and the number of athletes.

In (6) and (7), "football player," "swimmer," etc., each refer to a student who practices the respective sport (alone or in addition to a second sport).

Give the number of football players, swimmers, gymnasts, and athletes in my class!

After some consideration, Jürgen says that this problem is not uniquely solvable. Determine all solutions to this problem.

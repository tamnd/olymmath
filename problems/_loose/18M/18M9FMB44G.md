One non-multiplicative function. For any natural number $n$, denote by $r_{s}(n)$ the number of solutions to the equation

$$
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{s}^{2}=n
$$

in integers $x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{n}$. Let us further set $f_{s}=(2 s)^{-1} r_{s}(n)$. It is known that for $s=1,2,4,8$ the function $f_{s}$ is multiplicative, that is, for any pair of coprime natural numbers $m$ and $n$, the equality $f_{s}(m n)=f_{s}(m) f_{s}(n)$ holds. Prove that $f_{s}$ is not multiplicative for any other value of $s$.

Let the function $f(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x}-\frac{1}{2} a x^{2}-x$.
(1) If the function $f(x)$ is monotonically increasing on $\mathbf{R}$, find the value of $a$;
(2) When $a>1$,
(i) Prove: The function $f(x)$ has two critical points $x_{1}, x_{2}\left(x_{1}<x_{2}\right)$, and $x_{2}-x_{1}$ increases as $a$ increases; (ii) Under the conclusion of (i), prove:
$$
f\left(x_{2}\right)<1+\frac{\sin x_{2}-x_{2}}{2}
$$

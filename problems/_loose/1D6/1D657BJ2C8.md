(Bertrand's Paradox.) A well-known "classical" paradox proposed by Bertrand serves as a good illustration of the fact that when solving probability problems (and, in particular, geometric probability problems, with which the considered paradox is associated), one must clearly formulate the probability model and clearly define what is meant by expressions such as "a randomly chosen point," "a randomly selected object," etc.

The problem presented by Bertrand in his textbook [35] and the different answers (which is where the "paradox" lies) found by different methods, were interpreted by him as evidence that in the case of probabilistic experiments with an infinite number of outcomes, it is fundamentally impossible to give a definite answer to the question of the values of probabilities of certain events.

Bertrand's problem is formulated as follows: in the circle $\left\{(x, y): x^{2}+y^{2} \leqslant 1\right\}$, a chord $AB$ with endpoints $A$ and $B$ lying on the circle's circumference is "randomly chosen" (?!). What is the probability that the length $|AB|$ of the "randomly chosen chord" $AB$ is less than $r$?

We will present three possible formulations of this problem.

(a) We will interpret the phrase "chord $AB$ is chosen randomly" to mean that points $A$ and $B$ are independently chosen on the circle with a uniform distribution on it.

Show that in this case

$$
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{a}}(|A B|<r)=1 / 3
$$

(b) Each chord $AB$ is completely determined by the position of the point $M$, which is the foot of the perpendicular dropped from the center of the circle to the chord. We will interpret the phrase "chord is chosen randomly" to mean that the point $M$ has a uniform distribution on the circle.

Show that in this case

$$
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{b}}(|A B|<r)=1 / 4
$$

(c) The length of the chord $AB$ does not depend on its direction but only on its distance from the center of the circle. It is therefore natural to assume that the chord has a certain direction, say, parallel to the horizontal diameter $CD$, and that the point $M$ of intersection with the vertical diameter $EF$ (perpendicular to $CD$) has a uniform distribution on $EF$.

Show that

$$
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{c}}(|A B|<r)=1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} (\approx 0.13)
$$

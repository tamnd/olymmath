Let's denote by $P(n)$ the product of the digits of the number $n$. For example: $P(58)=5 \times 8=40$ and $P(319)=3 \times 1 \times 9=27$.

(a) Which natural numbers less than 1000 have a product of their digits equal to 12, that is: the natural numbers $n<1000$ such that $P(n)=12$?

(b) How many natural numbers less than 199 satisfy $P(n)=0$? That is: have the product of their digits equal to 0?

(c) Which natural numbers less than 200 satisfy the inequality $37 < P(n) < 45$?

(d) Among the numbers from 1 to 250, which number has the largest product of its digits?

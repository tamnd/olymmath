We will consider that the vertices of triangle $ABC$ follow each other in a positive - counterclockwise - order. For any two rays $\alpha$ and $\beta$, the symbol ( $\widehat{\alpha, \beta}$ ) will denote the angle by which ray $\alpha$ must be rotated counterclockwise to coincide with ray $\beta$. Let $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{1}^{\prime}$ be two rays emanating from $A$, for which $\left(A \widehat{B, \alpha_{1}}\right)=\left(\widehat{\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{1}^{\prime}}\right)=\left(\widehat{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}, A C}\right)=\frac{1}{3} \angle A, \alpha_{2}$ and $\alpha_{2}^{\prime}$ be rays for which $\left(A \widehat{B, \alpha_{2}}\right)=\left(\widehat{\alpha_{2}, \alpha_{2}^{\prime}}\right)=\left(\widehat{\alpha_{2}, A} C\right)=\frac{1}{3}(\angle A+2 \pi)$, and finally, $ \alpha_{3}$ and $\alpha_{3}^{\prime}$ be rays for which $\left(\widehat{A, \alpha_{3}}\right)=\left(\widehat{\alpha_{3}, \alpha_{3}^{\prime}}\right)=\left(\widehat{\alpha_{3}^{\prime}, A} C\right)=\frac{1}{3}(\angle A+4 \pi)\left(\alpha_{i}, \alpha_{i}^{\prime}\right.$, where $i=1,2,3$, will be called trisectors of the first, second, and third kind, respectively. Similarly, for vertices $B$ and $C$, we define $\beta_{j}, \beta_{j}^{\prime}$ and $\gamma_{k}, \gamma_{k}^{\prime}(j, k=1,2,3)$. By $\alpha_{i} \beta_{j} \gamma_{k}$ we will denote the triangle formed by the intersection of the respective lines (not rays) $\alpha_{i}$ and $\beta_{j}^{\prime}, \beta_{j}$ and $\gamma_{k}^{\prime}, \gamma_{k}$ and $\alpha_{i}^{\prime}$. Prove that for all $i, j, k$ such that $i+j+k-1$ is not divisible by three, the triangles $\alpha_{i} \beta_{j} \gamma_{k}$ are equilateral, their sides are parallel, and their vertices are located on nine lines, six on each line (the full Morley's theorem).

Let $M>0$ be a real number and $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function with the property that $$ |f(x+y)-f(x)-f(y)| \le M,~\forall x,y \in \mathbb{R}. $$ Prove that that for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ the limit $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{f(nx)}{n}$ exists, denoted by $g(x),$ and the function $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ thus defined is continuous at $0.$ Deduce that the limit $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{f(x)}{x}$ exists and is finite.

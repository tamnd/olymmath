(a) If a random variable $X$ is exponentially distributed, then the lack of aftereffect property holds:

$$
\mathrm{P}(X>x+y \mid X>x)=\mathrm{P}(X>y), x, y \geqslant 0
$$

Show that if for a non-negative extended (i.e., with values in $[0, \infty]$) random variable $X$ the stated property holds, then one of the three cases must be true: either $\mathrm{P}(X=0)=1$, or $\mathrm{P}(X=\infty)=1$, or $X$ has an exponential distribution.
(b) Suppose now $\mathrm{P}(X>x)>0, x \geqslant 0$, and for any $y \geqslant 0$ there exists a positive limit

$$
\mathrm{P}(X>x+y \mid X>x) \rightarrow f(y), x \rightarrow \infty .
$$

Show that $f(y)=e^{-\lambda y}$ for some $\lambda \geqslant 0$ and $\mathrm{P}(X>x)=e^{-\lambda x+o(x)}$ as $x$ grows (lack of aftereffect at infinity).

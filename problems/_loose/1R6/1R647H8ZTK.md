Around a circular table, six people are seated. Each one is wearing a hat. Between each two people, there is a partition so that each can see the hats of the three people opposite, but cannot see the hat of the person to their left, the person to their right, or their own. They all know that three of the hats are white and three are black. They also know that each of them is capable of making any logical deduction that is feasible. We start with one of the six people and ask, "Can you deduce the color of any hat that you cannot see?" Once they have answered (everyone hears the answer), we move to the person on their left and ask the same question, and so on. Prove that one of the first three will answer "Yes."

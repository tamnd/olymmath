Let $ABC$ be a triangle and $(MN)$ a line parallel to $(BC)$ with $M \in (AB)$ and $N \in (AC)$. $(BN)$ and $(CM)$ intersect at $P$. Let $Q$ be the second point of intersection of the circumcircles of $BMP$ and $CPN$. Show that $\widehat{BAP} = \widehat{CAQ}$.

It is interesting to note that (by the same proof), Ceva's theorem generalizes IN THE FORWARD DIRECTION to a polygon. Its converse is generally false, but a similar principle can still be applied: we can reassemble one by one the pieces of a figure where the sine condition is satisfied.

Consider triangles $X_{1}A_{1}B_{1}, X_{2}A_{2}B_{2}, \ldots, X_{n}A_{n}B_{n}$ satisfying $\hat{X}_{1} + \cdots + \hat{X}_{n} = 360^{\circ}$ and $\frac{\sin \hat{A}_{1}}{\sin \hat{B}_{1}} \cdots \frac{\sin \hat{A}_{n}}{\sin \hat{B}_{n}} = 1$. Place the first triangle with vertex $X_{1} = X$. Then glue the second triangle (or a scaled version) to the side $[XB_{1}]$ of the first triangle: we get $X_{2} = X$ and $A_{2} = B_{1}$. Continue in this manner until finally obtaining $X_{1} = X_{2} = \cdots = X_{n} = X, A_{2} = B_{1}, A_{3} = B_{2}, \ldots, A_{n} = B_{n-1}$. Then, the condition on the angles ensures that $[XB_{n}) = [XA_{1})$ and the condition on the sines ensures that (using the law of sines successively in all triangles) $XB_{n} = XA_{1}$. Hence, $B_{n} = A_{1}$: we can construct a closed figure under these conditions. This is the principle of living puzzles.

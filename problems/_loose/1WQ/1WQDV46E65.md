(Austria, 73). In space, there are $2 n$ distinct points $A_{1}, A_{2}, \ldots, A_{2 n}(n>1)$. No three of them lie on the same line. Let $M$ be the set of $\left(n^{2}+1\right)$ segments whose endpoints are the given points. Prove that there exists at least one triangle with vertices at some points $A_{r}, A_{s}, A_{t}$, all sides of which belong to the set $M$. Prove that if the number of elements in the set $M$ does not exceed $n^{2}$, then such a triangle may not exist.

You, an inhabitant of the island of knights, liars, and normal people, are in love with the king's daughter Margozita and want to marry her. The king does not want his daughter to marry a normal person and gives her paternal advice: "Believe me, dear, you really shouldn't marry a normal person. Normal people are capricious, changeable, and you can't rely on them. With them, you never know where you stand. One day he tells you the truth, the next day he lies. What's good about that? A knight, on the other hand, is as reliable as a rock. With him, you always know where you stand. With a liar, you also feel quite secure: whatever he says, you just need to replace his statement with the opposite, and you know how things really stand. I believe that a person should have some principles that they unwaveringly follow. If a person finds the highest pleasure in telling the truth, let them tell the truth. If they think that lying is above all, let them lie. And what do these decent normal people represent? Just so-so: half and half, neither truth nor lies. No, they are not for you!"

Now, assume that you are not a normal person (and therefore have a chance to marry the king's daughter). To get the king's consent to your marriage with his daughter, you need to convince him that you are not a normal person. The king grants you an audience, during which you can make as many statements as you like. The task is divided into two parts.

a) How many true statements will you need to convince the king that his future son-in-law is not a normal person?

b) How many false statements will you need to convince the king that his future son-in-law is not a normal person?

(Emphasize that in both cases, we are talking about the minimum number of statements.)

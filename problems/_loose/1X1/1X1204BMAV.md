It is known for a triangle that the center of the circumscribed circle and the orthocenter are either both inside the triangle, on its boundary, or outside the triangle.

To be proven for a tetrahedron is the following analogous theorem:

If the altitudes of a tetrahedron intersect at a single point and this orthocenter lies between the foot and the midpoint of each altitude, then the center of the circumscribed sphere is inside the tetrahedron. If the orthocenter bisects one of the altitudes, then the center of the circumscribed sphere lies on one of the faces of the tetrahedron. If the orthocenter lies outside the segment between the foot and the midpoint of one of the altitudes, then the center of the circumscribed sphere is outside the tetrahedron.

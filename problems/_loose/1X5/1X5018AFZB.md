It is known that by forming a 4-row, 4-column multiplication table from the numbers 1, 3, 7, 9 (i.e., writing the product of two such numbers in the cell where the row labeled by the first factor and the column labeled by the second factor intersect), the last digit in every row (and column) of the table will be the digits 1, 3, 7, 9 in some order. Show that if we were to form a 40-row, 40-column multiplication table for the multiplication of one- and two-digit numbers ending in 1, 3, 7, and 9, then the last two digits of the products in every row (and column) would be entirely different two-digit numbers (including the numbers 01, 03, 07, 09).

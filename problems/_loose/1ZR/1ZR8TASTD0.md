The fortieth question: Given a prime $p>3,\left(a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{p-1}\right)$ is a permutation of $1, 2, \ldots, p-1$, and satisfies $p \mid\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-2} a_{i} a_{i+1}\right)$. Let the number of such permutations be $K$. Prove: $K \equiv p-1(\bmod p(p-1))$.

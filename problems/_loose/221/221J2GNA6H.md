For which integers $n \geqslant 1$ is the polynomial $1+x^{2}+x^{4}+\cdots+x^{2 n-2}$ divisible by the polynomial $1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{n-1}$?

D'Alembert-Gauss Theorem We admit the following theorem (also known as the fundamental theorem of algebra):

Theorem 3.48. Every non-constant polynomial in $\mathbb{C}[X]$ has at least one root. We say that $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed.

By induction on the degree, we deduce:

Corollary 3.49. Let $\mathrm{P} \in \mathbb{C}[X]$. Then $\mathrm{P}$ can be written in the form:

$$
P(x)=c\left(x-\alpha_{1}\right)^{m_{1}} \cdots\left(x-\alpha_{k}\right)^{m_{k}}
$$

where $c, \alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{k}$ are complex numbers and $m_{1}, \ldots, m_{k}$ are strictly positive integers.

We finally define complex conjugation, which will be useful when we want to determine the irreducible polynomials of $\mathbb{R}[X]$.

Definition 3.50. Let $z=a+b i \in \mathbb{C}$. We define its conjugate $\bar{z}$ by $\bar{z}=a-b i$.

Proposition 3.51. For all $w, z \in \mathbb{C}$, we have $\overline{w z}=\bar{w} \cdot \bar{z}$.

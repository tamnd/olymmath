In the Land of Math, $n \geqslant 3$ cities are connected by $n$ airlines. Each airline offers two-way connections, following an odd-length cycle: for each airline, there exists an odd integer $m$ and cities $v_{1}, \ldots, v_{m}$ such that the connections offered are exactly $v_{1} v_{2}, \ldots, v_{m-1} v_{m}, v_{m} v_{1}$. Show that it is possible, during a trip, to visit an odd number of cities and never take a plane from the same airline twice.

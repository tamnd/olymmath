Let the three sides of $\triangle A B C$ be $B C=a, C A=b, A B=c, p=\frac{1}{2}(a+b+c)$. Let $m_{a}, h_{a}$ be the lengths of the median and altitude from $B C$ respectively; $t_{a}, t^{\prime}{ }_{a}$ be the lengths of the internal and external angle bisectors of the angle opposite to $B C$ respectively. Prove the following formulas:
(I) $m_{a}=\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{2\left(b^{2}+c^{2}\right)-a^{2}}$;
( II ) $t_{a}=\frac{2}{b+c} \sqrt{b \cdot c \cdot p(p-a)}$;
(III) $t^{\prime}{ }_{a}=\frac{2}{|b-c|} \sqrt{b \cdot c \cdot(p-b)(p-c)}$;
( IV ) $h_{a}=\frac{2}{a} \sqrt{p(p-a)(p-b)(p-c)}$.

Let $k_{1}, \ldots, k_{n}$ be solid spheres, each including its boundary points. These spheres are arbitrarily placed in space; it is also allowed that they intersect or touch each other. The union of the $k_{i}$ has the volume $V$.

Prove that under these conditions, it is always possible to select a subset of the spheres $k_{i}$ such that no two of the selected spheres have a common point and that the union of the selected spheres has a volume $U \geq \frac{1}{27} V$.

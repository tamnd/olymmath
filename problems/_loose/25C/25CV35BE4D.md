Someone drew a circle with radius $d$ around each of the points $A, B, C$ which are $d$ units apart, then divided the shorter arcs $BC$, $CA$, $AB$ into 4 equal parts with points $K_{1}, K_{2}, K_{3}$, $L_{1}, L_{2}, L_{3}$, and $M_{1}, M_{2}, M_{3}$, respectively. They found that the circle drawn around point $K_{i}$ with radius $K_{i} L_{i}$ for $i=1,2,3$ passes through point $M_{i}$ and the second intersection point $A^{\prime}$ of the circles around $B$ and $C$. Based on this, they claim that if $K, L, M$ are points on the shorter arcs $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$ respectively, such that the arcs $BK$, $CL$, $AM$ are equal; then the circle drawn around $K$ with radius $KL$ always passes through $M$ and $A^{\prime}$. Is this justified? Is the claim true?

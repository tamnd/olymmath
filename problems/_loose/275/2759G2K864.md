The set of lines and points featured in Desargues' theorem is an example of what is known as a configuration: on each line there is the same number of points, and through each point there passes the same number of lines. In the Desargues configuration, we have 10 points: $A, B, C, A^{\prime}, B^{\prime}, C^{\prime}, X, Y, Z, S$ and 10 lines: $B C, B^{\prime} C^{\prime}, C A, C^{\prime} A^{\prime}, A B, A^{\prime} B^{\prime}, A A^{\prime}, B B^{\prime}, C C^{\prime}, Y Z$. Each line contains three points (from the ten listed) and through each point pass three lines ${ }^{1}$.

Consider a spatial pentahedron in which no four points out of the five lie in the same plane. By drawing a line through every pair of points and a plane through every triplet, we obtain a set of lines and planes. Prove that the intersection of this figure with a plane that intersects all the lines of the figure is a Desargues configuration.

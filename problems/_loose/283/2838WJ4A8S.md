(i) $)^{-}$ Prove a simple generalization of Dilworth's theorem for any infinite poset $P$: If $P$ does not contain an antichain of order $k \in \mathbb{N}$, then $P$ can be partitioned into fewer than $k$ chains. (It suffices to prove this for countable $P$.)
(ii)- Find a poset that does not have infinitely many antichains but cannot be partitioned into finitely many chains.
(iii) For posets that do not contain infinite chains, derive from Theorem 8.4.8 the following Erdös-Menger type generalization of Dilworth's theorem: Every such poset contains a partition into chains $\mathcal{C}$ such that some antichain intersects all chains in $\mathcal{C}$.

In the cells of an $n \times n$ chessboard, some numbers are arranged in such a way that for any arrangement of $n$ rooks on the board, no two of which attack each other, the sum of the numbers covered by these rooks is always the same. Let the number at the intersection of the $i$-th row and the $j$-th column of the board be denoted by $a_{ij}$. Prove that there exist two sets of numbers $x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, y_{2}, \ldots, y_{n}$ such that $a_{ij} = x_{i} + y_{j}$.

Two players play a game on a line of 2000 squares. Each player in turn puts either $\mathrm{S}$ or $\mathrm{O}$ into an empty square. The game stops when three adjacent squares contain $S, O, S$ in that order and the last player wins. If
all the squares are filled without getting $\mathrm{S}, \mathrm{O}, \mathrm{S}$, then the game is drawn. Show that the second player can always win.

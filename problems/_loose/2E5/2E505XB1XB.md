Let $A$ denote the set of all sequences (of arbitrary finite or infinite length) $\left\{a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots\right\}$ formed by the elements of $\{1,2, \cdots, 2017\}$. If the first several consecutive terms of sequence $M$ are the terms of sequence $T$, then we say that sequence $M$ "starts with" sequence $T$. A set $S \subset A$ composed of some finite sequences satisfies: for any infinite sequence $M$ in set $A$, there exists a unique sequence $T$ in $S$ such that $M$ starts with $T$. Which of the following three conclusions is true:
(1) $S$ must be a finite set;
(2) $S$ must be an infinite set;
(3) $S$ can be either a finite set or an infinite set?
Prove your conclusion.

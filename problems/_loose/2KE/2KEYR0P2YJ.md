Consider sixteen points, which are the centers of all possible inscribed and exscribed circles for four triangles formed by four intersecting lines on a plane. Prove that these sixteen points can be divided into four quartets in two ways such that each quartet lies on one circle. The centers of these circles, when divided by the first method, lie on one straight line, and when divided by the second method, lie on another straight line. These lines are perpendicular and intersect at the Miquel point - the common point of the circumcircles of the four triangles.

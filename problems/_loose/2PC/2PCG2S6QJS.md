(2001 Hunan Provincial Competition Question) In a rhombus $A_{1} B_{1} C D$ with side length 1, the two diagonals intersect at $A_{2}$. Through $A_{2}$, draw $A_{2} B_{2} / / A_{1} B_{1}$ intersecting $B_{1} C$ at $B_{2}$. Connect $B_{2} D$ intersecting $A_{1} C$ at $A_{3}$. Through $A_{3}$, draw $A_{3} B_{3} / / A_{1} B_{1}$ intersecting $B_{1} C$ at $B_{3}, \cdots$, and continue this process to get $A_{n} B_{n}$. Taking $B_{1}$ as the origin and the line $B_{1} C$ as the $x$-axis, establish a Cartesian coordinate system. Let $\frac{1}{A_{n} B_{n}}$ be the radius of a series of circles $T_{n}(n=1,2, \cdots)$ with centers on the $y$-axis, and these circles are externally tangent to each other (i.e., $T_{k}$ is tangent to $T_{k+1}$, $\left.k=1,2, \cdots\right)$, and if circle $T_{1}$ is tangent to the parabola $y=x^{2}$. Prove that all circles $T_{n}(n=1,2, \cdots)$ are tangent to the parabola $y=x^{2}$.

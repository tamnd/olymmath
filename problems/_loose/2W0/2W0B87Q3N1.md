(England, 75). Prove that for any value of $n \in \mathbf{N}$ there exists a unique set of numbers $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{u}$, satisfying the equation

$$
\left(1-x_{1}\right)^{2}+\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)^{2}+\ldots+\left(x_{n-1}-x_{n}\right)^{2}+x_{n}^{2}=1 /(n+1)
$$

The shortest period of time after which we see the Moon in the same position relative to the stars, or the time it takes for the Moon to rotate exactly $360^{\circ}$ relative to the center of the Earth, is on average 27 days 7 hours 43 minutes. The same period of time relative to the Sun is 365 days 6 hours 9 minutes, which is the time it takes for the Earth to complete one orbit around the Sun (this is the so-called sidereal, or star-related, month or year). How often would the phases of the Moon repeat if the Earth's orbit around the Sun and the Moon's orbit around the Earth were circular, their motion were uniform, and the planes of the two orbits coincided, that is, how often would the centers of the Sun, Earth, and Moon fall in a straight line in this order (i.e., what would be the so-called synodic, or conjunction, month)? Would new moons, first and last quarters also repeat at the same intervals? (The Moon orbits the Earth in the same sense as the Earth orbits the Sun.)

In some English-speaking countries, temperature is measured in degrees Fahrenheit. An English schoolboy, observing a thermometer in a glass of cooling water, noticed that it cooled by $10^{\circ} \mathrm{F}$. He became curious about how much heat was released. In books, he found the following formula that allows calculating the answer to his question: $Q=4200 \cdot V \cdot \Delta T$, where $V$ is the volume of water in liters, $\Delta T$ is the change in its temperature. However, the change in temperature in this formula must be substituted in degrees Celsius. Degrees Fahrenheit are related to degrees Celsius by the following relationship ${ }^{\circ} F={ }^{\circ} C \cdot \frac{9}{5}+32$. What result should he get if he had 1 liter of water at his disposal?

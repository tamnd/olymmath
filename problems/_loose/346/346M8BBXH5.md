88 In the $x O y$ coordinate plane, given the circle $A_{0}:(x-1)^{2}+(y-1)^{2}=1^{2}$. In the first quadrant, construct circle $A_{1}$ tangent to both the $x$ and $y$ axes, and externally tangent to circle $A_{0}$. Then, construct circles $A_{i}(i=2,3,4, \cdots)$ successively, each externally tangent to both circle $A_{0}$ and $A_{i-1}$, and tangent to the $x$-axis. Let $L_{i}$ be the circumference of circle $A_{i}$, and $L=\lim _{n \rightarrow+\infty}\left(L_{1}+L_{2}+\right.$ $\left.L_{3}+\cdots+L_{n}\right)$, prove:
$$
\frac{1}{2}<\frac{L}{2 \pi}<\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} .
$$

Having solved an equation with one unknown, I performed a check by substitution; in doing so, did I only mean to ensure that no gross error was made in the calculations, or could I have had another, more significant goal in mind?

Definition. Two equations are called equivalent if all the roots of one equation are also roots of the other, and vice versa, with the multiplicities of the known roots being the same in both equations. For example, the equations $(x-2)(x-3)=0$ and $(x-2)(x-3)^{2}=0$ are not equivalent, despite the fact that both are satisfied by the numbers 2 and 3 and only these numbers, because in the first equation $x=3$ is a single root, while in the second it is a double root.

Let $n$ be a positive odd integer.

1) Determine, in terms of $n$, the largest integer $r$ having the following property: for all real numbers $a_{1}, \ldots, a_{n}$, there exist $r$ integers $1 \leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{r} \leqslant n$ such that for all integers $k, \ell$ satisfying $1 \leqslant k, \ell \leqslant r, a_{i_{k}}-a_{i_{\ell}} \neq 1$.
2) Let $\alpha$ be an irrational number. Determine, in terms of $n$, the largest integer $r$ having the following property: for all real numbers $a_{1}, \ldots, a_{n}$, there exist $r$ integers $1 \leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{r} \leqslant n$ such that for all integers $k, \ell$ satisfying $1 \leqslant k, \ell \leqslant r, a_{i_{k}}-a_{i_{\ell}} \neq 1$ and $a_{i_{k}}-a_{i_{\ell}} \neq \alpha$.

An irrational number is a real number that cannot be written in the form $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b \neq 0$.

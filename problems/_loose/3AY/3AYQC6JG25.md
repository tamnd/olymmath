Place 1 matchstick at one vertex of a square, leaving the other 3 vertices empty. The following operation is allowed: remove any number of matchsticks from a vertex, and simultaneously place matchsticks at the two adjacent vertices, with the total number of matchsticks placed being twice the number removed. Is it possible, after several operations, to have the number of matchsticks at each vertex (in counterclockwise order) be $1,9,8,9$?

For a convex quadrilateral $A B C D$, extend the sides $A B, B C, C D$, and $D A$ to points $B^{\prime}, C^{\prime}, D^{\prime}$, and $A^{\prime}$, respectively. Given that $A B=B B^{\prime}=6, B C=C C^{\prime}=7$, $C D=D D^{\prime}=8$, and $D A=A A^{\prime}=9$. If the area of $A B C D$ is 10, then the area of $A^{\prime} B^{\prime} C^{\prime} D^{\prime}$ is
(A) 20.
(B) 40.
(C) 45.
(D) 50.
(E) 60.
(29th American High School Mathematics Examination, 1978)

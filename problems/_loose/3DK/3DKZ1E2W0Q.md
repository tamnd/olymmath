In $\triangle A B C$, the three lines $A E$, $B D$, $C F$ intersect the opposite sides or their extensions at $E, D, F$, respectively, and the angles they form with the other two sides are $\gamma, \beta^{\prime} ; \alpha, \gamma^{\prime} ; \beta, \alpha^{\prime}$. Then $E, D, F$ are collinear $\Leftrightarrow \frac{\sin \alpha}{\sin \alpha^{\prime}} \cdot \frac{\sin \beta}{\sin \beta^{\prime}} \cdot \frac{\sin \gamma}{\sin \gamma^{\prime}}=1$. (An equivalent form of Menelaus' theorem)

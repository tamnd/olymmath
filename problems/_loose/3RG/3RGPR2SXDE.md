Two Paradoxes. A child can ask a question that will plunge a sophisticated philosopher into deep thought, and we often encounter paradoxes that require a little reflection before they can be explained in simple terms. Here are two examples.

Imagine a person walking to the North Pole. As is well known, the markings on a compass look like this

$$
\mathrm{w}_{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{N}} \mathrm{E}
$$

He reaches the pole and, passing through it, must turn around to look north. Now, to his left is the east, and to his right is the west, and thus the markings on the compass look like this

$$
E{ }_{S}^{N} w
$$

which is absurd.

How can this paradox be explained?

We stand with a child in front of a large mirror that reflects us in full.
- Why does this happen, asks the clever youngster, that when I look in the mirror, my right and left sides switch places, but the top and bottom do not? If the mirror changes sides in the horizontal direction, then why does it not change them in the vertical direction? Why don't I see myself standing on my head?

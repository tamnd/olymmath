Given a set $M$ of 9 different natural numbers $x_{i}$ with $1 \leq x_{i} \leq 60$, $i=1 ; 2 ; \ldots ; 9$.

$S(T)$ denotes for each subset $T$ of $M$ the sum of the numbers contained in $T$, provided that $T$ is not empty (the convention $S(\varnothing):=0$ is order-preserving; i.e., for any subsets $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$: $\left.T_{1} \subseteq T_{2} \Rightarrow S\left(T_{1}\right) \leq S\left(T_{2}\right)\right)$.

Prove that there are at least two disjoint subsets $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$ for which $S\left(T_{1}\right)=S\left(T_{2}\right)$ holds.

We will call a tetrahedron equifacial if all its faces are equal triangles, or, equivalently, if the opposite edges of the tetrahedron are pairwise equal. Prove that for a tetrahedron to be equifacial, it is necessary and sufficient that any of the following conditions be satisfied:

a) the sums of the planar angles at any three vertices of the tetrahedron are equal to $180^{\circ}$;
b) the sums of the planar angles at some two vertices of the tetrahedron are equal to $180^{\circ}$ and, in addition, some two opposite edges are equal;

c) the sum of the planar angles at some vertex of the tetrahedron is equal to $180^{\circ}$ and, in addition, there are two pairs of equal opposite edges in the tetrahedron;

d) the equality $\widehat{A B C}=\widehat{A D C}=\widehat{B A D}=\widehat{B C D}$ holds, where $A B C D$ is the given tetrahedron;

e) all faces are of equal area;

f) the centers of the inscribed and circumscribed spheres coincide;

g) the segments connecting the midpoints of opposite edges are perpendicular;

h) the centroid and the center of the circumscribed sphere coincide;

i) the centroid and the center of the inscribed sphere coincide.

(2004 Tianjin Preliminary Competition) Given that $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$ is an arithmetic sequence, $d$ is the common difference and is not equal to $0$, $a_{1}$ and $d$ are both real numbers, and the sum of the first $n$ terms is denoted as $S_{n}$. Let the set $A=$ $\left\{\left.\left(a_{n}, \frac{S_{n}}{n}\right) \right\rvert n \in \mathbf{N}^{*}\right\}, B=\left\{(x, y) \left\lvert \frac{1}{4} x^{2}-y^{2}=1\right.\right.$, $x, y \in \mathbf{R}_{\}}$. Determine whether the following conclusions are correct. If correct, provide a proof; if not, provide a counterexample.
(1) If the elements of set $A$ are used as coordinates of points, then these points all lie on a straight line;
(2) $A \cap B$ has at most one element;
(3) When $a_{1} \neq 0$, it is certain that $A \cap B \neq \varnothing$.

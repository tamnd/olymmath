Given four positive integers $m, n, p, q$, satisfying $p < m$ and $q < n$, in the Cartesian coordinate system, take four points $A(0,0), B(p, 0), C(m, q), D(m, n)$. Consider the path $f$ from $A$ to $D$ and the path $g$ from $B$ to $C$, where $f$ and $g$ can only move in the positive direction of the coordinate axes and can only change direction at integer points (from one positive direction of a coordinate axis to another positive direction of a coordinate axis).
Let $S$ be the number of pairs of paths $(f, g)$ such that $f$ and $g$ have no common points. Prove:
$$
S = C_{m+n}^{m} C_{m+q-p}^{q} - C_{m+q}^{m} C_{m+n-p}^{n}.
$$
(2003 Vietnam National Team Selection Exam Problem)

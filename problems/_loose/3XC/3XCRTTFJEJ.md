Given an ellipse $E$ and a line $H$ in the plane of the ellipse, which is perpendicular to one of its axes. Let a circle corresponding to the line $H$ be denoted by $h$, which satisfies the following conditions: Its center is on the axis of the ellipse that is perpendicular to the line $H$, and the ratio of the square of the tangent from a point $M$ on the ellipse to the circle to the square of the distance of the same point $M$ from the line $H$ is constant, independent of the position of the point $M$ on the ellipse.

$1^{0}$ Determine the position of the center of the circle, the size of its radius, and the numerical value of the constant ratio; what are the conditions for the possibility of the problem, and if these conditions are met, determine the position of the circle relative to the line $H$ and the ellipse $E$. In which case does the circle have no point outside or inside the ellipse.

$2^{0}$ Let $H$ and $K$ be two lines, each perpendicular to one of the axes of the ellipse. Let $P$ be the intersection point of the two lines and let $h$ and $k$ be the circles corresponding to the two lines. Prove that the radical axis of the circles passes through the point $P$. What will be the geometric locus of the point $P$ if the circles are tangent? What will be the geometric locus of the point $P$ if the common chord of the circles passes through the point $P$?

$3^{0}$ Let $H$ and $H^{\prime}$ be two lines perpendicular to the major axis of the ellipse, and let $h$ and $h^{\prime}$ be the corresponding two circles. Prove that if the point $M$ moves on the ellipse, the sum or difference of the tangents drawn to the circles is constant, whether the ellipse segment described by the point $M$ falls between the lines or not. Modify the statement of this property appropriately if the two lines are perpendicular to the minor axis.

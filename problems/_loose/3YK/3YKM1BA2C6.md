In an isosceles triangle $ABC$, the angle at vertex $C$ is $120^{\circ}$. From $C$ inside the triangle, two rays are constructed, forming an angle of $60^{\circ}$. The rays reach side $AB$, reflect off it (according to the rule "the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection"), and reach the legs $AC$ and $BC$. Thus, the given triangle is divided into five triangles; of these, we consider the three with a side on $AB$. It is to be proven that the area of the middle one is equal to the sum of the areas of the other two.

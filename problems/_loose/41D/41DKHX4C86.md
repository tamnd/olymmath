Given a triangle $ABC$ and a point $D$ in its plane. We need to draw a line through $D$ that divides the triangle into two regions of equal area. Does the following procedure always lead to the goal? (We choose the labeling so that $D$ is in the $C_{0} S B$ angle sector, where $C C_{0}$ is one of the medians of the triangle and $S$ is its centroid.)

1. Draw a line through $D$ parallel to $AB$, intersecting $AC$ at $E$;
2. take the midpoint $F$ of the segment $AC_{0}$;
3. draw a line through $C$ parallel to $EF$, intersecting $AB$ at $G$, then
4. draw a line through $G$ parallel to $AC$, intersecting $DE$ at $H$;
5. draw a circle with $DH$ as diameter, and
6. draw a circle with $D$ as center and $DE$ as radius, one of their intersection points is $J$;
7. measure the segment $HJ$ from $G$ towards $A$, the endpoint is $K$.
8. Then the desired line is $DK$.

Is the construction valid if in step I we use two other sides of the triangle instead of sides $AB$ and $AC$, and modify the subsequent steps accordingly? - Can the construction be simplified?

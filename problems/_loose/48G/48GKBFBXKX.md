If the size of a polygonal plate is increased by several ($k$) times (without changing its shape), then the distances from the center of gravity of the plate to the sides of the polygon will also increase by the same ($k$) times. Accepting this property without proof, derive from this that the center of gravity of a triangular plate is the point of intersection of its medians.

In the following, by the center of gravity of a given polygon, we mean the center of gravity of a homogeneous plate having the shape of such a polygon. By the center of gravity of the vertices of an $n$-gon, we mean the center of gravity of $n$ material points that are formed if equal masses are placed at all vertices of the $n$-gon.

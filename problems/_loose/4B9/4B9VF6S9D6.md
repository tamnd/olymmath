Let $k$ be an odd integer greater than $1$ . On a table there are $k$ piles of stones: the first has $1$ stone, the next has $2$ stones, the next has $3$ stones, and so on until the last one, which has $k$ stones. In the first step, $3$ piles are chosen and all their stones are combined into a new pile, from which $1$ stone is removed. In the second step, $3$ piles are chosen and all their stones are combined into a new pile, from which $2$ stones are removed. In general, in step $n$ , $3$ piles are chosen and all their stones are combined into a new pile, from which $n$ stones are removed. After several steps, only a single pile remains on the table, containing $p$ stones.

- Prove that $p$ is a perfect square if and only if the numbers $2k+2$ and $3k+1$ are perfect squares.

- Find the minimum value of $k$ for which $p$ is a perfect square.

**Note:** In each step, the stones that are removed are discarded permanently.

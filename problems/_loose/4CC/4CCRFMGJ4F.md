(a) Give an example of two matrices $A$ and $B \operatorname{from} \mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})$, such that

$$
A^{2}+B^{2}=\left(\begin{array}{ll}
2 & 3 \\
3 & 2
\end{array}\right)
$$

(b) Show that if $A$ and $B$ are two matrices $\operatorname{from} \mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})$, such that $A^{2}+B^{2}=\left(\begin{array}{ll}2 & 3 \\ 3 & 2\end{array}\right)$, then they do not commute, $A B \neq B A$.

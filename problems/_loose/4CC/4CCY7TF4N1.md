Seryozha chose two different prime numbers $p$ and $q$. He considers a natural number $n$ to be good if the number $p+q$ can be represented as the sum of exactly $q$ numbers, each of which has the form $n^{k}$ for an integer non-negative $k$. (For example, if Seryozha had chosen $p=7$ and $q=3$, he would have considered the number $n=2$ to be good, since $7+3=2^{3}+2^{0}+2^{0}$). Prove that Seryozha considers no more than two numbers to be good.

(S. Volchonkov)

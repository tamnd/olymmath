Let the midpoint of the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ of the convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ be denoted by $E$ and $F$, respectively. We draw the segments of the lines $AF$, $BE$, $CF$, and $DE$ that lie within the quadrilateral, and denote their new endpoints by $A_1$, $B_1$, $C_1$, and $D_1$, respectively. It is to be proven that among the ratios

$$
\frac{AF}{FA_1}, \frac{BE}{EB_1}, \frac{CF}{FC_1}, \frac{DE}{ED_1}
$$

there is one that is not greater than 1.

The old man has a certain number of apples and has said the following about them.

- If two of my grandchildren come, I will not be able to divide the apples equally between them.
- If three of my grandchildren come, I will be able to divide the apples equally among all of them.
- If four of my grandchildren come, I will be able to divide the apples equally among all of them.
- If five of my grandchildren come, I will be able to divide the apples equally among all of them.
- If six of my grandchildren come, I will not be able to divide the apples equally among all of them.

However, the old man is a bit forgetful, so exactly one of these statements is not true. Under these conditions, what is the minimum number of apples the old man can have?

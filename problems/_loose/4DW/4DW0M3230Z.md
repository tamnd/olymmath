On the day of the solar eclipse on May 20, 1966, the positions of the Sun and the Moon in the celestial coordinates corresponding to the geographical coordinates, the so-called right ascension ($R A$, longitude, given in time units, where $24^{h}$ corresponds to $360^{\circ}$) and declination ($D$, latitude):

$$
\begin{array}{rrr}
\text { Sun, May 20th } 0^{\mathrm{h}}-\text { at } & R A=3^{\mathrm{h}} 45^{\mathrm{m}} 10,77^{\mathrm{s}} & D=+19^{\circ} 50^{\prime} 17,3^{\prime \prime} \\
\text { May 21st } 0^{\mathrm{h}}-\text { at } & 3^{\mathrm{h}} 49^{\mathrm{m}} 10,64^{\mathrm{s}} & +20^{\circ} 02^{\prime} 52,5^{\prime \prime} \\
\text { Moon, May 20th } 9^{\mathrm{h}}-\text { at } & 3^{\mathrm{h}} 44^{\mathrm{m}} 53,50^{\mathrm{s}} & +20^{\circ} 07^{\prime} 29,34^{\prime \prime} \\
10^{\mathrm{h}}-\text { at } & 3^{\mathrm{h}} 47^{\mathrm{m}} 07,77^{\mathrm{s}} & +20^{\circ} 17^{\prime} 41,69^{\prime \prime}
\end{array}
$$

(The angular data refer to the centers of the Sun and Moon and are to be understood as measured from the center of the Earth, the time data are in universal time, i.e., Greenwich local time.)

In the description of eclipses, the ecliptic coordinate system is also used, the base plane of which (instead of the Equator) is the plane of the Sun's apparent annual path, the coordinates being the longitude and latitude, the latter being theoretically always $0^{\circ}$ for the Sun.

When will the $R A$, $D$, longitude, and latitude $\left(=0^{\circ}\right)$ of the Sun and Moon be equal?

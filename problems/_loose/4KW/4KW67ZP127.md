A circle $k$ is given, a point $O$ which does not lie on $k$, and a line $p$ which does not intersect $k$. Consider an arbitrary circle $l$, which is externally tangent to $k$ and is also tangent to $p$. Denote the corresponding common points by $A$ and $B$, respectively. If $O, A$ and $B$ are not collinear, we construct the circumcircle $m$ of the triangle $O A B$. Prove that all such circles $m$ either have a common point different from $O$, or are tangent to the same line.

On a line $d$ consider the points $A_{0}, A_{1}, A_{2}, A_{3}, A_{4}, \ldots, A_{n}$, in this order, such that $A_{0} A_{1}=1 \mathrm{~cm}, A_{1} A_{2}=2 \mathrm{~cm}, A_{2} A_{3}=2^{2} \mathrm{~cm}, A_{3} A_{4}=2^{3} \mathrm{~cm}, \ldots, A_{n-1} A_{n}=2^{n-1} \mathrm{~cm}$. Show that there does not exist $p \in\{0,1,2, \ldots, n\}$ such that $A_{p}$ is the midpoint of the segment $A_{i} A_{j}$, for any $i, j \in\{0,1,2, \ldots, n\}, i<j$.

Let $P \in \mathbb{K}[X]$. We say that $P$ is even if and only if $P(-X)=P(X)$, and we say that $P$ is odd if and only if $P(-X)=-P(X)$.

1. Let $P \in \mathbb{K}[X]$. Show that the following statements are equivalent

(i) $P(-X)=P(X)$

(ii) all the coefficients of odd degree of $P$ are zero

(iii) there exists a polynomial $Q$ such that $P(X)=Q\left(X^{2}\right)$.

State and prove a similar result for odd polynomials.

2. Show that every polynomial can be written uniquely as the sum of an even polynomial and an odd polynomial.

One of the companies that sells office equipment and computers has launched a promotion under which every buyer of a laptop or desktop computer receives wireless headphones with a charging case as a gift. Ivan Ivanovich decided to buy a laptop, which costs 130,000 rubles. When forming the order in the company's online store, he was offered to add headphones to the order, costing 15,000 rubles. The headphones will be free for the buyer of the laptop, and delivery of goods to any address specified by the buyer is also free. Some time after placing the order, Ivan Ivanovich received a message from another company - a delivery service - that the laptop and headphones would be delivered to him on different days with an interval of about one week: the headphones would be delivered first, followed by the laptop. The buyer has the option to refuse any purchased item even after receiving it (subject to return conditions).

(a) Explain why it might be beneficial for the delivery service to deliver items from one order on different days, even when delivery is free for customers? This delivery policy is used by many large logistics companies that deliver goods to end customers.

(b) If the buyer has the option to refuse any purchased item even after receiving it, how can the company selling office equipment limit the buyer's desire to keep the free headphones but refuse the laptop? Provide an example of a mechanism the company could use and explain how it would work on the buyer.

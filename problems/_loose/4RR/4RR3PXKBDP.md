This case, the most interesting of all, outwardly resembles the previous ones, but in reality, it is fundamentally different from them. It also took place on the island of knights, liars, and normal people.

The main characters were the defendant, the prosecutor, and the defense attorney. The trial proceeded in a very unusual manner. To begin with, one of its three main participants was a knight, another a liar, and the third a normal person, but it was unknown who was the knight, the liar, or the normal person. Even more strange was another circumstance: the court knew for sure that if the defendant was not guilty, then either the defense attorney or the prosecutor was guilty. It was also known that the guilty one was not a liar. During the trial, the defendant, the prosecutor, and the defense attorney made the following statements:

Defendant. I am not guilty.

Defense Attorney. My client is indeed not guilty.

Prosecutor. That's not true, the defendant is guilty.

Their statements sounded quite natural. The jury retired for deliberation but could not reach any decision: the information contained in the three statements made in court turned out to be insufficient. At the time of the trial, the island was part of British territory, so the government of the island sent a telegram to Scotland Yard requesting that Inspector Craig be sent to help resolve the difficulty.

Several weeks later, Inspector Craig arrived on the island, and the trial resumed. Craig decided to thoroughly investigate everything at any cost. He intended not only to establish who was guilty of the crime but also to determine who among the three participants in the trial was the knight, who the liar, and who the normal person. Inspector Craig decided to find out the information he was interested in with the minimum number of questions. First, he asked the prosecutor: "Are you, by any chance, not guilty?" The prosecutor answered. After pondering, Inspector Craig turned to the defendant with the question: "Is the prosecutor guilty?" The defendant answered, and Inspector Craig learned everything he was interested in.

Who is guilty? Who was the normal person, who the knight, and who the liar?

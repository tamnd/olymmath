A right circular cylinder has a height and diameter of $1 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~m}$. The cylinder was cut by planes parallel to the base, starting from the base, and spaced $1 \mathrm{mcm}$ apart. Construct a regular hexagon in each of the 101 circles, such that the hexagons directly above each other are rotated by 30 degrees relative to each other around the axis of the cylinder.

Consider the solid whose vertices are the vertices of the hexagons, with the base and top being two hexagons; the side faces are triangles, two vertices of which are two adjacent vertices of a hexagon, and the third vertex is the vertex between them on an adjacent hexagon. Show that the surface area of this solid is greater than the surface area of the cylinder.

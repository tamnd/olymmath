Let $a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{n}$, where $n>2$, be integers; from these numbers, a new system of numbers is formed: $a_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}, a_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{a_{2}+a_{3}}{2}, \ldots, a_{n-1}^{\prime}=\frac{a_{n-1}+a_{n}}{2}, a_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{a_{n}+a_{1}}{2} ;$ from the numbers $a_{1}^{\prime}, a_{2}^{\prime}, \ldots, a_{n}^{\prime}$, the numbers $a_{1}^{\prime \prime}\left(=\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}+a_{2}^{\prime}}{2}\right), a_{2}^{\prime \prime}, \ldots, a_{n}^{\prime \prime}$ are found in the same way, and so on. Prove that if all the numbers obtained in this way are integers, then $a_{1}=a_{2}=\ldots=a_{n}$.

Let's prove that the number of distinct Eulerian cycles in a simple graph cannot be exactly three. (The points $A_{1}, A_{2}, \ldots, A_{k}$ form a cycle in this order if $A_{1} A_{2}, A_{2} A_{3}, \ldots, A_{k} A_{1}$ are all edges of the graph. An Eulerian cycle is a cycle in which each edge of the graph appears exactly once. We do not consider cycles different if they only differ in the starting point or the direction of traversal.)

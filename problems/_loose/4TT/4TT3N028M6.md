A circle $p$ with center $K$ passing through point $M$ and a semicircle $q$ above the diameter $K M$ are given. Through any point $L$ inside the segment $K M$, we draw a perpendicular to $K M$. This perpendicular intersects the semicircle $q$ at point $Q$ and the circle $p$ at points $P_{1}, P_{2}$ such that $\left|P_{1} Q\right|>\left|P_{2} Q\right|$. The line $M Q$ intersects the circle $p$ again at point $R \neq M$. Prove that for the areas $S_{1}$ and $S_{2}$ of triangles $M P_{1} Q$ and $P_{2} R Q$, respectively, the following holds: $1<S_{1}: S_{2}<3+\sqrt{8}$.

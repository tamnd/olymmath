Two players $A$ and $B$ play the following game:

At the beginning, they agree on a natural number $K (K \geq 17)$, for example, through a random method. Then, $A$ selects a number from the set $M=\{2,4,8,16\}$; let this number be denoted by $a_{1}$. Next, $B$ multiplies the number $a_{1}$ by a number from the set $M$ and obtains the number $b_{1}$. Then, $A$ multiplies the number $b_{1}$ again by a number from the set $M$ and obtains the number $a_{2}$. Subsequently, $B$ and $A$ continue this process alternately until one of the players reaches a product that is greater than the previously agreed number $K$. The player who first reaches a product greater than $K$ wins.

a) How must player $A$ play to win with certainty if $K=100$ is given?

b) Which of the two players can always enforce a win, and what winning strategy must he apply if $K=1000000$ is given?

c) How can one decide for any given $K$ which of the two players can enforce a win, and how must this player proceed?

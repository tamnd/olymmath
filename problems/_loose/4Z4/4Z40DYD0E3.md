A triangulation of a convex polygon $\Pi$ refers to a partition of $\Pi$ into triangles using diagonals of $\Pi$ that do not share vertices. If all the triangles in the partition have equal area, such a triangulation is called "good". Prove: In any two different good triangulations of the convex polygon $\Pi$, exactly two triangles are different, i.e., prove that in the first good triangulation, a pair of triangles can be replaced by another pair of different triangles to obtain the second good triangulation.

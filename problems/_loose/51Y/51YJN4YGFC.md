Let $A A_{1}, B B_{1}, C C_{1}$ be the medians of triangle $A B C$, and $O$ be the point of their intersection. Then

$$
\frac{A_{1} O}{A_{1} A}=\frac{1}{3}, \frac{B_{1} O}{B_{1} B}=\frac{1}{3}, \frac{C_{1} O}{C_{1} C}=\frac{1}{3}
$$

so that $\frac{A_{1} O}{A_{1} A}+\frac{B_{1} O}{B_{1} B}+\frac{C_{1} O}{C_{1} C}=1$. The French geometer Gergonne, whom we have already mentioned, claimed that this equality is preserved if $A A_{1}, B B_{1}, C C_{1}$ are any three segments having a common point $O$ and connecting the vertices of the triangle with points on the opposite sides. Prove this statement by Gergonne. (U. 122, P. 159.)

In two triangles, there cannot be two pairs of corresponding angles that sum up to $180^{\circ}$, because otherwise their total sum would be $360^{\circ}$ and the third angles of the triangles would have to be zero. Let the angles of the first triangle be $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$, and the angles of the second triangle be $180^{\circ}-\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$. The sum of the angles of the two triangles is $360^{\circ}$. Therefore, $180^{\circ}+2 \beta+2 \gamma=360^{\circ}$, i.e., $\beta+\gamma=90^{\circ}$. Then $\alpha=90^{\circ}=180^{\circ}-\alpha$. And all corresponding angles of the triangles are equal.

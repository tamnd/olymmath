Let $m$ and $n$ be two natural numbers, $n \geq 2$, let $A$ be a ring that has exactly $n$ elements, and let $a$ be an element of $A$ such that $1-a^{k}$ is invertible for any $k \in\{m+1, m+2, \ldots, m+n-1\}$. Show that $a$ is nilpotent (i.e., there exists a non-zero natural number $p$ such that $a^{p}=0$).

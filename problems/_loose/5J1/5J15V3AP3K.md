Janete plays with numbers by creating sequences from a four-digit number:

I) First, she divides the number into two parts, the first part being formed by the first two digits and the second part by the last two digits.

II) If the number of one of the parts is even, she divides it by 2; if it is odd, she adds 1.

III) Then, she combines the two results (in the original order).

IV) If the new number still has 4 digits, she repeats the process; if the new number has 3 digits, she separates it into two parts, the first part being the first digit and the second part being the last two digits, and repeats the process; if it has 2 digits, she repeats the process without dividing the number.

V) This process is repeated until she reaches a number with only 1 digit, at which point she ends the sequence.

For example, let's construct the sequence starting with 1,617:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& 1617 \rightarrow 16|17 \rightarrow 818 \rightarrow 8| 18 \rightarrow 49 \rightarrow \\
& 50 \rightarrow 25 \rightarrow 26 \rightarrow 13 \rightarrow 14 \rightarrow 7 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the sequence is:

$$
1617 \rightarrow 818 \rightarrow 49 \rightarrow 50 \rightarrow 25 \rightarrow 26 \rightarrow 13 \rightarrow 14 \rightarrow 7
$$

a) What is the sequence that starts with 2,020?

b) What is the 5th term of the sequence that starts with 8,998?

c) Let's call a subsequence the last 5 numbers of a sequence. How many different subsequences exist that end in 7, but where all terms are formed by 2-digit numbers, except for the 7?

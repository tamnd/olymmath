A question about cubes. Professor Rackbrain one morning noticed that the cubes of consecutive numbers, starting from 1, can sum up to a perfect square. For example, the sum of the cubes of 1, 2, and 3 (i.e., $1 + 8 + 27$) is 36, or $6^2$. The professor claimed that if taking consecutive numbers starting not from 1, the smallest numbers whose cubes sum to the square of some number are 23, 24, and 25 ($23^3 + 24^3 + 25^3 = 204^2$). Professor Rackbrain proposed finding the two smallest sets of consecutive numbers, starting not from 1 and consisting of more than three numbers, whose sum of cubes is also equal to the square of some natural number.

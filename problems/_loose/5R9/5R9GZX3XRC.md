In the final of a ballroom dance competition, each of the twenty-five judges had to rate five dance couples with grades 1 to 5 as in school, with each grade being used exactly once. After the competition, a table was published showing the average grades for each couple:

| couple $\mathrm{A}$ | couple $\mathrm{B}$ | couple $\mathrm{C}$ | couple $\mathrm{D}$ | couple $\mathrm{E}$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 4.68 | 3.86 | 3.36 | 1.44 | 1.60 |

It soon became clear that exactly one of the averages in the table was incorrect. Determine which average is incorrect and correct it.

One of the judges was the uncle of a dancer from couple E. However, he rated the couples honestly and without bias in the following manner: 1 for $\mathrm{D}$, 2 for $\mathrm{E}$, 3 for $\mathrm{B}$, 4 for $\mathrm{C}$, and 5 for $\mathrm{A}$. Nevertheless, after the corrected results were announced, he wondered whether he could have moved couple $\mathrm{E}$ to the first place by dishonest grading. Determine whether he could have achieved this.

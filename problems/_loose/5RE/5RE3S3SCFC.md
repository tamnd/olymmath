Let $ABCD$ be a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle $S$, $P$ and $Q$ be the points of intersection of its opposite sides, and $O$ be the point of intersection of its diagonals. Prove that:

a) there exist infinitely many triangles inscribed in the circle $S$ whose sides (or their extensions) pass through the points $P, Q$, and $O$ (specifically, if two sides of a triangle inscribed in $S$ pass through two of the points $P, Q$, and $O$, then the third side necessarily passes through the third point);

b) there exist infinitely many quadrilaterals inscribed in the circle $S$ whose points of intersection of opposite sides coincide with $P$ and $Q$ (specifically, if two opposite sides of a quadrilateral inscribed in $S$ intersect at point $P$ and the third side passes through $Q$, then the opposite side also passes through $Q$), and the points of intersection of the diagonals of all these quadrilaterals coincide with $O$;

c) there exist infinitely many quadrilaterals inscribed in the circle $S$ whose point of intersection of diagonals coincides with $O$, and one of the points of intersection of opposite sides coincides with $P$ (specifically, if the point of intersection of the diagonals of a quadrilateral inscribed in $S$ coincides with $O$ and one of the sides passes through $P$, then the opposite side also passes through $P$), and the second point of intersection of opposite sides of all these quadrilaterals coincides with $Q$.

At the market, there is a man with many carpets. He agrees to exchange a carpet of size $a \times b$ for either a carpet of size $1 / a^{1} \times \frac{1}{b}$, or two carpets of sizes $c \times b$ and $a / c \times b$ (for each such exchange, the customer can choose the number $c$ themselves). The traveler told us that initially, he had one carpet, the sides of which were greater than 1, and after several such exchanges, he ended up with a set of carpets, each of which had one side longer than 1 and the other shorter than 1. Is he lying? (At the customer's request, a carpet of size $a \times b$ is considered to be a carpet of size $b \times a$.)

The Complicated Will. An elderly gentleman had three married daughters: Mrs. Jones, Mrs. Smith, and Mrs. White. Each of them had exactly one child. The gentleman died, leaving his entire estate to his daughters, sons-in-law, and grandchildren, and according to the will, the money was to be divided among them as follows.

Each of the nine heirs received a certain number of envelopes (a different number for each of them). Each envelope contained as many dollars as the number of envelopes received by that person. The share of each woman exceeded her husband's share by the same amount that her husband's share exceeded their child's share. Although no family received the same amount, each family nevertheless received the same number of envelopes. The total number of envelopes received by Mrs. Jones and Mrs. Smith equaled the total number of envelopes received by Mrs. White and Mr. Jones.

Before the notary read the will, each family was "penniless." After the deceased's wishes were carried out, they all became wealthy, although none of them became millionaires. What amount did each of the nine heirs receive?

(Lemme de Schür, variant of IMO SL 2009 N3) Let $P \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ be a non-constant polynomial. Show that there exists an infinite number of prime numbers that divide $P(n)$ for at least one $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. Show that in fact, for any $r \in \mathbb{N}^{*}$, there exists an infinite number of integers $n>0$ such that $P(n)$ has at least $r$ distinct prime factors.

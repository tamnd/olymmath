(Tournament of the Towns). Let $\Gamma_{1}$ and $\Gamma_{2}$ be two circles that are external to each other. A line $d$ that intersects both circles is called fair if the chords defined by $d$ on $\Gamma_{1}$ and $\Gamma_{2}$ have the same length. Let $O_{1}$ and $O_{2}$ be the centers of $\Gamma_{1}$ and $\Gamma_{2}$, respectively, and $M$ the midpoint of $\left[O_{1} O_{2}\right]$. Show that for any three fair lines $a, b$, and $c$, the circumcircle of the triangle defined by $a, b$, and $c$ passes through $M$.

Recall: Theorem of Simson's Line. Let $ABC$ be a triangle, $\Gamma$ its circumcircle, and $P$ a point in the plane. We call $P_{A}, P_{B}$, and $P_{C}$ the orthogonal projections of $P$ on $(B C),(C A)$, and $(A B)$, respectively. Then $P_{A}, P_{B}$, and $P_{C}$ are collinear if and only if $P \in \Gamma$.

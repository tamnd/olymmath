Dividing a Triangular Cake. Pavel and Gavel must divide a triangular cake between themselves. Gavel set the condition that he would cut his portion with a straight cut, and Pavel agreed on the condition that he would mark a point $P$ in advance, through which the cut must pass. Since the cake has the same thickness everywhere and is uniform in taste, the problem is a planimetric one. The question is as follows: how should Pavel choose the point $P$ to better protect himself from Gavel's gluttony? The second question: what excess will go to Gavel if Pavel successfully solves the first problem, and Gavel then cuts himself the largest possible portion of the cake?

If the shape of the cake depended on Pavel, he could choose a figure with a center of symmetry (i.e., a circle, square, ellipse, etc.), and place $P$ at this center. Then Gavel would have no advantage. However, the interesting question is, what shape of the cake (while maintaining the initial conditions of the division) would be most convenient for Gavel, and what is the largest excess he could ensure for himself by choosing the shape of the cake wisely?

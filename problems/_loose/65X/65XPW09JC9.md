If we draw perpendiculars from a point P on the circumference of the circumcircle of a triangle to the sides of the triangle, then the feet of these perpendiculars lie on a straight line, the Simson line corresponding to point P.

Let's prove the following theorems: $1^{\circ}$. The Simson line corresponding to point P bisects the line segment joining point P and the orthocenter of the triangle.

$2^{\circ}$. The angle between the Simson lines $s_{1}$ and $s_{2}$ corresponding to points $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ is equal to the inscribed angle subtended by the arc $P_{1} P_{2}$.

$3^{\circ}$. The geometric locus of the intersection points of the Simson lines corresponding to the endpoints of a variable diameter of the circumcircle is the Feuerbach circle.

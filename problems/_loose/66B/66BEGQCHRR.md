Prove that there is no such inclined plane that any tetrahedron placed on it with any of its faces will not tip over. This means the following. Given a plane $ \pi $ and a line $ l $ not perpendicular to it, prove that there exists a tetrahedron $ T $ such that for each of its faces $ S $, there exists a triangle $ ABC $ in the plane $ \pi $ congruent to $ S $, and there exists a point $ D $ such that the tetrahedron $ ABCD $ is congruent to $ T $ and the line parallel to $ l $ passing through the centroid of the tetrahedron $ ABCD $ does not intersect the triangle $ ABC $.
Note. The centroid of a tetrahedron is the point of intersection of the segments connecting the centroids of its faces with the opposite vertices (it is known that such a point always exists).

Try to prove that there exists a set of positive integers $A$, such that for any set $S$ consisting of infinitely many primes, there exist a natural number $k \geqslant 2, m \in A$ and $n \notin A$, where $m$ and $n$ are both the product of $k$ distinct elements of $S$.

Along with the generating function $G(s)$, in many cases the concept of the moment generating function is also useful:

$$
M(s)=\mathrm{E} e^{s X}
$$

(assuming that $s$ are such that $\mathrm{E} e^{s X} < \infty$). If the moment generating function $M(s)$ is finite in a neighborhood of zero, i.e., if there exists a $\delta > 0$ such that $M(s) < \infty$ for $|s| < \delta$, then the derivatives $M^{(n)}(s)$ exist for $|s| < \delta$. If $X$ is a Poisson random variable with parameter $\lambda > 0$, then the moment generating function has the form $M(s)=e^{-\lambda\left(1-e^{s}\right)}$ for all $s \in \mathbb{R}$.

(d) Provide an example of two dependent random variables $X$ and $Y$ for which the moment generating function $M_{X+Y}(s)=\mathrm{E} e^{s(X+Y)}$ is the product of the moment generating functions $M_{X}(s)=\mathrm{E} e^{s X}$ and $M_{Y}(s)=\mathrm{E} e^{s Y}$.

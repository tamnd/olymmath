a) Given points $A, B, C$ not lying on the same straight line. Construct a point $D$ such that the area of quadrilateral $ABCD$ is three times the area of triangle $ABC$. The quadrilateral $ABCD$ can be concave but must not be self-intersecting. For the construction, only the usual right-angled triangle set squares can be used, and sliding is allowed (non-Euclidean construction).

b) What is the geometric locus of point $D$? (The work of first-grade contestants will be considered complete if they provide a correct answer to part a).)

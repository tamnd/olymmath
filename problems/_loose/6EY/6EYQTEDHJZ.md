Prove that any integer power of the number $\sqrt{2}-1$ can be represented in the form $\sqrt{N}-\sqrt{N-1}$, where $N$ is an integer (for example, $(\sqrt{2}-1)^{2}=3-2 \sqrt{2}=\sqrt{9} \div \sqrt{8}$, and $\left.(\sqrt{2}-1)^{3}=5 \sqrt{2}-7=\sqrt{50}-\sqrt{49}\right)$.

Note: The division symbol in the example should be a minus sign, as $\sqrt{9} - \sqrt{8} = 3 - 2\sqrt{2}$.

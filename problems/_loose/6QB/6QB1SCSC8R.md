If the digits $M$, $X$, and $Y$ are distinct, and the equation $\frac{\overline{M X \cdots X Y}}{\overline{M Y}}=$ $X M \cdots M Y$
$-\frac{n \uparrow}{\overline{X Y}}$ holds for any $n \geqslant 1$ (where $n$ is the number of $X$'s in $\overline{M X \cdots X Y}$, and the number of $M$'s in $\overline{X M \cdots M Y}$), find $M$, $X$, and $Y$.
(Kyiv Mathematical Olympiad, 1977)

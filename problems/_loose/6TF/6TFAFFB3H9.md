In a desert, the oases $A, B, C, D$ are located at the vertices of a rhombus with a $60^{\circ}$ angle, with $B$ and $D$ being the endpoints of the longer diagonal. The side of the rhombus is $15 \mathrm{~km}$ long. They want to establish a road network that connects any two oases. Ali, a resident of the $A$ oasis, suggested: Connect $A$ directly to $B$, $C$, and $D$. Hassán: It would be shorter and fairer to build the two diagonals. Finally, Ibrahim, based on Problem P. 20 - K. M. L. 39 (1969) p. 215, provided the shortest total length road network. This is what they built, and the costs were distributed among the 4 oases so that each one's contribution would be proportional to the total distance that a resident of that oasis would travel if they went to each of the other oases once. What was the distribution ratio?

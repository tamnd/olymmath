Mutually distinct non-zero real numbers $a, b, c$ can be used in six different ways to fill in the coefficients of a quadratic equation

$$
\square x^{2}+\square x+\square=0 \text {. }
$$

a) Decide whether there exists a triple $(a, b, c)$ such that all six constructed equations have at least one real root.

b) Decide whether there exists a triple $(a, b, c)$ such that exactly five out of the six constructed equations have at least one real root.

(Michal Rolinek)

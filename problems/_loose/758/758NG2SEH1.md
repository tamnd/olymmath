Let $f: \mathbb{N} \rightarrow \mathbb{N}^{*}$ be a strictly increasing function. Show that:

(a) There exists a decreasing sequence of strictly positive real numbers, $\left(y_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, converging to 0, such that $y_{n} \leq 2 y_{f(n)}$, for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$;

(b) If $\left(x_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is a decreasing sequence of real numbers, converging to 0, then there exists a decreasing sequence of real numbers, $\left(y_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, converging to 0, such that $x_{n} \leq y_{n} \leq 2 y_{f(n)}$, for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

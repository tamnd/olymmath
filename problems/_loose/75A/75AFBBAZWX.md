The 89th question: Let $\mathrm{P}$ be the set of all prime numbers. For subsets $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{S}$ of $\mathrm{Z}^{+}$, if there exists a positive integer $\mathrm{N}$ such that for all $a \in A$ and integers $b(0 \leq b<a)$, there exist $s_{1}, s_{2}, \ldots, s_{n} \in S\left(s_{1}, s_{2}, \ldots, s_{n}\right.$ can be the same), satisfying $b \equiv s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{n}(\bmod a)$, and $1 \leq n \leq N$, then $\mathrm{A}$ is called "$\mathrm{S}$-appropriate". Find a subset $\mathrm{S}$ of $\mathrm{Z}^{+}$ such that $\mathrm{P}$ is "$\mathrm{S}$-appropriate", but $\mathrm{Z}^{+}$ is not "$\mathrm{S}$-appropriate".

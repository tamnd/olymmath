Let $n \geq 2$ be the number of participants at a party. For any two participants $A, B$, the following two statements are true:

(1) If $A$ is acquainted with $B$, then there is no other participant different from $A$ and $B$ who is acquainted with both $A$ and $B$.

(2) If $A$ is not acquainted with $B$, then there are exactly two participants different from $A$ and $B$ who are acquainted with both $A$ and $B$.

Prove that under these conditions, the following always holds:

All participants have the same number of acquaintances at this party.

Non-convex polyhedron. Can a non-convex polyhedron be bounded by congruent quadrilaterals?

44a. Models of regular polyhedra. From the six edges of a regular tetrahedron, four edges can be selected to form a closed skew, i.e., non-planar quadrilateral. This quadrilateral can be considered a model of the tetrahedron, as it contains all the vertices of the tetrahedron. The same can easily be done with a cube; then we get a (non-planar) octagon containing all the vertices of the cube. Can this method be extended to other regular polyhedra, i.e., to the octahedron, dodecahedron, and icosahedron? How many solutions does the problem have?

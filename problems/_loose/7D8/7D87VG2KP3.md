A quadrilateral in Lobachevsky's non-Euclidean geometry, where the diagonals intersect and are divided proportionally at the point of intersection, is called a non-Euclidean parallelogram. Prove that:

a) opposite sides of a non-Euclidean parallelogram are equal;

b) opposite angles of a non-Euclidean parallelogram are equal;

c) if the diagonals of a non-Euclidean parallelogram are mutually perpendicular, then all its sides are equal and the diagonals are angle bisectors at the vertices (such a parallelogram can be called a non-Euclidean rhombus);

d) if the diagonals of a non-Euclidean parallelogram are equal, then all its angles are equal (such a parallelogram can be called a non-Euclidean rectangle, although its angles are not right angles; see p. 150);

e) if the diagonals of a non-Euclidean parallelogram are both equal and mutually perpendicular, then all its sides are equal and all its angles are equal (such a parallelogram can be called a non-Euclidean square).

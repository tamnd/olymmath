(2008 Hebei Province High School Mathematics Competition Question) Let the function $f(x)$ defined on $[0,2]$ satisfy the following conditions:
(1) For $x \in[0,2]$, there is always $f(2-x)=f(x)$, and $f(x) \geqslant 1, f(1)=3$;
(2) For $x, y \in[1,2]$, if $x+y \geqslant 3$, then $f(x)+f(y) \leqslant f(x+y-2)+1$.

Prove: (1) $f\left(\frac{1}{3^{n}}\right) \leqslant \frac{2}{3^{n}}+1\left(n \in \mathbf{N}^{+}\right)$; (2) When $x \in[1,2]$, $1 \leqslant f(x) \leqslant 13-6x$.

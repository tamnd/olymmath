Consider a hexagonal rhombus of side $n$, divided into hexagonal cells of side 1. Alice and Bob alternately place blue and red hexagons on the cells of the rhombus. Alice wins if she can connect the north shore to the south shore using a bridge of blue hexagons, and Bob if he can connect the east shore to the west shore using a bridge of red hexagons. Why does this problem fall under the scope of Zermelo's theorem? Which of the two has a winning strategy?

Unfortunately, the results provided by such arguments are non-constructive: we know who has a winning strategy, but not which one! In the case of the Chomp game and the Hex game, winning strategies are known only for boards of up to about a dozen cells on a side, except for a particular arrangement of the Chomp game.

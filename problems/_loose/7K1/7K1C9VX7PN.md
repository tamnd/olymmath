A regular nonagon is to be divided into triangles by drawing non-intersecting diagonals such that each vertex of the nonagon is a vertex of an odd number of the resulting triangles. Show that this can only be done in one way, disregarding symmetry. (That is, any two such partitions can be transformed into each other by a suitable rotation or reflection of the nonagon.)

a) Prove that among all triangles with two given sides, the one with the largest area is the one where these sides are perpendicular to each other.

b) Prove that among two unequal triangles with equal bases and equal angles at the opposite vertex, the one with the larger area and the larger perimeter is the one where the difference in the angles at the base is smaller (or, equivalently, the one where the difference in the lateral sides is smaller); among all triangles with a given base and a given angle at the opposite vertex, the one with the largest area and the largest perimeter is the isosceles triangle.

c) Prove that among all parallelograms with a given acute angle and a given perimeter, the one with the largest area is the rhombus.

d) Prove that among two unequal triangles with the same bases and the same perimeters, the one with the larger area is the one where the difference in the angles at the base is smaller (or, equivalently, the one where the difference in the lateral sides is smaller); among all triangles with a given base and a given perimeter, the one with the largest area is the isosceles triangle.

e) Prove that among all trapezoids with given bases and a given perimeter, the one with the largest area is the isosceles trapezoid.

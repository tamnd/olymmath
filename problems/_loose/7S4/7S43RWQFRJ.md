From point $A$, we draw rays that enclose an angle $\alpha$ with each other. From a random point $B_{1}$ on one of the rays, we draw a perpendicular to the adjacent ray, the foot of which is $B_{1}$. From this point, we again draw a perpendicular to the next adjacent ray, and so on, infinitely. What is the sum of the perpendiculars $B_{1} B_{2}, B_{2} B_{3}, B_{3} B_{4}$, etc.? What is the sum of the areas of the triangles $A B_{1} B_{2}, A B_{2} B_{3}$, etc., if $A B_{1}=a$? What should $\alpha$ be so that the sum of the areas of all the triangles is $a^{2}$?

A rotation of space about an axis $l$ is a movement of space in which the points of the line $l$ remain fixed, and each point $A$, not belonging to $l$, is mapped to a point $A^{\prime}$ such that points $A$ and $A^{\prime}$ belong to a plane $\gamma$ perpendicular to the line $l$, and the angle $A A_{0} A^{\prime}$, where $A_{0}=\gamma \cap l$, does not depend on $A$ - this is the angle of rotation. Additionally, it is required that the direction of rotation (in the plane $\gamma$) from point $A$ to point $A^{\prime}$ is the same for all points $A$, when viewed "along the line $l$ " in a fixed direction.

Prove that a line $a$, perpendicular to the axis of rotation, is mapped by the rotation to a line $a^{\prime}$, lying in the same plane as $a$, and the angle between the lines $a$ and $a^{\prime}$ is equal to the angle of rotation.

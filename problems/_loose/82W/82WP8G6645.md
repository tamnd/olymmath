Let's define the magnitude of the trihedral angle $S A B C$ as $\widehat{A} + \widehat{B} + \widehat{C} - \pi$, where $\widehat{A}, \widehat{B}, \widehat{C}$ are the magnitudes of the dihedral angles at the edges $S A, S B, S C$. Prove that: a) the magnitude of the trihedral angle is always positive; b) if the given trihedral angle is divided into two trihedral angles by a plane passing through one of its edges, then the magnitude of the given angle is equal to the sum of the magnitudes of the two resulting angles.

In the triangle $ABC$, the incircle touches the sides of the triangle at points $T_{1}, T_{2}, T_{3}$; the points symmetric to these with respect to the midpoints of the sides are $T_{1}^{\prime}, T_{2}^{\prime}, T_{3}^{\prime}$. Connect the points $T_{1}^{\prime}, T_{2}^{\prime}, T_{3}^{\prime}$ with the opposite vertices $A, B, C$. The lines $A T_{1}^{\prime}, B T_{2}^{\prime}, C T_{3}^{\prime}$ intersect at a point $J$. Draw lines through the vertices $A, B, C$ parallel to the opposite sides, thus forming the triangle $A_{1} B_{1} C_{1}$. Draw again lines through the vertices of this triangle parallel to the opposite sides, and continue this process, thus forming the triangles $A_{2} B_{2} C_{2}, A_{3} B_{3} C_{3}, \ldots, A_{n} B_{n} C_{n}$. Determine the points $J^{\prime}, J^{\prime \prime}, \ldots, J^{(n)}$ in these triangles, such that they correspond to $J$ in the triangle $ABC$, and prove that:

$1^{\circ}$

$$
J, J^{\prime}, J^{\prime \prime}, \ldots, J^{(n)}
$$

lie on a straight line and

$2^{\circ}$

$$
J^{(n)} S=2^{n+1} J_{0} S,
$$

where $J_{0}$ is the center of the incircle of the triangle $ABC$ and $S$ is the centroid of this triangle.

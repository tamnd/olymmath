A competition involves 100 participants. For any two participants $ x $ and $ y $, they compete exactly once and one of them wins, denoted by $ x \rightarrow y $ indicating that participant $ x $ defeats participant $ y $. If for any two participants $ x $ and $ y $, there exists a sequence of participants $ u_{1}, u_{2}, \cdots, u_{k} (k \geqslant 2) $ such that
$$
x=u_{1} \rightarrow u_{2} \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow u_{k}=y,
$$
then the competition result is called "friendly".
(1) Prove: For any friendly competition result, there exists a positive integer $ m $ such that for any two participants $ x $ and $ y $, there exists a sequence of participants $ z_{1}, z_{2}, \cdots, z_{m} $ (where $ z_{1}, z_{2}, \cdots, z_{m} $ can be repeated) such that
$$
x=z_{1} \rightarrow z_{2} \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow z_{m}=y;
$$
(2) For any friendly competition result $ T $, let the smallest positive integer $ m $ satisfying the condition in (1) be denoted as $ m(T) $. Find the minimum value of $ m(T) $.

Prove that if the fractions

$$
\frac{a_{1}}{p}, \frac{a_{2}}{p^{2}}, \frac{a_{3}}{p^{3}}, \ldots, \frac{a_{n}}{p^{n}}, \ldots
$$

( $p$ - a prime number different from 2 and 5, $a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{n} \ldots$ are any integers coprime with $p$) are expanded into infinite periodic decimal fractions, then a few of the first fractions (possibly one) will have the same number of digits in the period, and each subsequent fraction will have $p$ times more digits in the period than the previous one.

For example, $\frac{1}{3}=0,(3), \frac{4}{9}=0,(4), \frac{10}{27}=0,(370)$, $\frac{80}{81}=0,(987654320), \frac{116}{243}$ has 27 digits in the period, $\frac{653}{729}$ has 81 digits in the period, and so on.

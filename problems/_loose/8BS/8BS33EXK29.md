In his famous book [69], Hausdorff introduced an important concept of "distance" $ d(A, B) $ between two sets $ A $ and $ B $ in a metric space $(E, \rho)$. Despite the simplicity of the definition, it is not a priori obvious that such a construction exists.

(a) Two natural candidates for the "distance" between $ A $ and $ B $ are the infimum $ m(A, B) $ and the supremum $ M(A, B) $ of the distances $ \rho(a, b) $, where $ a $ runs through all points of the set $ A $, and $ b $ through all points of the set $ B $. However, it should be verified that for $ m(A, B) $ and $ M(A, B) $, the axioms of a distance are not satisfied.

(b) Now consider $ \delta(A, B) $ - the length of the smallest neighborhood of the set $ A $ containing $ B $ (more precisely, the infimum of the lengths). Recall that the $ \varepsilon $-neighborhood of the set $ A $ is the set

$$
A^{\varepsilon}=\{x: \rho(x, a)<\varepsilon \text { for some } a \in A\} \text {. }
$$

Establish the inequality

$$
m(A, B) \leqslant \delta(A, B) \leqslant M(A, B), A, B \subseteq E
$$

(c) Prove that $ \delta(A, B) $ satisfies the triangle inequality

$$
\delta(A, B) \leqslant \delta(A, C)+\delta(C, B), A, B, C \subseteq E
$$

but is not a symmetric function. Determine in what case $ \delta(A, B)=0 $. Propose how to modify $ \delta(A, B) $ to obtain a function $ d(A, B) $ that defines a distance at least on the system $ \mathscr{F} $ of closed bounded sets in $(E, \rho)$, such that

$$
d\left(F_{n}, F\right) \rightarrow 0 \Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}{l}
\delta\left(F_{n}, F\right) \rightarrow 0 \\
\delta\left(F, F_{n}\right) \rightarrow 0
\end{array}\right.
$$

(d) Along with the set-theoretic operation

$$
\overline{\lim } A_{n}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} \bigcup_{m=n}^{\infty} A_{m}
$$

in the obtained metric space $(\mathscr{F}, d)$, the operation of the upper closed limit $\lim A_{n}$ plays an important role, defined as follows:

$ x \in[\lim ] A_{n} $, if any neighborhood of the point $ x $ contains points common with $ A_{n} $ for infinitely many $ n $.

Similarly, $[\underline{\lim }] A_{n}$ is defined:

$ x \in[\varliminf \lim ] A_{n} $, if any neighborhood of the point $ x $ contains points common with $ A_{n} $ starting from some finite $ n=n(x) $.

How do $[\overline{\lim }] A_{n}$ and $\overline{\lim } A_{n}$ relate? Establish the following statement, called the Hausdorff selection theorem:

if the space $(E, \rho)$ is separable, then from any sequence $\left(F_{n}\right) \subseteq(\mathscr{F}, d)$ one can select a subsequence $\left(F_{m}\right) \subseteq\left(F_{n}\right)$ for which $[\lim ] F_{m}=[\underline{\mathrm{lm}}] F_{m}$.

(e) Prove the following implications, valid for $ F_{n}, F \in \mathscr{F} $ $(n \geqslant 1)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \delta\left(F_{n}, F\right) \rightarrow 0 \Rightarrow F \subseteq[\underline{\lim }] F_{n} \\
& \delta\left(F, F_{n}\right) \rightarrow 0 \Rightarrow F \supseteq[\lim ] F_{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

in particular, meaning that if the limit of the sets $ F_{n} $ in the space $(\mathscr{F}, d)$ exists, then it necessarily coincides with $[\lim ] F_{n}=[\lim ] F_{n}=[\underline{\lim }] F_{n}$.

(f) Note that, generally speaking, the existence of $ F=[\lim ] F_{n} $ does not imply that $ F_{n} \rightarrow F $ in the metric $ d $. However, if the space $(E, \rho)$ is compact, i.e.,

every infinite sequence of elements of $ E $ contains a subsequence converging in $ E $, then $[\lim] F_{n}$ exists if and only if the limit $ F $ of the sets $ F_{n} $ in the metric $ d $ exists, and in this case $ F=[\lim ] F_{n} $.

(g) Let $ \mu $ be a finite measure on $(E, \mathscr{E})$, where $\mathscr{E}$ is the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, and $ F_{n} \rightarrow F $ in $(\mathscr{F}, d)$. Show that

$$
\lim _{n} \mu\left(F_{n}\right) \leqslant \mu(F)
$$

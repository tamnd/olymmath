In the plane, there are two anti-similar triangles $\triangle B X C$ and $\triangle A X D$ (where $A, B, C, D$ form a convex quadrilateral), i.e., $\angle A D X = \angle B C X, \angle D A X = \angle C B X$. If all four angles are less than $90^{\circ}$, and let the perpendicular bisectors of $A B$ and $C D$ intersect at $Y$, prove: $\angle \Lambda Y B = 2 \angle A D X$.

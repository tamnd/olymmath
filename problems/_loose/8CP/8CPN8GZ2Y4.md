(England, 80). Prove that for no value of $n \in \mathbf{N}$, greater than 1, does the equation $x^{n}+y^{n}=z^{n}$ have solutions in natural numbers satisfying the conditions $x \leqslant n, y \leqslant n$.

2.25%. (Austria, 72). Prove that for any value of $n \in \mathbf{N}$, greater than 2, the equation

$$
x^{n}+(x+1)^{n}=(x+2)^{n}
$$

has no solutions in natural numbers.

2.26*. (New York, 81). Solve the equation

$$
x^{x+y}=(x+y)^{y}
$$

in positive rational numbers.

2.27*. (PRC, 80; BGR, 81). Prove that if the number $n \in \mathbf{N}$ is odd, then the equation

$$
\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}=\frac{4}{n}
$$

has a solution in natural numbers if and only if

$$
n=m(4 k-1) \text { for } m, k \in \mathbf{N} .
$$

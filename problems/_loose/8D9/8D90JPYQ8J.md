In a company of $n>1$ people, some of its members are acquainted with each other, while others are not. If X is acquainted with Y, then Y is also acquainted with X, and a person is neither considered their own acquaintance nor non-acquaintance. Find all $n$ for which in the company there will always be two people A and B who have the same number of acquaintances in this company, and such that there will be either a person C who is acquainted with both A and B, or a person $\Gamma$, who is not acquainted with both A and B.

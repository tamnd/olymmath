Daphné wrote on the board the five numbers 1, 3, 6, 8, and 11. She then allows herself, as many times as she wishes, to erase three numbers written on the board, say $a, b$, and $c$, and to write in their place their arithmetic means $(a+b) / 2, (b+c) / 2$, and $(c+a) / 2$. Can she manage to obtain the five numbers 2, 4, 5, 7, and 9 after a finite number of such operations?

And if, instead of the arithmetic means, Félicie had replaced the numbers $a, b$, and $c$ by their geometric means $\sqrt{a b}, \sqrt{b c}$, and $\sqrt{c a}$?

For a positive integer $n$, let $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{n}}=\{1,2, \ldots, \mathrm{n}\}, \mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{n}}=\left\{\left.\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\sqrt{k}} \right\rvert \mathrm{m} \in \mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{n}}, \mathrm{k} \in \mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{n}}\right\}$. If the sum of any two elements in a subset $\mathrm{A}$ of $\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{n}}$ is not a perfect square, then $\mathrm{A}$ is called a "sparse set". Find the maximum value of $\mathrm{n}$ such that $\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{n}}$ can be partitioned into the union of two disjoint sparse sets.

a) Prove: If

$$
a=BC, b=AC, c=AB, d=AD, e=BD, f=CD
$$

are the edge lengths of a tetrahedron $ABCD$, then the surface area $A_{O}$ of this tetrahedron satisfies the inequality

$$
A_{O}<\frac{1}{3}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}+e^{2}+f^{2}\right)
$$

b) Investigate whether the statement in (2) can be strengthened to the following: There exists a smallest real number $\lambda$ with $\lambda<\frac{1}{3}$, such that for the surface area $A_{O}$ of any tetrahedron $ABCD$, if its edge lengths are denoted as in (1), the inequality

$$
A_{O} \leq \lambda\left(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}+e^{2}+f^{2}\right)
$$

holds. If this is the case, determine this number $\lambda$.

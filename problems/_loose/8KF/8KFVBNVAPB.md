Let $B_{1} B_{2} B_{3} B_{4}=B$ be a square, and let the midpoints of its consecutive sides be $C_{1}, C_{2}, C_{3}, C_{4}$ (with $C_{1}$ on the side $B_{1} B_{2}$, and so on). Draw the line segments $B_{1} C_{2}, B_{2} C_{3}, B_{3} C_{4}, B_{4} C_{1}$, and let the intersection points of consecutive pairs be $D_{1}, D_{2}, D_{3}, D_{4}$, respectively. - On the other hand, let the second trisection points of the sides of the square $B$ be $E_{1}, E_{2}, E_{3}, E_{4}$ (i.e., $B_{1} E_{1}=2 E_{1} B_{2}$, and so on), draw the line segments $E_{1} B_{3}, E_{2} B_{4}, E_{3} B_{1}, E_{4} B_{2}$, and let the intersection points of consecutive pairs be $F_{1}, F_{2}, F_{3}, F_{4}$, respectively. Prove that

1. the area of the square $F_{1} F_{2} F_{3} F_{4}=F$ is twice the area of the square $D_{1} D_{2} D_{3} D_{4}=D$;
2. the vertices of the square $D$ bisect the sides of the square $F$.

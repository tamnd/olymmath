(20 points) Given $\boldsymbol{m}$ natural numbers, not exceeding $\boldsymbol{n}$, arranged in non-decreasing order: $a_{1} \leq a_{2} \leq \cdots \leq a_{m}$. Similarly, $n$ natural numbers, not exceeding $\boldsymbol{m}$, are arranged in non-decreasing order: $\boldsymbol{b}_{1} \leq \boldsymbol{b}_{2} \leq \cdots \leq \boldsymbol{b}_{\boldsymbol{n}}$. Is it true that there will always be two indices $\boldsymbol{i}$ and $\boldsymbol{j}$ such that $\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{i}}+\boldsymbol{i}=\boldsymbol{b}_{\boldsymbol{j}}+\boldsymbol{j}$.

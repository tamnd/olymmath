A pirate decided to bury his treasure on an island. Near the shore, there were two distinctive rocks, $A$ and $B$, and three coconut trees inland: $C_{1}, C_{2}, C_{3}$. Using these, the pirate marked the hiding spot as follows. He stood at $C_{1}$ and marked point $A_{1}$ such that the segment $C_{1} A_{1}$ is equal to $C_{1} A$, perpendicular to it, and on the side of the line $C_{1} A$ where $B$ is located. Similarly, he marked $B_{1}$ on the side of the line $C_{1} B$ that contains $A$, such that $C_{1} B_{1}$ is perpendicular to $C_{1} B$ and equal to it. He then stood at the intersection of the lines $A B_{1}$ and $B A_{1}$ and planted a stake labeled $D_{1}$. He then marked points $D_{2}$ and $D_{3}$ in the same way from $C_{2}$ and $C_{3}$, respectively, as he did from $C_{1}$ to mark $D_{1}$. Finally, he found the point $K$ that is equidistant from $D_{1}, D_{2}, D_{3}$, and buried the treasure there. - A few years later, upon returning, he found that the tide had washed away two of the trees. How can he find the location of his buried treasure?

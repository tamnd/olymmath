To introduce rectangular (Cartesian) coordinates on a plane, a reference (repère - marker) is constructed, consisting of the origin - point $O$ and two unit vectors (orths) $\vec{i}$ and $\vec{j}$, perpendicular to each other. The vector $\vec{i}$ defines the direction of the $O x$ axis, and the vector $\vec{j}$ defines the direction of the $O y$ axis. Symbolically, this reference is denoted as: $(0, \vec{i}, \vec{j})$. The position of a point $M$ on the plane is determined by the radius vector $\overrightarrow{O M}=\vec{r}$ of this point. By decomposing it into vectors $\vec{i}$ and $\vec{j}$, we get $\vec{r}=\overrightarrow{x i}+y \vec{j}$. The numbers $x$ and $y$, determined by this decomposition, are called the coordinates of the point $M$, and the vectors $\overrightarrow{x i}$ and $\vec{y}$ are called the components of the vector $\vec{r}$, or its projections onto the $O x$ axis and the $O y$ axis.

Prove that the projection of a vector onto an axis has the following properties:

a) when a vector is multiplied by a number, its projection is multiplied by the same number;

b) the projection of the sum of vectors is equal to the sum of the projections of the addends.

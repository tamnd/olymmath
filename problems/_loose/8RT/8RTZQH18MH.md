At a chemistry conference, there were $k$ scientists, chemists, and alchemists, with chemists outnumbering the alchemists. It is known that chemists always tell the truth to any question, while alchemists sometimes tell the truth and sometimes lie. A mathematician attending the conference wants to determine whether each scientist is a chemist or an alchemist. To do this, he can ask any scientist the question: "Is such-and-such a chemist or an alchemist?" (In particular, he can ask whether the scientist himself is a chemist or an alchemist.) Prove that the mathematician can determine this in $2 k-3$ questions.

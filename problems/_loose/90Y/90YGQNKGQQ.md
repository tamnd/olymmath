A group of psychologists has developed a test that, upon completion, each person receives a score - a number $Q$ - an indicator of their mental abilities (the higher the $Q$, the greater the abilities). The rating of a country is taken as the arithmetic mean of the $Q$ values of all its residents.

a) A group of citizens of country A emigrated to country B. Show that in this case, the ratings of both countries could have increased.

b) After this, a group of citizens of country B (which may include former emigrants from A) emigrated to country A. Is it possible that the ratings of both countries increased again?

c) A group of citizens of country A emigrated to country B, and a group of citizens of B emigrated to country C. As a result, the ratings of each country were higher than the initial ones. After this, the direction of migration flows changed to the opposite - some residents of C moved to B, and some residents of B moved to A. It turned out that as a result, the ratings of all three countries increased again (compared to those that were after the first move, but before the start of the second). (At least, this is what the information agencies of these countries claim.) Could this be possible (if yes, then how, if no, then why)?

(It is assumed that the $Q$ of the citizens did not change during the considered time, no one died or was born.)

The median of two positive fractions is understood to be the fraction whose numerator and denominator are the sums of the numerators and denominators, respectively, of the simplified forms of the original fractions. Now consider three fractions written next to each other, where the middle one is equal to the median of the two outer ones. We can arbitrarily delete one of the outer ones and write the median of the remaining two as the third. Thus, we get a similar triplet to the starting one. Let $0<q<1$ be any fraction. Show that starting from the triplet $\left(\frac{0}{1}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{1}\right)$, the sequence of the above steps can exactly reach a triplet whose middle element is $q$.

There are three planes $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ intersecting in a line $s$; further, there are three points $A, B, C$ on a line $g$; finally, there is an arbitrary point $P$ which does not lie on the line $s$. Draw a line $\ell$ through the point $P$ such that if the intersection points of this line with the planes $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are $A^{\prime}, B^{\prime}, C^{\prime}$, then $A^{\prime} B^{\prime}=A B$ and $B^{\prime} C^{\prime}=B C$.

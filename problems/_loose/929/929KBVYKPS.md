Prove that if, along with ordinary points and lines, we consider infinitely distant ones, then

a) through any two points there passes a unique line;

b) any two lines lying in the same plane intersect at a unique point;

c) the central projection of one plane onto another is a one-to-one mapping.

Definition. A mapping $P$ of a plane $\alpha$ onto a plane $\beta$ is called projective if it is a composition of central projections and affine transformations, i.e., if there exist planes $\alpha_{0}=\alpha, \alpha_{1}, \ldots, \alpha_{n}=\beta$ and mappings $P_{i}$ of the planes $\alpha_{i}$ onto $\alpha_{i+1}$, each of which is either a central projection or an affine transformation, and $P$ is the composition of the transformations $P_{i}$. In the case where the plane $\alpha$ coincides with the plane $\beta$, the mapping $P$ is called a projective transformation of the plane $\alpha$. The preimage of the infinitely distant line will be called the exceptional line of the given projective transformation.

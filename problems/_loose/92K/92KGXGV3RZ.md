Four football players were chatting on the train, and four young students were listening to them. When three football players got off, the boys asked the fourth one which team he played for. The answer: "One of us plays for Honvéd, one for Vasas, one for Dorog, the rest you can figure out from the conversation you overheard." - The boys then opined as follows:

I. From the conversation, I gathered that if the bearded one is from Dorog, then the tall one is from Vasas, because he said that $\ldots$

II. I gathered that if the tall one plays for Vasas, then the brown-haired one is from Dorog, because . . .

III. If the brown-haired one is not a member of Honvéd, then the bearded one is from Vasas, because ...

IV. I think that if the tall one is from Honvéd, then the brown-haired one is from Vasas.

The fourth football player said: "It's enough that all four of your statements are correct. By working together, you can already get the answer, and in fact, one of your statements is not even necessary to solve the question."

Which team does the tall, the brown-haired, and the bearded player belong to, and which of the boys' statements can be omitted?

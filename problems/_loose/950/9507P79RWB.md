Prove that continued fractions are related to the Euclidean algorithm in the following way. Let $m<n$ be two natural numbers. Apply the Euclidean algorithm to them: $n=a_{0} m+r_{1}, m=a_{1} r_{1}+r_{2}, r_{1}=a_{2} r_{2}+r_{3}, \ldots, r_{s-1}=a_{s} r_{s}$. Let $x_{k}=\left[0 ; a_{k}, a_{k+1}, \ldots, a_{s}\right]$ for $k=0,1, \ldots, s$. Then $x_{0}=\frac{m}{n}$, $x_{1}=\frac{r_{1}}{m}, x_{2}=\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}, \ldots, x_{s}=\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s-1}}$.

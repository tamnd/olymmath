Given in the plane are the point $O$ and the distinct lines $x, y$, and $e$ passing through it. We introduce an operation $\oplus$ among the points of the line $x$. If $P$ and $Q$ are points on $x$, then the point $P \oplus Q$ is obtained as follows: the intersection point of the line through $P$ parallel to $y$ and $e$ is $A$. The intersection point of the line through $A$ parallel to $x$ and $y$ is $B$. The intersection point of the line through $B$ parallel to $e$ and the line through $Q$ parallel to $y$ is $C$. The intersection point of the line through $C$ parallel to $x$ and $e$ is $D$. Finally, the intersection point of the line through $D$ parallel to $y$ and $x$ is $P \oplus Q$. Show that for any points $P, Q, R$ on the line $x$:
a) $O \oplus P = P$,
b) $P \oplus Q = Q \oplus P$,
c) $(P \oplus Q) \oplus R = P \oplus (Q \oplus R)$.

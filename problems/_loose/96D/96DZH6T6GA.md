Let $u, v$ be positive integers. If their least common multiple is $n$, we denote this as $[u, v]=n$, and say that the least common multiple of the ordered pair of positive integers $(u, v)$ is $n$ (of course, the least common multiple of the ordered pair of positive integers $(v, u)$ is also $n$). Prove that the number of ordered pairs of positive integers whose least common multiple is $n$ is exactly equal to the number of positive divisors of $n^{2}$.

For the ordered pairs I $(a, b)$ and II $\left(a^{\prime}, b^{\prime}\right)$, we associate the ordered pair $(\alpha, \beta)$, where $\alpha=a a^{\prime}, \beta=b a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}$, and denote this as: $(a, b)^{*}\left(a^{\prime}, b^{\prime}\right)=(\alpha, \beta)=\left(a a^{\prime}, b a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\right)$. For example, $(-3,4 / 5)^{*}(-2 / 3,1)=(2,7 / 15)$. (By order, we mean that one of the two numbers in the pairs, or one of the two pairs, is designated as the first.) The pairs $(u, v)$ and $\left(u^{\prime}, v^{\prime}\right)$ are said to be equal if and only if $u=u^{\prime}$ and $v=v^{\prime}$. Show that

$$
\left[(a, b)^{*}\left(a^{\prime}, b^{\prime}\right)\right]^{*}\left(a^{\prime \prime}, b^{\prime \prime}\right)=(a, b)^{*}\left[\left(a^{\prime}, b^{\prime}\right)^{*}\left(a^{\prime \prime}, b^{\prime \prime}\right)\right]
$$

Find an ordered pair $(x, y)$ such that for any $(a, b)$, $(a, b)^{*}(x, y)=(a, b)$, and then prove that in this case

$$
(a, b)^{*}(x, y)=(x, y)^{*}(a, b) \text {. }
$$

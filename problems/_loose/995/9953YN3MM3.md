Let the function $f(x)$ be differentiable at the point $x_{0}$. The line passing through the points $\left(x_{0}, y_{0}\right)$ and $\left(x_{1}, y_{1}\right)$, where $y_{0}=f\left(x_{0}\right)$ and $y_{1}=f\left(x_{1}\right)$, is given by the equation $y-y_{0}=k\left(x_{1}\right)\left(x-x_{0}\right)$. Prove that $\lim _{x_{1} \rightarrow x_{0}} k\left(x_{1}\right)=f^{\prime}\left(x_{0}\right)$.

The line $y-f\left(x_{0}\right)=f^{\prime}\left(x_{0}\right)\left(x-x_{0}\right)$ is called the tangent to the graph $y=f(x)$ at the point $\left(x_{0}, f\left(x_{0}\right)\right)$.

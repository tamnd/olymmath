Consider a geographical map $M$ on a sphere. Suppose that at each vertex of the map $M$, exactly three countries meet, and that no country border is a loop (i.e., each border contains at least two vertices). The map is colored with colors $A, B, C, D$ such that adjacent countries are colored differently. We use the term "country" for all regions on the map, regardless of whether they are land or water.

We will call a country even or odd depending on whether the number of its borders is even or odd. Prove that the total number of odd countries that are colored with one of two colors, say $A$ or $B$, is always an even number.

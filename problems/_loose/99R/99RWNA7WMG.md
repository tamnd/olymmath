Three points $P_1, P_2,$ and $P_3$ and three lines $\ell_1, \ell_2,$ and $\ell_3$ lie in the plane such that none of the three points lie on any of the three lines. For (not necessarily distinct) integers $i$ and $j$ between 1 and 3 inclusive, we call a line $\ell$ $(i, j)$ -*good* if the reflection of $P_i$ across $\ell$ lies on $\ell_j$ , and call it *excellent* if there are two distinct pairs $(i_1, j_1)$ and $(i_2, j_2)$ for which it is good. Suppose that exactly $N$ excellent lines exist. Compute the largest possible value of $N$ .

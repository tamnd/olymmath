Divide a square with a side length of 2 into four equal areas, such that in one of these parts, there exist three points that can form an equilateral triangle with a side length greater than 1. The number of such divisions that satisfy the above property
(A) does not exist.
(B) is exactly one.
(C) is finite but more than one.
(D) is infinitely many.
(1st "Hope Cup" National Mathematics Invitational, 1990)

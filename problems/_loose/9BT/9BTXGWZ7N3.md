The fiftieth question: Let $p$ be an odd prime, and integers $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}, \ldots, a_{p-1}$ all be coprime with $p$. If for $k=1, 2, \ldots, p-2$, we have $a_{1}^{k} + a_{2}^{k} + a_{3}^{k} + \cdots + a_{p-1}^{k} \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, prove: The remainders of $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}, \ldots, a_{p-1}$ when divided by $p$ are all distinct.

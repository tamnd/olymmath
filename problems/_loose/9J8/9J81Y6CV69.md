On the side $A_{1} A_{2}$ of a regular $n$-gon $A_{1} A_{2} A_{3} \ldots A_{n}$, a point $M_{1}$ is taken. This point is projected from vertex $A_{n}$ to point $M_{2}$ on side $A_{2} A_{3}$ or its extension (i.e., $M_{2}$ is the point of intersection of line $A_{n} M_{1}$ with line $A_{2} A_{3}$); then point $M_{2}$ is projected from vertex $A_{1}$ to point $M_{3}$ on side $A_{3} A_{4}$; point $M_{3}$ is projected from vertex $A_{2}$ to point $M_{4}$ on side $A_{4} A_{5}$, and so on. The process does not stop after one complete traversal of all sides of the $n$-gon but continues indefinitely. Prove that

a) if $n=4$, then the point $M_{13}$ on side $A_{1} A_{2}$, which is obtained after three complete traversals of all sides of the polygon, coincides with $M_{1}$ (and, therefore, $M_{14}$ coincides with $M_{2}$, $M_{15}$ coincides with $M_{3}$, and so on);

b) if $n=6$, then the point $M_{13}$ on side $A_{1} A_{2}$, which is obtained after two complete traversals of all sides of the polygon, coincides with $M_{1}$ (and, therefore, $M_{14}$ coincides with $M_{2}$, $M_{15}$ coincides with $M_{3}$, and so on);

c) if $n=10$, then the point $M_{11}$ on side $A_{1} A_{2}$, which is obtained after one complete traversal of all sides of the polygon, coincides with $M_{1}$ (and, therefore, $M_{12}$ coincides with $M_{2}$, $M_{13}$ coincides with $M_{3}$, and so on).

Note. It can be proven that if $n$ is different from 4, 6, and 10, then none of the points $M_{k n+1}, k=1,2,3, \ldots$, which are obtained in the described manner on side $A_{1} A_{2}$, will coincide with the initial point $M_{1}$, i.e., the process will continue indefinitely without the obtained points starting to repeat.

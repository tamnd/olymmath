For a set $A \subset \mathbb{N}$, we say it is good if for some natural number $n$, the equation $x-y=n$ has infinitely many solutions $(x, y)$, where $x \in A, y \in A$. If $\mathbb{N}=A_{1} \cup A_{2} \cup \ldots \cup A_{1988}$, then at least one of the sets $A_{1}, A_{2}, \ldots, A_{1988}$ is good. Prove it!

At the mathematics camp, a circle leader proposes to the $n$ students $(n \geq 3)$ in his group to select the student who will take on the blackboard duty according to the following method:

The students are numbered $P_{1}, P_{2}, \ldots, P_{n}$ and stand in a circle in this order. In the clockwise direction, $P_{1}, P_{2}, \ldots$ follows $P_{n}$ with $P_{1}$ again. A coin toss is used to decide whether $P_{1}$ or $P_{2}$ will be eliminated from the circle. If the coin lands on heads, $P_{1}$ is eliminated; if it lands on tails, $P_{2}$ is eliminated.

The elimination then continues with the two students who follow the most recently eliminated student in the specified clockwise direction. If the coin lands on heads, the first of these two is eliminated; if it lands on tails, the second is eliminated. This process is repeated until only one student remains, who is then designated as the duty holder.

a) Calculate, for the case $n=3$, the probabilities $W_{1}, W_{2}, W_{3}$ that $P_{1}, P_{2}$, or $P_{3}$, respectively, will be designated as the duty holder.

b) Prove for any $n \geq 3$ that the selection method is unfair, i.e., that the probability of being designated as the duty holder is not the same for all students $P_{1}, P_{2}, \ldots, P_{n}$. Note:

If a random event $A$ occurs as a result of any one of $m$ events out of a total of $N$ possible events (which are mutually exclusive and equally likely), the probability of event $A$ is denoted by $p=\frac{M}{N}$.

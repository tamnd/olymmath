Let $\mathrm{n}$ and $\mathrm{b}$ be positive integers. We say that $\mathrm{n}$ is "$\mathrm{b}$-distinguishable" if there exists a set of $\mathrm{n}$ distinct positive integers, each less than $\mathrm{b}$, such that for any two different subsets $\mathrm{U}$ and $\mathrm{V}$, the sum of the elements in $\mathrm{U}$ is not equal to the sum of the elements in $\mathrm{V}$.
(1) Prove that 8 is "100-distinguishable";
(2) Prove that 9 is "100-indistinguishable".

During a written history test, two poor students, Jean and Pierre, were sitting next to each other. They needed to indicate in their work the dates of the following two events: the Battle of Marignano and the assassination of Henry IV ${ }^{1}$. Jean remembered that the dates were 1515 and 1610, but he had no idea which date corresponded to which event. So he whispered to Pierre, knowing that Pierre gives the correct historical dates three out of four times. However, Pierre is mischievous and in one out of four cases, he deceives Jean by suggesting the opposite of what he believes to be correct.

Jean doesn't know whether he should follow Pierre's advice or answer randomly. Can you help him?

${ }^{1}$ The Battle of Marignano (1515, Marignano-a town in northern Italy) between the army of the French king and the Swiss mercenaries of the Duke of Milan ended with the victory of the French and led to the temporary establishment of French dominance over Lombardy.

The French king Henry IV was stabbed in 1610 by a fanatical Catholic, Ravaillac. (Both dates are among those that are supposed to be known according to the French school curriculum.) - Ed. note.

In a group of $n$ people $(n \geqq 4)$, some know each other. The relationship "knowing each other" is mutual: if person $A$ knows person $B$, then $B$ also knows $A$, and we call them a pair of acquaintances.

a) If among any four people there are at least four pairs of acquaintances, then any two people who do not know each other have a common acquaintance. Prove this.

b) Determine for which $n \geqq 4$ there exists a group of people in which there are at least three pairs of acquaintances among any four people, and at the same time, some two people do not know each other and do not have a common acquaintance.

c) Decide whether in a group of six people, there can be exactly three pairs of acquaintances and exactly three pairs of strangers in every quartet.

We draw a circle of radius $r$ around the origin of the Cartesian coordinate system of the plane. Let $\delta(r)$ denote the distance of the closest point with integer coordinates to the circle.

Prove that $\delta(r)$ can be arbitrarily small if $r$ is chosen sufficiently large.

(The distance of a point from the circle is measured by drawing a line through the point and the center of the circle, and taking the smaller of the distances from the point to the intersection points of the line and the circle.)

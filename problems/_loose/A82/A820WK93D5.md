Given two non-intersecting circles with centers at points $O_{1}$ and $O_{2}$. Let $a_{1}$ and $a_{2}$ be the internal tangents to these circles, $a_{3}$ and $a_{4}$ be the external tangents to them. Further, let $a_{5}$ and $a_{6}$ be the tangents to the circle centered at $O_{1}$, drawn from point $O_{2}$, and $a_{7}$ and $a_{8}$ be the tangents to the circle centered at $O_{2}$, drawn from point $O_{1}$. Denote by $O$ the point of intersection of $a_{1}$ and $a_{2}$. Prove that two circles can be drawn with center at point $O$ such that the first circle is tangent to $a_{3}$ and $a_{4}$, the second circle is tangent to $a_{5}, a_{6}, a_{7}, a_{8}$, and the radius of the second circle is half the radius of the first.

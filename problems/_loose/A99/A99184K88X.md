Let $n$ be an integer greater than 3. We select three distinct numbers from the set $\{2,3, \cdots, n\}$, and connect these three numbers using addition or multiplication, and can use parentheses to distinguish the order of addition and multiplication operations. However, in each expression, the three selected numbers appear exactly once. Consider all expressions obtained in this way.
(1) If the three selected numbers are all greater than $\frac{n}{2}$, prove that the values of all expressions formed by these three numbers are distinct.
(2) Let $p$ be a prime number and $p \leqslant \sqrt{n}$. If the smallest of the three selected numbers is $p$, and the values of all expressions formed by these three numbers are not all distinct, prove that the number of ways to select such three numbers is exactly equal to the number of positive divisors of $p-1$.

The first cross sum of a natural number $z$ is understood to be the cross sum formed in the usual way. If the first cross sum of $z$ is a number with more than one digit, then its cross sum is called the second cross sum of $z$.

Examples: The first cross sum of 98 is $9+8=17$, the second cross sum of 98 is $1+7=8$. The first cross sum of 43 is $4+3=7$, a second cross sum of 43 is not defined.

If the second cross sum of $z$ is a number with more than one digit, then its cross sum is called the third cross sum of $z$. In a similar manner, higher cross sums are defined if applicable.

a) Determine the number of natural numbers from 10 to 1000 for which no second cross sum is defined!

b) Determine the number of natural numbers from 10 to 1000 for which the second, but not the third, cross sum is defined!

c) Determine the smallest natural number for which a fourth cross sum is defined!

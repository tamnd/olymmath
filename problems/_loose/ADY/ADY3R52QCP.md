For every six-digit natural number $n$, whose unit digit is different from zero, one can form the number $n^{\prime}$ obtained by writing the digits of $n$ in reverse order. Subsequently, the number $n+n^{\prime}$ can be calculated.

a) Form some examples! Determine if there is a prime number by which the number $n+n^{\prime}$ is divisible in your examples! State a conjecture!

b) Try to prove your conjecture!

c) Now let $k$ be any even natural number greater than zero. For every $k$-digit natural number $n$, whose unit digit is different from zero, one can form the number obtained by writing the digits of $n$ in reverse order.

Does a corresponding statement like in a), b) also hold for $n+n^{\prime}$?

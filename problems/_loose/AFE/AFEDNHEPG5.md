We placed 4 circular disks on the plane such that the first two are concentric, the third and fourth touch the first two, as well as each other, and all points of contact lie on a single straight line. Show that the total area of the regions covered once in the plane is equal to the total area of the regions covered twice (i.e., by two disks). For first-year students, it is sufficient to draw the arrangement of the circles and verify the statement with 2 numerical examples by appropriately choosing the radii of the circles.

Through the opposite edges $AB$ and $CD$ of the tetrahedron $ABCD$, two parallel planes are drawn. Similarly, two parallel planes are drawn through the edges $BC$ and $AD$, and also through the edges $AC$ and $BD$. These six planes define a parallelepiped. Prove that if the tetrahedron $ABCD$ is orthocentric (its altitudes intersect at one point), then all edges of the parallelepiped are equal; and if the tetrahedron $ABCD$ is equifacial (all its faces are equal triangles), then the parallelepiped is rectangular.

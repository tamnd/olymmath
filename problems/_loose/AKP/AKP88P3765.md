(See [36].) Let $\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n \geqslant 1}$ be i.i.d. random variables having a gamma distribution with parameters $(\alpha, 1)$. Show that

$$
Y_{\alpha}=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{X_{n}}{n^{2}}, Z_{\alpha}=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{X_{n}}{(n-1 / 2)^{2}}
$$

- are well-defined random variables as almost sure and $L^{1}$ limits, whose Laplace transforms are given by the formulas

$$
\mathrm{E} e^{-\lambda Y_{\alpha}}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{2 \lambda}}{\operatorname{sh} \sqrt{2 \lambda}}\right)^{\alpha} \text { and } \mathrm{E} e^{-\lambda Z_{\alpha}}=\left(\frac{1}{\operatorname{ch} \sqrt{2 \lambda}}\right)^{\alpha}, \lambda>0 .
$$

To this end, use Euler's formulas

$$
\operatorname{sh} z=z \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z^{2}}{n^{2} \pi^{2}}\right), \operatorname{ch} z=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z^{2}}{(n-1 / 2)^{2} \pi^{2}}\right)
$$

Remark. The distributions of the variables $Y_{\alpha}$ and $Z_{\alpha}$ often arise when considering the distributions of various functionals of Brownian motion (see [36] for more details).

Four non-adjacent corners of a cuboid $V=abc$ are beveled by planar cuts such that a segment $x$ is removed from each edge. The remaining corners are treated similarly, with the cuts being made such that at each corner, exactly the remaining edge segments that were left after the first beveling are removed.

How large must the segment $x$ be chosen so that the resulting fourteen-faced polyhedron, bounded by eight triangles and six parallelograms, has an extreme volume?

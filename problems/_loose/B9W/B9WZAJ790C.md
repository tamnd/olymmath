For a finite set of points $A$ in the plane, by the triangulation of $A$, we mean the decomposition of the convex hull of $A$ into non-overlapping triangles, where the vertices of each triangle are chosen from the points of $A$, and except for the vertices, no triangle contains any other point of $A$. Prove that any two triangulations of the set $A$ contain the same number of triangles.

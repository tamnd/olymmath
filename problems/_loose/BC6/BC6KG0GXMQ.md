A general wants to arrange his soldiers in identical square formations (of course, there must be more than one person in a square) for a parade, but he does not know how many soldiers (from 1 to 37) are in the hospital. Prove that the general can have such a number of soldiers that, regardless of the number of soldiers in the hospital, he can fulfill his intention. For example, an army of 9 people can be arranged in a $3 \times 3$ square, and if one person is sick, in two $2 \times 2$ squares.

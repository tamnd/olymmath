The seventy-fifth problem: Given the integer sequence $\mathrm{a}_{0}, \mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}, \ldots$ satisfying:
(1) $a_{n+3}=3 a_{n+2}-3 a_{n+1}+a_{n}, n \in N$;
(2) $2 \mathrm{a}_{1}=\mathrm{a}_{0}+\mathrm{a}_{2}-2$;
(3) For any positive integer $m$, there exists $k \geq m$ in the sequence $a_{0}, a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots$ such that $a_{k+1}$ and $a_{k}$ are both perfect squares.
Prove: Every term in the sequence $\mathrm{a}_{0}, \mathrm{a}_{1}, \mathrm{a}_{2}, \ldots$ is a perfect square.

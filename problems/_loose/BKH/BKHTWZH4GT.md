Provide examples showing that the assumption that the system of sets $\mathscr{A}$ involved in the Carathéodory theorem is an algebra is essential both for the fact of the possibility of extending a probability measure $\mathrm{P}$ defined on the system $\mathscr{A}$ to a measure on the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathscr{F}=\sigma(\mathscr{A})$, and for the uniqueness of the extension. Specifically, it is required

(a) to construct a space $\Omega$ and systems of its subsets $\mathscr{E}$ and $\mathscr{F}$ such that $\mathscr{E}$ is not an algebra, but $\sigma(\mathscr{E})=\mathscr{F}$, and a probability measure $\mathrm{P}$ on $\mathscr{E}$ that cannot be extended to a probability measure on the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathscr{F}$;

(b) to construct a space $\Omega$ and systems of its subsets $\mathscr{E}$ and $\mathscr{F}$ with the property $\mathscr{F}=\sigma(\mathscr{E})$, as well as two different probability measures $\mathrm{P}$ and $\mathrm{Q}$ on the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathscr{F}$ that coincide on the sets from $\mathscr{E}$.

a) Let $f\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}\right)$ be a symmetric polynomial. Prove that there exists a polynomial $g\left(y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n}\right)$ such that $f\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}\right)=g\left(\sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{n}\right)$. Moreover, the polynomial $g$ is unique (the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials).

b) Prove that if $f\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}\right)$ is a symmetric polynomial with integer coefficients, then $f\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}\right)=g\left(\sigma_{1}, \ldots, \sigma_{n}\right)$, where $g$ is also a polynomial with integer coefficients.

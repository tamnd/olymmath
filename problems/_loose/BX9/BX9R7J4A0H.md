Let $(A,+,\cdot)$ be a finite unit ring, with $n\geq 3$ elements in which there exist **exactly** $\dfrac {n+1}2$ perfect squares (e.g. a number $b\in A$ is called a perfect square if and only if there exists an $a\in A$ such that $b=a^2$). Prove that

a) $1+1$ is invertible;

b) $(A,+,\cdot)$ is a field.

*Proposed by Marian Andronache*

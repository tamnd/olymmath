Let $\mathrm{t}_{1}<\mathrm{t}_{2}<\ldots<\mathrm{t}_{\mathrm{n}}$ be $\mathrm{n}\left(\mathrm{n} \in \mathrm{Z}^{+}\right)$ positive integers, and there are $\mathrm{t}_{\mathrm{n}}+1$ players participating in a chess tournament. Any two players play at most one game against each other. Prove: There exists a match arrangement such that the following two conditions are simultaneously satisfied:
(1) The number of games each player plays belongs to the set $\left\{t_{1}, t_{2}, \ldots, t_{n}\right\}$;
(2) For each $\mathrm{i}(1 \leq \mathrm{i} \leq \mathrm{n})$, there exists a player who has played exactly $\mathrm{t}_{\mathrm{i}}$ games.

Given positive integers $a, b, p$ are pairwise coprime, and $p$ is a prime number. The set $S=\{x \mid x=a+n b, n \in \mathbf{N}\}$.
(1) $T=\{x \mid x=a+n b, n=0,1, \cdots, p-1\}$.

If for any $i \neq j, i \in T, j \in T$, we have
$$
y^{i}+i \not \equiv y^{j}+j(\bmod p),
$$

what conditions should the positive integer $y$ satisfy?
(2) For any $y, t \in \mathbf{Z}_{+}$, does there always exist $r \in S$, such that $y^{r}+r \equiv t(\bmod p)$? Answer and explain the reason.

Prove that if a tetrahedron is orthocentric, i.e., such that the lines containing its altitudes intersect at one point, then: a) each pair of opposite edges are mutually perpendicular; b) if one of the dihedral angles at any vertex of the tetrahedron is a right angle, then the other two dihedral angles are also right angles; c) the sums of the squares of the lengths of its opposite edges are equal; d) any vertex projects onto the orthocenter of the opposite face (the point of intersection of the lines containing the altitudes of the face).

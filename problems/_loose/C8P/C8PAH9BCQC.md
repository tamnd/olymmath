Two Ships. Two ships set out from one port to another, located 200 nautical miles away, and return back. The "Mary Jane" travels in one direction at a speed of 12 miles/h and on the return trip at a speed of 8 miles/h, spending a total of $41 \frac{2}{3}$ hours for the entire journey. The "Elizabeth Anne" travels at 10 miles/h in both directions, spending a total of 40 hours for the entire journey.

We see that both ships travel at an average speed of 10 miles/h. Why does the "Mary Jane" take more time for the entire journey than the "Elizabeth Anne"? How can we explain this small paradox?

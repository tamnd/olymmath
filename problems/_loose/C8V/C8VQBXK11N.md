[8] Initially, a finite number of cells on a white checkered plane are colored black. On the plane lies a checkered paper polygon M, which contains more than one cell. It can be moved in any direction by any distance without rotating, but so that after the move it lies "by cells". If after a certain move exactly one cell of M lies on a white cell of the plane, this white cell is colored black and the next move is made. Prove that there exists a white cell that will never be colored black, no matter how many times M is moved according to the described rules.

(D. Zakharov)

Let $l_{1}, l_{2}, \ldots, l_{n}$ be several lines on a plane, not all of which are parallel. Prove that it is possible to uniquely choose a point $X_{1}, X_{2}, \ldots, X_{n}$ on each of these lines such that the perpendicular erected to the line $l_{k}$ at the point $X_{k}$ (for any natural $k<n$) passes through the point $X_{k+1}$, and the perpendicular erected to the line $l_{n}$ at the point $X_{n}$ passes through the point $X_{1}$.

Try to formulate and prove an analogous theorem in space.

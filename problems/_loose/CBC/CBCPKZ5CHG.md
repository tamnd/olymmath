In this problem, we consider figures drawn on graph paper. Each such figure consists of whole cells glued together along their sides, forming a single piece. If some cells are cut out of the figure, it may fall apart into several pieces (when two pieces touch only at a corner, they fall apart).

(a) Provide an example of a figure consisting of more than two cells that will not fall apart no matter which cell is cut out. (1 point)

(b) Provide an example of a figure from which exactly 4 cells can be cut out so that the figure without these four cells does not fall apart, and there are four ways to cut out a single cell so that the remaining part falls apart. (3 points)

(c) Five identical figures were stacked one on top of the other. Then one figure was shifted one cell to the left, another one cell to the right, a third one cell down, and a fourth one cell up. Then one cell was cut out from each figure, and all the cut-out cells ended up strictly one on top of the other. Could it be that one figure fell apart while the other four did not? (6 points)

(d) Prove that any figure made of 533 cells can be made to fall apart by cutting out no more than two cells. (10 points)

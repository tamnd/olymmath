Dividing the Perimeter of a Triangle into Equal Parts. Take an arbitrary triangle. Of course, we can intersect it with a line so as to divide its perimeter in half. We can even predefine the direction of the intersecting line. If we do this twice, in two different directions, the lines will intersect at some point $Q$. Then through point $Q$ will pass two lines that divide the perimeter in half.

Does there exist a point through which three such lines can pass? If it exists, how can it be found?

15a. Center of Mass. Let $P$ be the center of mass of three points $A, B, C$ (when speaking of the center of mass of any three points, we will assume that the masses placed at these points are equal). Let $A_{1}, B_{1}, C_{1}$ be the centers of mass of the following sets of three points: $B, C, P ; C, A, P ; A, B, P$.

Prove that the center of mass of the set $A_{1}, B_{1}, C_{1}$ is again the point $P$.

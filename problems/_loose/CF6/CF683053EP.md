Given a non-zero $x$, the value of $x^{8}$ can be found in three arithmetic operations: $x^{2}=x \cdot x, x^{4}=x^{2} \cdot x^{2}$, $x^{8}=x^{4} \cdot x^{4}$, and $x^{15}$ in five operations: the first three are the same, then $x^{8} \cdot x^{8}=x^{16}$ and $x^{16}: x=x^{16}$. Prove that a) $x^{16}$ can be found in 12 operations (multiplications and divisions);

b) for any natural number $n$, raising $x$ to the $n$-th power can be done in no more than $1+1.5 \cdot \log _{2} n$ operations.

Consider an $m \times n$-board that is divided into unit squares. An L-triomino consists of three unit squares, a center square and two leg squares. There is an L-triomino in the top left corner so that the center square is on the corner square. In one move, the L-triomino can be rotated by multiples of $90^{\circ}$ around the center point of one of the two leg squares. For which $m$ and $n$ is it possible for the L-triomino to come to rest in the bottom right-hand corner after a finite number of such moves?

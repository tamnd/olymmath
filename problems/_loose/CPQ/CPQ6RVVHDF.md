Let $q$ be a natural number. Suppose there exist $a$ and $b$, two natural numbers such that $a^{2} - q a b + b^{2} = q$.

a) By considering the polynomial $P(x) = x^{2} - q b x + b^{2} - q$, determine an integer $c$ different from $a$ such that $c^{2} - q b c + b^{2} = q$.

b) Show that $q$ is a perfect square. (One may consider a pair $(a, b)$ of natural numbers satisfying $a^{2} - q a b + b^{2} = q$ with $a$ minimal.)

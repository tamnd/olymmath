Let the geometric sequences $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{k}$ and $b_{1}, b_{2}, \cdots, b_{k}$, and let $c_{n}=a_{n}-b_{n}(n=1,2, \cdots, k)$.
(1) Write a set of $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}$ and $b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}$, such that $c_{1}, c_{2}, c_{3}$ form an arithmetic sequence with a non-zero common difference;
(2) When $k \geqslant 4$, prove: $\left\{c_{n}\right\}$ cannot be an arithmetic sequence with a non-zero common difference.

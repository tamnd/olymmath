It is said that a permutation $\left(x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{2 n}\right)$ has the property $\mathfrak{P}$ if there exists an $i$ such that $\left|x_{i}-x_{i+1}\right|=n$.

Prove that there are more permutations that have the property $\mathfrak{P}$ than permutations that do not have it.

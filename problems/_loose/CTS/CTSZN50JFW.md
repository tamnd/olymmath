a) Find an example of distinct matrices $A, B, C \in \mathcal{M}_{4}(\mathbb{R})$, such that

$$
\operatorname{det}(A)=\operatorname{det}(B)=\operatorname{det}(C)=-1 \text { and } \operatorname{det}(A+B)>\operatorname{det}(B+C)
$$

b) Let matrices $A, B, C \in \mathcal{M}_{4}(\mathbb{R})$ with $\operatorname{det}(A)=\operatorname{det}(B)=\operatorname{det}(C)$ and $\operatorname{det}(A+B)>\operatorname{det}(B+C)$

If $\operatorname{det}(A+i \cdot B)=\operatorname{det}(B+i \cdot C)$, prove that

$$
\operatorname{det}(A+\sqrt{3} B)+\operatorname{det}(B+\sqrt{2} C)>\operatorname{det}(A+\sqrt{2} B)+\operatorname{det}(B+\sqrt{3} C)
$$

It is known that the last digits of the squares of natural numbers can be the digits $0,1,4$, 5, 6, and 9. Is it true that any group of digits can precede the last digit in them, i.e., that for any set of $n$ digits $a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots, a_{n}$, one can find a natural number whose square ends with the group of digits $a_{1} a_{2} a_{3} \ldots a_{n} b$ (where $b$ is one of the digits listed above)?

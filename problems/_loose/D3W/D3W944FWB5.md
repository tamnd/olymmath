The Catalan numbers $C_{n}, n \geqslant 1$, admit many interesting combinatorial interpretations. For example, consider the number $N_{n}$ of different ways to compute the sum of $n$ numbers $a, b, c, d, \ldots$, where each time only two numbers are added (changing the order of the addends is not allowed). For instance, for $n=3$, the sum $a+b+c$ can be computed, satisfying the requirement of "adding two numbers at a time," by placing parentheses ($\cdot$) as follows: $a+b+c=(a+(b+c))=((a+b)+c)$. (Here the number of different ways $N_{3}=2$.) For $n=4$, there are already five possibilities $\left(N_{4}=5\right)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a+b+c+d & =((a+b)+(c+d))=(((a+b)+c)+d)= \\
& =((a+(b+c))+d)=(a+((b+c)+d))= \\
& =(a+(b+(c+d))) .
\end{aligned}
$$

(a) Show that for any $n \geqslant 3$, the number $N_{n}$ of different ways to compute the sum is equal to $C_{n}$.

(b) Consider the diagonal triangulations of a regular $n$-gon, $n \geqslant 4$, i.e., partitions into triangles using non-intersecting diagonals (emanating from any vertex; clearly, the number of such diagonals emanating from one vertex is $n-3$). Show that the number $N_{n}$ of such triangulations is equal to $C_{n-1}$.

(c) Show that the Catalan numbers $C_{n}, n>1$, satisfy the recurrence relations

$$
C_{n}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} C_{i}^{*} C_{n-i}^{*}
$$

(with $C_{0}^{*}=0$ and $C_{1}^{*}=1$).

(d) Establish that the generating function $F^{*}(x)=\sum_{n \geqslant 1} C_{n}^{*} x^{n}$ of the sequence $\left(C_{n}^{*}\right)_{n \geqslant 1}$, defined by the recurrence relations $(*)$, satisfies the equation

$$
F^{*}(x)=x+\left(F^{*}(x)\right)^{2} .
$$

(e) Show that (taking into account the condition $F^{*}(0)=0$)

$$
F^{*}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-(1-4 x)^{1 / 2}\right), |x| \leqslant \frac{1}{4},
$$

and derive from this that the coefficients $C_{n}^{*}$ (of $x^{n}$) coincide, as expected, with the Catalan numbers $C_{n}$:

$$
C_{n}^{*}=-\frac{1}{2} C_{1 / 2}^{n}(-4)^{n}=\frac{1}{n} C_{2(n-1)}^{n-1}=C_{n}
$$

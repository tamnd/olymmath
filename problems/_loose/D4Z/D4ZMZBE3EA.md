If $\mathrm{P}$ and $\mathrm{Q}$ are two points in the plane, let $\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{PQ})$ be the perpendicular bisector of $\mathrm{PQ} . \mathrm{S}$ is a finite set of $n>1$ points such that: (1) if $P$ and $Q$ belong to $S$, then some point of $m(P Q)$ belongs to S, (2) if PQ, $\mathrm{P}^{\prime} \mathrm{Q}^{\prime}, \mathrm{P}^{\prime \prime} \mathrm{Q}^{\prime \prime}$ are three distinct segments, whose endpoints are all in S, then if there is a point in all of $\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{PQ}), \mathrm{m}\left(\mathrm{P}^{\prime} \mathrm{Q}^{\prime}\right), \mathrm{m}\left(\mathrm{P}^{\prime \prime} \mathrm{Q}^{\prime \prime}\right)$ it does not belong to $\mathrm{S}$. What are the possible values of $n$ ?

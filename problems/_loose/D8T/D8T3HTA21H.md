Let $\mathscr{B}_{1}$ and $\mathscr{B}_{2}$ be $\sigma$-algebras of subsets of a space $\Omega$. Will the systems of sets

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathscr{B}_{1} \cap \mathscr{B}_{2} \equiv\left\{A: A \in \mathscr{B}_{1} \text { and } A \in \mathscr{B}_{2}\right\}, \\
& \mathscr{B}_{1} \cup \mathscr{B}_{2} \equiv\left\{A: A \in \mathscr{B}_{1} \text { or } A \in \mathscr{B}_{2}\right\}
\end{aligned}
$$

be $\sigma$-algebras?

Let $\mathscr{B}_{1} \vee \mathscr{B}_{2}$ be the smallest $\sigma$-algebra $\sigma\left(\mathscr{B}_{1}, \mathscr{B}_{2}\right)$ generated by the sets from $\sigma$-algebras $\mathscr{B}_{1}$ and $\mathscr{B}_{2}$. Show that $\mathscr{B}_{1} \vee \mathscr{B}_{2}$ coincides with the smallest $\sigma$-algebra generated by sets of the form $B_{1} \cap B_{2}$, where $B_{1} \in \mathscr{B}_{1}$ and $B_{2} \in \mathscr{B}_{2}$.

On the skin of a Rhinoceros, there are folds - vertical and horizontal. If a Rhinoceros has $a$ vertical and $b$ horizontal folds on its left side, and $c$ vertical and $d$ horizontal folds on its right side, we will say that this Rhinoceros is in the state ( $a b c d$ ) or simply a Rhinoceros ( $a b c d$ ).

If a Rhinoceros scratches one of its sides up and down against a baobab, and there are two horizontal folds on that side, these two horizontal folds will be smoothed out. If there are not two such folds, nothing happens.

Similarly, if a Rhinoceros scratches one of its sides back and forth, and there are two vertical folds on that side, these two vertical folds will be smoothed out. If there are not two such folds, nothing happens.

If two folds of any kind are smoothed out on one side, then on the other side, two new folds immediately appear: one vertical and one horizontal.

Rhinoceroses often scratch themselves, against random baobabs in random directions with a random side.

Initially, in the savanna, there was a herd of Rhinoceroses (0221). Prove that after some time, a Rhinoceros (2021) will appear in the savanna.

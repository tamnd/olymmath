Let $G$ be a connected graph. A set $U \subseteq V(G)$ is called dispersed if every ray of $G$ can be separated from $U$ by finitely many vertices. (In the topology of Section 8.5, these sets are precisely the closed subsets of $V(G)$.)
(i) Prove Jung's theorem: $G$ has a normal spanning tree if and only if $V(G)$ is the union of countably many dispersed sets.
(ii) Derive: If $G$ has a normal spanning tree, then so does every connected subgraph of $G$.

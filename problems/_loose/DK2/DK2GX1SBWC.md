In a boarding school, $512$ students learn $9$ disciplines. These students live in $256$ double rooms; two students are called *neighbors* if they share a room. It is known that for every two students the sets of disciplines in which they are interested are distinct (in particular, exactly one student is interested in nothing). Prove that all the students can be arranged in a circle so that (i) each two neighbors are adjacent in a circle, and (ii) for each two adjacent students which are not neighbors, one of them is interested in all the disciplines in which the other is interested, and the first one is interested in exactly one additional discipline.

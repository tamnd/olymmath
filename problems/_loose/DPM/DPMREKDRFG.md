\section*{Problem $4-340914=341014$}

Arne draws a triangle $A B C$ and a circle $k_{1}$ chosen such that it passes through $B$, intersects the segment $A B$ at a point $X$ different from $B$, and intersects the segment $B C$ at a point $Y$ different from $B$. Then Arne constructs the circumcircle $k_{2}$ of triangle $A C X$ and the circumcircle $k_{3}$ of triangle $A C Y$.

He then notices that in his drawing, the centers $M_{1}, M_{2}, M_{3}$ of the circles $k_{1}, k_{2}, k_{3}$ lie on a common line; he finds this remarkable.

Britta says: For any triangle $A B C$, there are infinitely many possibilities for the circle $k_{1}$ such that the three mentioned centers always lie on a common line, and there are also infinitely many possibilities for $k_{1}$ where this is not the case.

Is Britta right?

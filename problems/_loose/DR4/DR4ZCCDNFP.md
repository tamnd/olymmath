Let $p>5$ be a prime, and $p \equiv 1(\bmod 4)$. For an integer $a$, if there exists an integer $x$, such that $x^{2} \equiv a(\bmod p)$, then $a$ is called a "quadratic residue modulo $p$". Prove: for each integer $a$, there exist integers $b, c$, such that $a=b+c$, and $b, c$ are both not quadratic residues modulo $p$.
(2015, Tsinghua University Autumn Camp Advanced Mathematics Test)

On the sides of the square $A_{1} A_{2} A_{3} A_{4}$, we measure a distance of $x$ from each vertex in both directions. By connecting the endpoints of these $x$ distances in the same direction, we obtain the squares $B_{1} B_{2} B_{3} B_{4}$ and $C_{1} C_{2} C_{3} C_{4}$. The sides of these two squares intersect at the vertices of the octagon $D_{1} D_{2} \ldots D_{8}$.

$1^{0}$. For which value of $x$ will this octagon be regular?

$2^{0}$. The vertices of this regular octagon that lie on the diagonals of the original square form another square. Repeating the previous construction on this square, we obtain another regular octagon, and so on. What are the sums of the perimeters and areas of the infinitely many regular octagons thus obtained?

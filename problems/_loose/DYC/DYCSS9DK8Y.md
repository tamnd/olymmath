Let $x$ be the number of oranges the father had before distributing them among his sons. He gives the first son a number of oranges equal to $\frac{x}{2}+\frac{1}{2}$; he is left with $x-\left(\frac{x}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{x}{2}-\frac{1}{2}$. Then he gives the second son half of the remainder, or $\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)$, increased by $\frac{1}{2}$; this amounts to $\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{x}{4}+\frac{1}{4}$. Then he is left with $\frac{x}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-$ $\left(\frac{x}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\right)=\frac{x}{4}-\frac{3}{4}$. Finally, he gives the third son $\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x}{4}-\frac{3}{4}\right)+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{x}{8}+\frac{1}{8}$. Since he has nothing left, it must be that $\frac{x}{8}+\frac{1}{8}=\frac{x}{4}-\frac{3}{4}$, from which $x=7$.

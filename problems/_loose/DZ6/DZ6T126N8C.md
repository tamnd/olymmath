We aim to demonstrate Wilson's theorem (not very useful in practice) in this exercise:

"For any integer $p \geqslant 2, p$ is prime if and only if $(p-1)!\equiv-1[p]$".

1. Let $n \geqslant 2$ be a non-prime integer and not the square of a prime number. Show that there exist $a, b$ between 1 and $n-1$ and distinct such that $n=a b$.

Deduce that $(n-1)!\equiv 0[n]$.

2. Let $n>4$ be a non-prime integer. Suppose there exists a prime number $p$ such that $n=p^{2}$.

By noting that $n \mid p \times(2 p)$, show that $(n-1)!\equiv 0[n]$.

3. Let $p$ be a prime number. By grouping the terms 2 by 2, show that $(p-1)!\equiv-1[p]$.
4. Conclude.

In space, there are $n$ points $(n \geq 3)$ arranged such that among any three of these points, there are always at least two that are less than 1 unit apart.

Prove that under this condition, there must always be two spheres $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$ with a radius of 1, such that each of the $n$ points belongs to (at least) one of the two spheres $K_{1}, K_{2}$. Note: Each sphere is understood here without its boundary (the spherical surface).

The Rhinoceros has vertical and horizontal folds on its skin. In total, there are 17 folds. If the Rhinoceros scratches itself against a tree, either two horizontal or two vertical folds on that side disappear, and on the other side, one horizontal and one vertical fold are added. (If there are not two folds of the same direction, nothing happens.) The Rhinoceros scratched itself several times. Could it be that on each side, the number of vertical folds became what was previously the number of horizontal folds, and the number of horizontal folds became what was previously the number of vertical folds?

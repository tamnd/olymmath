The set $S\subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is given with the properties: $(a) \mathbb{Z}\subset S$ , $(b) (\sqrt 2 +\sqrt 3)\in S$ , $(c)$ If $x,y\in S$ then $x+y\in S$ , and $(d)$ If $x,y\in S$ then $x\cdot y\in S$ .
Prove that $(\sqrt 2+\sqrt 3)^{-1}\in S$ .

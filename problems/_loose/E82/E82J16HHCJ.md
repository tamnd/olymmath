It is known that the divisors of each "non-square" number can be divided into pairs so that the products of the divisors in each pair are equal. For example, $18=1 \cdot 18=2 \cdot 9=3 \cdot 6$. But do there exist "non-square" numbers, all divisors of which can be divided into triples so that the products of the divisors in each triple are equal?

Fair Division. According to the will of a deceased father, three sons were to divide 7 horses among themselves so that the eldest would receive half, the middle one a quarter, and the youngest an eighth. The will greatly puzzled the heirs - for its implementation would have required cutting the horses into pieces. An old neighbor suggested a way out. He added his own horse to the horses. Then everything went smoothly: the first son received 4 horses, which was half of all the horses, the second received 2, which was a quarter, and the third received 1, which was an eighth. After that, the neighbor took his horse back. Explain how the neighbor reasoned when dividing the horses.

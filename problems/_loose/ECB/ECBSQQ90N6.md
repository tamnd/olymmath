Let $\omega$ be a circle and $P$ a point outside the circle. We take two lines $(d)$ and $\left(d^{\prime}\right)$ passing through $P$ which each intersect the circle twice. We name these intersections $A, A^{\prime}$ and $B, B^{\prime}$. Let $X$ be the intersection of the lines $\left(A B^{\prime}\right)$ and $\left(A^{\prime} B\right)$. Show that $X A \cdot X B^{\prime}=X B \cdot X A^{\prime}$

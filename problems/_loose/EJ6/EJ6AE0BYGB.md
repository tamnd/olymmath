In the modern world, every consumer often has to make decisions about replacing old equipment with more energy-efficient alternatives. Consider a city dweller who uses a 60 W incandescent lamp for 100 hours each month. The electricity tariff is 5 rubles/kWh.

The city dweller can buy a more energy-efficient lamp with a power of 12 W, which costs 120 rubles and provides the same light output as the aforementioned incandescent lamp. Alternatively, he can approach an investor (an energy service company) that will install such an energy-saving lamp, but in return, the city dweller must pay the company 75% of the resulting savings in electricity costs over a period of 10 months (the tariff payment is made regardless of the method of lamp installation).

(a) If the city dweller plans his expenses only 10 months ahead, will he install the energy-saving lamp himself or approach the investor company?

(b) If the city dweller plans his expenses for the entire service life of a typical energy-saving lamp, what decision will he make?

(c) Suppose that at the moment the city dweller is making a decision, a friend approaches him and asks for a loan of 120 rubles for a year. If he lends the money to his friend, he will not be able to pay for the lamp himself. What should the interest rate on the loan be for the city dweller to agree to lend the money?

(d) What economic factors influencing the decision-making process did we not consider in parts (a)-(c) of the problem?

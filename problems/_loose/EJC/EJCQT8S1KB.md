Prove the "Hauber's Theorem": "Suppose several theorems have been proved, the conditions of which are $A_{1}, A_{2}, \ldots, A_{n}$, and the conclusions - respectively $B_{1}, B_{2}, \ldots, B_{n}$. If the conditions $A_{1}, A_{2}, \ldots, A_{n}$ exhaust all possible cases, and each of the conclusions is incompatible with any of the others, then all the converse theorems are valid: from $B_{1}$ follows $A_{1}$, from $B_{2}$ follows $A_{2}$, from $B_{3}$ follows $A_{3}$, and so on."

Exercise on the multiplication table. We build the following sequence of digits.

Let the first digit be 2, the next 3,

$$
2 \cdot 3=6,
$$

the third digit in the sequence will be 6;

$$
3 \cdot 6=18
$$

the fourth digit will be 1, and the fifth 8;

$$
6 \cdot 1=6, 1 \cdot 8=8
$$

the sixth digit is 6, followed by the digit 8, and so on.

Here is the sequence of digits we get:

$$
2 \_3 \_6 \_1 \_8 \_6 \_8 \ldots
$$

The arcs below the digits indicate the multiplications we have already performed, the results of which we have written as the next digits in the sequence; for example, now we should multiply 8 by 6 and write the digits of the result: 4, 8. We will never run out of digits to multiply, as with each multiplication the arcs move one step, and the result is at least a single digit, and often a double digit, and therefore at least one digit is added.

Prove that the digits $5, 7, 9$ will never appear in this sequence.

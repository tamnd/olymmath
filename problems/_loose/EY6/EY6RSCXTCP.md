Tying Boxes. In confectionery shops, to tie boxes of candies, they proceed as follows: the ribbon runs diagonally and forms one closed diagonal (non-planar) octagon; on the lid, two parallel segments of the ribbon are visible, and at the bottom, the ribbon runs in a similar manner. Knowing all the measurements of the box, one can calculate the length of the ribbon, as well as the angles at which it intersects the edges of the box. Finally, it can be proven that the ribbon can be shifted not only along itself but also along the box.

66a. Another Way of Tying. A box in the shape of a rectangular parallelepiped is usually tied in a crisscross pattern: the strings intersect at right angles at the center of the lid $N$ and at the center of the base $P$.

Prove that firmly gluing the strings at $N$ and $P$ makes any movement of them impossible.

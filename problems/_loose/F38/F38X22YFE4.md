Let $G$ be a graph with a tangle $\mathcal{T}$ of order $k$.
(i) Prove: For each $i < k$, the graph $G$ also has a tangle of order $i$. (Thus, it is harder for a graph to have a tangle of a large order than a small one.)
(ii) Show the reasonableness of defining the "small side" by proving that if $(A, B) \in \mathcal{T}$, and $\left\{A^{\prime}, B^{\prime}\right\}$ is a separation of order $<k$ satisfying $A^{\prime} \subseteq A$ or $B^{\prime} \supseteq B$, then $\left(A^{\prime}, B^{\prime}\right) \in \mathcal{T}$.
(iii) Prove: As long as $\left\{A \cup A^{\prime}, B \cap B^{\prime}\right\}$ is a separation of order $<k$, if $(A, B),\left(A^{\prime}, B^{\prime}\right) \in \mathcal{T}$, then $\left(A \cup A^{\prime}, B \cap B^{\prime}\right) \in \mathcal{T}$.
(iv) Derive: For every set $X$ with fewer than $k$ vertices, $G-X$ has exactly one component $C$ that is "large", where "large" means $(V(G-C), X \cup V(C)) \in \mathcal{T}$.

In an $n \times 6$ grid table, each cell is filled with the integer 1 or $0(n \geqslant$ 2). It is known that the integers in each row are not all the same, and for any two rows of numbers $\left(a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{6}\right)$ and $\left(b_{1}, b_{2}, \cdots, b_{6}\right)$, there must be a row in the table that is $\left(a_{1} b_{1}, a_{2} b_{2}, \cdots, a_{6} b_{6}\right)$. Prove that there must be a column of integers in the table where the number of 0s is no less than half.

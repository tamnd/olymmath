A cuboid has a volume of $V_{1}=0.216 \mathrm{dm}^{3}$, and the edges of its base are $12 \mathrm{~cm}$ and 60 $\mathrm{mm}$ long, respectively.

For a second cuboid, it is known that it has the same height as the first cuboid, and that the longer edge of its base is $2 \mathrm{~cm}$ longer than the longer edge of the base of the first cuboid, and the shorter edge is $10 \mathrm{~mm}$ shorter than the shorter edge of the first cuboid. Calculate the difference in the surface areas of the two cuboids and give it in square centimeters!

For the first cuboid:

Its longer base edge is $a_{1}=12 \mathrm{~cm}$, its shorter base edge is $b_{1}=6 \mathrm{~cm}$. Its volume is $V_{1}=216 \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$; since $216:(12 \cdot 6)=3$, its height is therefore $h_{1}=3 \mathrm{~cm}$.

For the second cuboid:

Its longer base edge is $a_{2}=14 \mathrm{~cm}$, its shorter base edge is $b_{2}=5 \mathrm{~cm}$, and its height is $h_{2}=h_{1}=3 \mathrm{~cm}$.

The surface area of the first cuboid is therefore

$$
A_{1}=2 \cdot\left(12 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}+12 \cdot 3 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}+3 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}\right)=252 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}
$$

The surface area of the second cuboid is

$$
A_{2}=2 \cdot\left(14 \cdot 5 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}+14 \cdot 3 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}+5 \cdot 3 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}\right)=254 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}
$$

The difference in the surface areas of the two cuboids is therefore $2 \mathrm{~cm}^{2}$.

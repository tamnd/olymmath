Aline and Théo are playing the following game. First, Théo chooses prime numbers $p_{1}, p_{2}, \ldots$ Then Aline chooses distinct non-zero natural numbers $n_{1}, n_{2}, \ldots$ Next, Théo chooses two integers $k \geqslant 1$ and $\ell \geqslant 1$. He wins the game if there are infinitely many integers $n_{i}$ that are not divisible by $p_{k}^{\ell}$. Otherwise, Aline wins.

a) Who, Aline or Théo, has a winning strategy?

b) To make the game even more challenging, Aline now has an additional constraint: each integer $n_{i}$ can only divide a finite number of integers $n_{j}$. With these new rules, who, Aline or Théo, has a winning strategy?

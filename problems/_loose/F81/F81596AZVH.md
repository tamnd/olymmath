(a) In the Bochner-Hinchin theorem, it is assumed that the non-negative definite function $\varphi(t)$ is continuous. Prove that the continuity of $\varphi(t)$ at zero already ensures continuity on the entire real line. Derive this by establishing the inequality

$$
|\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)|^{2} \leqslant 2[1-\operatorname{Re} \varphi(t-s)],
$$

which holds when $\varphi(0)=1$.

(b) Also prove the following result of Riesz, which shows to what extent one can generally dispense with the continuity assumption.

Let $\varphi=\varphi(t)$ be a complex-valued Lebesgue measurable function, and let $\varphi(0)=1$. Then the function $\varphi=\varphi(t)$ is positive definite if and only if it coincides (almost everywhere with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the real line) with some characteristic function.

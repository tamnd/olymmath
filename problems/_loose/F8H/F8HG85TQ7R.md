For an arbitrary tetrahedron, the center of its circumscribed sphere is $O$, and the reflection of $O$ with respect to the centroid $S$ is $T$. It needs to be proven that the center of the sphere determined by the centroids of the faces is the point that divides the distance $OT$ in the ratio 1:2, closer to $T$, and its radius is one-third of the radius of the circumscribed sphere. The sphere passes through the points that divide the segments connecting $T$ to the vertices of the tetrahedron in the ratio 1:2, closer to $T$, and through the orthogonal projections of these points onto the opposite faces. (Generalization of the first Feuerbach sphere.)

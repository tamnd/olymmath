The fifteenth question: Given that each side and diagonal of a regular polygon is colored with one of $M\left(M \geq 3, M \in \mathrm{Z}^{+}\right)$ colors, and not all sides and diagonals are the same color. If there are no two-colored triangles (i.e., triangles whose three sides are exactly two colors), then the coloring of the polygon is called "wonderful." It is known that $\mathrm{N}$ is the largest positive integer such that there exists a wonderful coloring of a regular $\mathrm{N}$-sided polygon. Prove:
(1) $\mathrm{N} \leq (\mathrm{M}-1)^{2}$;
(2) If $M-1$ is a prime number, then there exists a wonderful coloring of a regular $(M-1)^{2}$-sided polygon.

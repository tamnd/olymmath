Through the vertex $B$ of triangle $ABC$, a line $\ell$ is drawn parallel to side $AC$. The bisector of angle $A$ intersects $\ell$ at point $A'$, and the bisector of angle $C$ intersects $\ell$ at point $C'$. It is known that the area of triangle $ABA'$ is $50\%$ more, and the area of triangle $CBC'$ is $25\%$ more than the area of triangle $ABC$. The bisectors intersect at point $O$. What fraction of the area of triangle $AOC$ is of the area of triangle $ABC?$

Let $P$ and $Q$ be two points lying on a line that also passes through a pair of points $A$ and $B$. One point is inside, and the other is outside the segment $AB$. It is said that points $P$ and $Q$ harmonically divide the segment $AB$ (or that the segment $PQ$ harmonically divides the segment $AB$), if $\frac{PA}{PB}=\frac{QA}{QB}$. By swapping the middle terms of the proportion, we get that then $AB$ also harmonically divides the segment $PQ$.

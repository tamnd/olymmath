(Jury, Canada, 82). Prove that the set of all values $n \in \mathbf{N}$ for which the equation

$$
\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}=\frac{3}{n}
$$

has no solutions in natural numbers cannot be represented as a union of a finite number of arithmetic progressions (both finite and infinite).

2.29*. (NRB, 79). Prove that the equation $x^{2}+5=y^{3}$ has no solutions in integers.

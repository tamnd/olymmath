When I was a child, this puzzle was incredibly popular. Now it is less well-known. This puzzle has one remarkable feature: most people give the wrong answer to the question and stubbornly defend their solution despite all arguments. I remember that about 50 years ago in one company, a multi-hour debate arose over this puzzle, but those who solved it correctly were unable to convince the others of the correctness of their solution. Here is the puzzle.

A person is looking at a portrait. "Whose portrait are you looking at?" they are asked, and the person replies: "I grew up alone, as a single person, alone. And yet, the father of the one in the portrait is the son of my father (you didn't mishear, everything is correct - the son!)."

Whose portrait is the person looking at?

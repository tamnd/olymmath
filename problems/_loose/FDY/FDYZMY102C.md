Let us denote by $\{x\}$ the fractional part of the number $x$, i.e., $\{x\}=x-[x]$, where $[x]$ denotes the integer part of $x$, i.e., the greatest integer less than $x$. For example,

$$
\left\{\frac{1}{3}\right\}=\frac{1}{3}, \{3\}=0, \left\{\frac{7}{3}\right\}=\left\{2 \frac{1}{3}\right\}=\frac{1}{3}, \text{ etc. }
$$

Prove that the equation

$$
\{x\}+\left\{\frac{1}{x}\right\}=1
$$

has no solution in the set of rational numbers.

Given three quadratic trinomials $P(x), Q(x)$, and $R(x)$ with positive leading coefficients, each having two distinct roots. It turns out that substituting the roots of the trinomial $R(x)$ into the polynomial $P(x)+Q(x)$ yields equal values. Similarly, substituting the roots of the trinomial $P(x)$ into the polynomial $Q(x)+R(x)$ yields equal values, and substituting the roots of the trinomial $Q(x)$ into the polynomial $P(x)+R(x)$ also yields equal values. Prove that the three numbers: the sum of the roots of the trinomial $P(x)$, the sum of the roots of the trinomial $Q(x)$, and the sum of the roots of the trinomial $R(x)$ are equal to each other.

(N. Agakhanov)

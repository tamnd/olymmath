Statistics. A certain statistician decided to investigate how non-smoking compartments are used on railways in different countries. He identified the following possibilities:
a) smokers most often travel in smoking compartments,

$a^{\prime}$ ) not $a$ ("not $a$ " denotes the negation of proposition $a$ ),

b) non-smokers most often travel in smoking compartments, $b^{\prime}$ ) not $b$,

c) in smoking compartments, most travelers are smokers, $c^{\prime}$ ) not $c$,

d) in non-smoking compartments, most travelers are smokers, $d^{\prime}$ ) not $d$.

Each country can be characterized using the four letters $a, b, c, d$ with or without primes; of course, no letter can appear in the symbol both with and without a prime, as each prime denotes a proposition that contradicts the proposition without the prime. Therefore, there are 16 possible symbols.

Can travelers be accommodated in 16 trains so that each train corresponds to a different symbol?

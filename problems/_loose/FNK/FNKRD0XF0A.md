Let $n$ be a natural number and $S$ the set of all points $(x, y)$, where $x, y \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$. Let $\mathscr{T}$ be the set of all squares whose vertices belong to the set $S$. Denote by $a_{k} (k \geqslant 0)$ the number of pairs of points from $S$ that are vertices of exactly $k$ squares in $\mathscr{T}$. Prove that $a_{0}=a_{2}+2 a_{3}$.

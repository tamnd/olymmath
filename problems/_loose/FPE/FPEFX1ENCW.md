(CRR, 78). The set $X$ is partitioned into pairwise disjoint subsets $A_{1}, \ldots, A_{n}$, and also partitioned into pairwise disjoint subsets $\boldsymbol{B}_{i}, \ldots, B_{n}$. It is known that the union $A_{i} \cup B_{j}$ of any two disjoint subsets $A_{i}, B_{j}(1 \leqslant i \leqslant n, 1 \leqslant$ $\leqslant j \leqslant n)$ contains at least $n$ elements. Prove that the number of elements in the set $X$ is not less than $n^{2} / 2$. Can it be equal to $n^{2} / 2$?

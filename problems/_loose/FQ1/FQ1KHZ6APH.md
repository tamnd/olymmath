The number 76 has an interesting property: the last two digits of the number $76^{2}=5776$ are 76 again.

a) Are there any other two-digit numbers with this property?

b) Find all such three-digit numbers $A$ such that the last three digits of the number $A^{2}$ form the number $A$.

c) Does there exist an infinite sequence of digits $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}, \ldots$, such that for any natural number $n$, the square of the number $\overline{a_{n}} \overline{a_{n-1}} \bar{\ldots} \bar{\ldots} \overline{\overline{2}} \overline{a_{1}^{-}}$ ends with these same $n$ digits? The obvious answer $a_{1}=1$ and $0=a_{2}=a_{3}=\ldots$ is excluded.

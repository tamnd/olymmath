Points in the plane with both coordinates being rational numbers are called rational points. Prove that all rational points in the plane can be divided into three pairwise disjoint sets, satisfying the conditions:
(1) In any circle centered at each rational point, there must be points from each of the three sets;
(2) On any straight line, it is impossible to have three points each belonging to one of the three sets.
Use the notation $(x, y),(x, y, z)$ to represent coordinates or vectors, $(x, y, z)_{D}$ to represent the greatest common divisor of the integers, and $Q^{2}$ to represent the set of all rational points in the plane.

There are $n$ contestants $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{n}$ participating in a mathematics competition, some of whom are acquainted with each other, and any two contestants who are not acquainted have exactly two common acquaintances. If it is known that contestants $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ are acquainted, but they do not have any common acquaintances, prove that they have the same number of acquaintances.

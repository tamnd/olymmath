On an eleven-kilometer circular track, many cars are driving at a constant speed of 120 km/h. At one unfortunate spot on the road, there is an electronic police officer. At any moment, his baton can be in one of two positions: raised or lowered. If a car passes the electronic police officer with the baton lowered, it instantly reduces its speed to 60 km/h, and after a minute, it instantly accelerates back. If the baton is raised, the car instantly stops, and after a minute, it instantly accelerates from a stop with its original speed. The electronic police officer is controlled by a programmer who cannot see the positions of the cars on the road. Prove that the programmer can write such a sequence of commands for the police officer (how many minutes to stand with the baton raised, then - how many with the baton lowered, and so on), so that immediately after the last command, all cars on the track stop.

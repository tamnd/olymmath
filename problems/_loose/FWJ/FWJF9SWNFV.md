At the beginning of the 19th century, the Italian geometer Malfatti posed the following problem: to cut three circles from a given triangle so that the sum of their areas is the largest. In later research, the Malfatti circles came to be understood as three circles that are pairwise tangent to each other, each of which also touches two sides of the given triangle. Prove that for an equilateral triangle, the Malfatti circles do not provide a solution to the original problem. (It was only in the mid-20th century that it was established that the Malfatti circles do not provide a solution to the original problem for any triangle.)

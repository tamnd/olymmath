An ant crawls on the area bounded by the curve described by the equation $x^{2}+y^{2}+x y=6$. Its path consists of line segments parallel to the coordinate axes. The ant starts from an arbitrary point on the curve and moves straight into the interior of the curve until it reaches the boundary again. Then it turns $90^{\circ}$ and continues its path inside the curve. The ant stops only when it reaches a boundary point it has already visited, or if it cannot continue its path according to the above rule. Prove that, regardless of the starting point, the ant will eventually stop.

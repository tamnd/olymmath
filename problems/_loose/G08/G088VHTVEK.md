For example, $9\left\{a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{n}\right\}$ and $\left\{b_{1}, b_{2}, \cdots, b_{n}\right\}$ are two different $n$-element subsets of $Q$, and it is known that $\left\{a_{i}+a_{j} \mid 1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant n\right\}=\left\{b_{i}+b_{j} \mid 1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant n\right\}$, and for any $r \in\left\{a_{i}+a_{j} \mid 1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant n\right\}$, the number of $(i, j)$ pairs such that $a_{i}+a_{j}=r, 1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant n$ is equal to the number of $(i, j)$ pairs such that $b_{i}+b_{j}=r, 1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant n$. Prove: $n$ is a power of 2.

Pinching a convex $n$-corner means the following: You select two neighboring sides $A B$ and $B C$ and replace them by the line $A M, M N, N C$, where $M \in A B$ and $N \in B C$ are arbitrary points inside these lines. In other words, you cut off a corner and get an ( $n+1$ )-corner.

Starting from a regular hexagon $\mathcal{P}_{6}$ with area 1, a sequence $\mathcal{P}_{6}, \mathcal{P}_{7}, \mathcal{P}_{8}, \ldots$ of convex polygons is generated by continuous pinching. Show that the area of $\mathcal{P}_{n}$ is greater than $\frac{1}{2}$ for all $n \geq 6$, regardless of how it is squared.

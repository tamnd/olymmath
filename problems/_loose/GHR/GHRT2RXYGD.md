In the plane, a convex polygon $ Q $ and a line $ l $ are given.
(a) Prove that there exists exactly one polygon $ R $ symmetric with respect to $ l $ such that for every line $ s $ perpendicular to $ l $, the intersections $ s \cap R $ and $ s \cap Q $ are segments of equal length, or single-point sets, or empty sets.
(b) Prove that the polygon $ R $ is convex, has the same area as $ Q $, and the diameter of $ R $ is not greater than the diameter of $ Q $.
Note: The diameter of a set $ X $ is the supremum of the distances between any two points in the set $ X $.

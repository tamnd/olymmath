Vера enters her knowledge of planimetry into tables, the rows of which correspond to figures, and the columns - to properties. If a figure has the required property, then a 1 is written at the intersection of the row and column, and otherwise - 0. In one of the $4 \times 4$ tables, it turned out that each row and each column has exactly one zero. It is known that the first column corresponds to the property "has an acute angle," and the second - to the property "has equal sides." Select two more properties, and for the rows - two triangles and two quadrilaterals, to achieve the required arrangement of zeros and ones.

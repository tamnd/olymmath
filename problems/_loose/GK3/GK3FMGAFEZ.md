Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a collection of subsets of a countable set $A$, such that for distinct $A^{\prime}, A^{\prime \prime} \in \mathcal{A}$ and some fixed $k \in \mathbb{N}$, we have $\left|A^{\prime} \cap A^{\prime \prime}\right| \leqslant k$. Consider a fixed $k$-set $S$, how many sets in $\mathcal{A}$ contain $S$?

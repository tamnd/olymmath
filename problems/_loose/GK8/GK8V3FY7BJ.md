Given a real number $c>0$, the function $f(x)=\sqrt{x+c}$, its graph $C_{1}: y=f(x)$ intersects with the line $l: y=x$ at point $P$. On $C_{1}$, take a point $P_{1}\left(x_{1}, y_{1}\right)$, draw $P_{1} Q_{1}$ parallel to the $x$-axis to intersect line $l$ at point $Q_{1}$, and draw a line $Q_{1} P_{2}$ parallel to the $y$-axis to intersect $C_{1}$ at point $P_{2}\left(x_{2}, y_{2}\right)$; continue this process, and we get points $P_{3}\left(x, y_{3}\right)$, $P_{4}\left(x_{4}, y_{4}\right)$, $\cdots$, $P_{n}\left(x_{n}, y_{n}\right)$, $\cdots$. Suppose the coordinates of point $P$ are $(a, a)$, $x_{1}=b$, $0<b<a$. Prove: For all positive integers $n$, $x_{n}<a$.

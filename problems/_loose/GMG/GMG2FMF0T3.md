It is quite simple to form a rectangle with adjacent squares of repeated sizes, see for example the problem of square numbers. A much more complicated activity is to form a rectangle, also with adjacent squares, all having distinct sizes. The first publication of a rectangle formed by squares with all distinct sizes was made in 1925 by Z. Morón. He formed a $47 \times 65$ rectangle with ten squares of sides: 3, 5, 6, 11, 17, 19, 22, 23, 24, and 25. Zeca Mourão cut out paper squares in the shape of the squares used by Z. Morón and decided to assemble the $47 \times 65$ rectangle. After some time, Zeca finally managed to do it. Let's try to figure out how his assembly turned out?

(a) Knowing that the perimeter of the rectangle was made by only six squares, which squares were used on the border?

(b) Place these squares in an appropriate manner.

(c) Complete Zeca Mourão's assembly.

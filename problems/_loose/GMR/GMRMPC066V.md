A square $ABCD$ is divided into $n^2$ equal smaller squares ($n$ is a positive integer). The opposite vertices $A, C$ are painted red, and $B, D$ are painted blue. The other intersection points are arbitrarily painted in one of the two colors, red or blue. Prove: the number of smaller squares that have exactly two vertices of the same color must be even.

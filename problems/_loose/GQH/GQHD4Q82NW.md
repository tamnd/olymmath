If the product of two natural numbers, when divided by 9, leaves a remainder of 1, we say that these two natural numbers are "mod 9 reciprocals." For example, $2 \times 5=10$, when divided by 9 leaves a remainder of 1, so 2 and 5 are "mod 9 reciprocals"; $1 \times 1=1$, so the "mod 9 reciprocal" of 1 is itself. Clearly, if a natural number has a "mod 9 reciprocal," then its reciprocal is not unique; for example, 10 is another "mod 9 reciprocal" of 1. Determine whether 1, 2, $3,4,5,6,7,8$ have "mod 9 reciprocals," and write down the numbers that have "mod 9 reciprocals" and their corresponding smallest "mod 9 reciprocals" respectively. $ $

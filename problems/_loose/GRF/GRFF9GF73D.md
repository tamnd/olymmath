Let integer $n \geq 3$. In a $3 \times 3$ grid, if the center cell is white and the rest are black, or the center cell is black and the rest are white, then this grid is called a "good square". Using black and white to color an infinitely large grid, such that there are some $\mathrm{a} \times \mathrm{b}$ rectangles containing at least $\mathrm{n}^{2}-\mathrm{n}$ good squares, find the minimum value of $\mathrm{a}+$ $b$, where $a$ and $b$ are positive integers, and the boundaries of the $\mathrm{a} \times \mathrm{b}$ rectangle do not necessarily align with the grid lines.

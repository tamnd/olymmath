$ABC$ is an acute - angled triangle with $\odot(ABC)$ and $\Omega$ as the circumcircle and incircle respectively. Let $D, E, F$ to be the respective intouch points on $\overline{BC}, \overline{CA}$ and $\overline{AB}$. Circle $\gamma_A$ is drawn internally tangent to sides $\overline{AC}, \overline{AB}$ and $\odot(ABC)$ at $X, Y$ and $Z$ respectively. Another circle $(\omega)$ is constructed tangent to $\overline{BC}$ at $\mathcal{T}_1$ and internally tangent to $\odot(ABC)$ at $\mathcal{T}_2$. A tangent is drawn from $A$ such that it touches $\omega$ at $W$ and meets $BC$ at $V$, with $V$ lying inside $\odot(ABC)$. Now if $\overline{EF}$ meets $\odot(BC)$ at $\mathcal{X}_1$ and $\mathcal{X}_2$, opposite to vertex $B$ and $C$ respectively, where $\odot(BC)$ denotes the circle with $BC$ as diameter, prove that the set of lines $\{\overline{B\mathcal{X}_1}, \overline{ZS}, \overline{C\mathcal{X}_2}, \overline{DU}, \overline{YX}, \overline{\mathcal{T}_1W} \}$ are concurrent where $S$ is the mid-point of $\widehat{BC}$ containing $A$ and $U$ is the anti-pode of $D$ with respect to $\Omega$. If the line joining that concurrency point and $A$ meets $\odot(ABC)$ at $N\not = A$ prove that $\overline{AD}, \overline{ZN}$ and $\gamma_A$ pass through a common point.

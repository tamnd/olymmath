Let $\left(d_{1}\right)$ and $\left(d_{2}\right)$ be two lines that intersect at a point $X$. A circle $\mathcal{C}$ passing through $X$ intersects $\left(d_{1}\right)$ at $A_{1}$ and $\left(d_{2}\right)$ at $A_{2}$. Another circle $\mathcal{C}^{\prime}$, passing through $X$ but not tangent to $\mathcal{C}$, intersects $\left(d_{1}\right)$ at $B_{1}$ and $\left(d_{2}\right)$ at $B_{2}$. The circles $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{C}^{\prime}$ intersect at $Y$. We denote $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ as the midpoints of $\left[A_{1} B_{1}\right]$ and $\left[A_{2} B_{2}\right]$.

Show that $X, Y, M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$ are concyclic.

Given a triangle $ABC$ and a point $M$ on its circumcircle, the projections of $M$ onto the sides $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$ are $A_1$, $B_1$, and $C_1$, respectively. The perpendiculars from $M$ to the lines $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$ intersect the circle again at points $A'$, $B'$, and $C'$, respectively. It is known that the points $A_1$, $B_1$, and $C_1$ lie on a straight line, which is called the Simson line of point $M$ with respect to triangle $ABC$. It is to be proven that the Simson lines of points $A'$, $B'$, and $C'$ with respect to triangle $ABC$ form a triangle that is symmetrically similar to triangle $ABC$.

Unfortunately, the story I want to tell you is not true. But since it is interesting, I still want to tell it to you.

In the ocean (which one exactly - I don't remember) there are three islands located not far from each other: A, B, and C. I managed to find out that at least one of them has buried treasures, but which one exactly remained unknown. Islands B and C were uninhabited, and the population of Island A consisted of knights and liars. It is not impossible that there were also normal people among the locals, but I cannot say with certainty whether there was at least one normal person on the island.

I was fortunate enough to obtain a map of the islands, drawn by the famous pirate Captain Marston, known for his eccentricities (he was the one who buried the treasures). The map was accompanied by a note, of course encrypted. When I deciphered it, I found out that it consisted of only two sentences. Here is what it said:

(1) There are no treasures on Island A.

(2) If there are normal people among the inhabitants of Island A, then the treasures are buried on two islands.

I hurried to Island A. I knew for sure that the inhabitants of this island knew everything about the buried treasures down to the smallest details. The king of the island guessed why I had come to his lands and, in no uncertain terms, allowed me to ask only one question to any of his randomly chosen subjects. There was no way for me to determine whether my choice fell on a knight, a liar, or a normal person.

I needed to come up with a question so that, upon receiving the answer, I could point to one of the islands and be sure that the treasure was buried there.

What question should I have asked the islander?

(To the Borel-Cantelli lemma.) (a) Let $\Omega=(0,1), \mathscr{B}=$ $\mathscr{B}((0,1)), \mathrm{P}-$ be the Lebesgue measure. Consider the events $A_{n}=(0,1 / n)$. Show that $\sum \mathrm{P}\left(A_{n}\right)=\infty$, but each $\omega$ from $(0,1)$ can belong to only a finite number of sets $A_{1}, \ldots, A_{[1 / \omega]}$, i.e., $\mathrm{P}\left(A_{n}\right.$ i.o. $)=0$.

(b) If $\sum \mathrm{P}\left(\left|\xi_{n}\right|>\varepsilon\right)0$, then $\xi_{n} \rightarrow 0$ a.s. Provide an example of random variables $\left(\xi_{n}\right)_{n \geqslant 1}$ such that $\xi_{n} \rightarrow 0$ a.s., but $\sum \mathrm{P}\left(\left|\xi_{n}\right|>\varepsilon\right)=\infty$ for every $\varepsilon>0$.

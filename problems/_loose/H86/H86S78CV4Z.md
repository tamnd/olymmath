In space, consider a sphere. If point $P$ lies on the sphere and point $Q$ does not, let $P_{Q}$ denote the other intersection point of the line $P Q$ with the sphere. If the line $P Q$ is tangent to the sphere, then let $P_{Q}=P$.

Prove that if the points $A, B, C, D$ on the sphere are not coplanar, then there are at most two points $Q$ such that the tetrahedron $A_{Q} B_{Q} C_{Q} D_{Q}$ is equilateral. (A tetrahedron is called equilateral if its faces are congruent.)

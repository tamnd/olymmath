For the tetrahedron $A_{1} A_{2} A_{3} A_{4}$, the sum of the squares of the products of the three pairs of opposite edges is not less than four times the sum of the squares of the areas of the four faces. That is, if we let $A_{1} A_{2}=a, A_{3} A_{4}=a^{\prime}, A_{1} A_{3}=b, A_{2} A_{4}=b^{\prime}, A_{1} A_{4}=c, A_{2} A_{3}=c^{\prime}$, and the area of the face opposite vertex $A_{i}$ is $S_{i}(i=1,2,3,4)$, then
$a^{2} a^{\prime 2}+b^{2} b^{\prime 2}+c^{2} c^{\prime 2} \geqslant 4\left(S_{1}^{2}+S_{2}^{2}+S_{3}^{2}+S_{4}^{2}\right)$, where equality holds if and only if the sums of the squares of each pair of opposite edges are equal.

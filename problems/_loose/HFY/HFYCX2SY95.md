Let $A, B, C, D$ be any four points in space; the 12 angles formed by these points $(\angle B A C, \angle A B C, \angle A C B, \ldots, \angle B D C)$ we denote by $\mathrm{A}_{1}, \mathrm{~A}_{2}, \ldots, \mathrm{A}_{12}$. Prove that

a) the smallest of the angles $A_{1}, A_{2}, \ldots, A_{12}$ is always no more than $60^{\circ}$ (but can be equal to $60^{\circ}$);

b) the largest of the angles $A_{1}, A_{2}, \ldots, A_{12}$ is no less than $60^{\circ}$ (but can be equal to $60^{\circ}$).

We also note that "problems about angle estimates" can be posed in a slightly different way. So far, we have always considered the number $n$ of points in the plane or space as given and, under these conditions, tried to estimate certain angles $\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}, \ldots, \alpha_{N}$ (or $\mathrm{A}_{1}, \mathrm{~A}_{2}, \ldots, \mathrm{A}_{N}$). However, such a problem statement can be "reversed" - we can set certain estimates for (one or several) angles $\alpha_{1}, \alpha_{2}, \ldots, \alpha_{N}$ (respectively $\mathrm{A}_{1}, \mathrm{~A}_{2}, \ldots, \mathrm{A}_{N}$) in advance and ask for what number $n$ of points in the plane or space these estimates can be satisfied. Here we will limit ourselves to just two examples of such a problem statement, both apparently belonging to Paul Erdős.

In $n$-athlon competitions, $2^{n}$ people participate. For each athlete, their strength in each of the events is known. The competitions proceed as follows: first, all athletes participate in the first event, and the best half of them advance to the next round. This half participates in the next event, and half of them advance to the next round, and so on, until the winner is determined in the $n$-th event. We will call an athlete a "possible winner" if it is possible to arrange the events in such a way that he or she becomes the winner.

a) prove that it can happen that at least half of the athletes are "possible winners";

b) prove that the number of "possible winners" never exceeds $2^{n}-n$;

c)* prove that it can happen that there are exactly $2^{n}-n$ "possible winners".

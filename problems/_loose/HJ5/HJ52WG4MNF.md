The manager hands the letters to be printed to the secretary one by one, and always places them on top of the pile. The secretary, whenever free, takes the topmost letter to print. One day, there are 9 letters to be printed, and the manager hands them over in the order of the 1st, 2nd, ..., 9th letter. At lunchtime, the secretary tells a colleague that the 8th letter has been printed, but does not reveal any other details about the morning's work. The colleague is curious to know in what order the letters were printed, and how many possible orders there are for printing the letters in the afternoon (including the possibility of no letters left to print).

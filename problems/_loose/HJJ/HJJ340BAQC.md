Another paradox with the wheel. Two cyclists stopped on a railway bridge somewhere in Sussex when a train passed by.

- This train is going from London to Brighton, - said Henderson.
- The greater part of it, - remarked Banks, - and the rest is moving in the direction of London.
- What on earth do you mean by that?
- I mean that if the train is going from London to Brighton, then part of this train is always moving in the opposite direction - from Brighton to London.
- And you seriously claim that when I ride from Croydon to Eastbourne, part of my bicycle is rushing back to Croydon?
- Don't get so worked up, old chap, - said Banks calmly. - I didn't say anything about bicycles. My statement only concerns railway trains.

Henderson decided that it was just a joke and that smoke or steam was being referred to, but his friend noted that a strong wind could also be in the direction of the train's movement. Then Henderson suggested that the thoughts of the passengers were meant, but this could not be verified, and besides, they could hardly be called part of the train! Finally, Henderson gave up.

Could the reader explain this curious paradox?

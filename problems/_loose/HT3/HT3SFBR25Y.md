For each integer $n(n \geq 2)$, let $f(n)$ denote the largest prime factor of $n$. A "singular pair" refers to an unordered pair of two distinct primes $p, q$, such that there is no integer $n \geq 2$ satisfying $f(n) \cdot f(n+1) = pq$. Prove that there are infinitely many singular pairs.

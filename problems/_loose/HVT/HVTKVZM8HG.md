Let $M$ be a convex polygon of area $S$ and $l$ be an arbitrary line. Prove that

a) in $M$ one can inscribe a triangle, one side of which is parallel to $l$ and the area of which is $\geqslant \frac{3}{8} S$;

1) See, for example, the book [22], entirely devoted to solving the following rather specific problem: what is the maximum number of triangular pyramids (tetrahedra) that can be placed in space so that any two pyramids touch each other along part of the face of each (although the author was ultimately unable to solve this problem completely).
b) the polygon $M$ and the line $l$ can be chosen such that in $M$ one cannot inscribe a triangle, one side of which is parallel to $l$ and the area of which is $>\frac{3}{8} S$.

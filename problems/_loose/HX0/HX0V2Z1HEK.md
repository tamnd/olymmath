Consider the equation
$$
x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+t^{2}-n x y z t-n=0 ;
$$

where $n$ is a given positive integer.
(1) Prove that there exist infinitely many positive integers $n$, such that the above equation has positive integer solutions $(x, y, z, t)$.
(2) Take $n=4^{k} \cdot(8 m+7)$, where $k, m$ are non-negative integers, prove that in this case the equation in the problem has no positive integer solutions.

Let $A$ be a corner square on a chessboard, and $B$ be its diagonally adjacent square. Prove that the number of different routes starting from $A$, which a "hopping" rook can choose to visit each square of the board exactly once, is greater than the number of different routes of a "hopping" rook starting from $B$ and visiting each square of the board exactly once. (For one move, a "hopping" rook moves to an adjacent square in a horizontal or vertical direction.)

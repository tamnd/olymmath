For a sequence $\left(a_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$, we define the sequences $\left(x_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ and $\left(y_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}^{*}}$ by the relations $x_{n}=\min \left(a_{n}, a_{n+1}\right)$ and $y_{n}=\max \left(a_{n}, a_{n+1}\right)$, for any $n \in \mathbb{N}^{*}$.

a) Show that if the sequence $\left(a_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}^{*}}$ has a limit, then the sequences $\left(x_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}^{*}}$ and $\left(y_{n}\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}^{*}}$ also have limits;

b) Is the converse true?

Let $X$ and $Y$ be independent random variables. Show that the distribution function of the random variable $X+Y$

(a) is continuous if the variable $Y$ is continuous, i.e., $\mathrm{P}(Y=y)=0$ for all $y \in \mathbb{R}$;

(b) has the density $g(z)=\mathrm{E} f(z-X)$, if $Y$ has the density $f=f(y)$

(c) is singular if $X$ and $Y$ are discrete and singular variables, respectively.

Establish that properties (a) and (b), in general, do not hold if $X$ and $Y$ are not independent.

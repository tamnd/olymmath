Shortcuts. From time to time, various, sometimes quite ingenious methods appear that facilitate mental arithmetic. Here is one such method that will interest those who are not familiar with it.

Can you multiply 993 and 879 in your head? It is curious that if we have two two-digit numbers containing the same number of tens, and the sum of the digits in their units place equals 10, such numbers can always be multiplied mentally in the following way. Suppose we need to multiply 97 by 93. Multiply 7 by 3 and write down the result, then add 1 to 9 and multiply by the other 9, $9 \times 10=90$. So, $97 \times 23=9021$.

This rule turns out to be very useful when squaring numbers ending in 5, for example, $85^{2}=7225$. There is also a simple rule for multiplying two fractions whose whole parts are the same, and whose fractional parts add up to one. For example, $7 \frac{1}{4} \times 7 \frac{3}{4}=56 \frac{3}{16}$. Multiplying the fractional parts, we get $\frac{3}{16}$; adding 1 to 7 and multiplying the result by the other 7, we get $7 \times 8=56$.

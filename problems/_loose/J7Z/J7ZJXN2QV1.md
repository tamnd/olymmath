Lines $A_{2} B_{2}$ and $A_{3} B_{3}$, $A_{3} B_{3}$ and $A_{1} B_{1}$, $A_{1} B_{1}$ and $A_{2} B_{2}$ intersect at points $P_{1}$, $P_{2}$, $P_{3}$ respectively.

a) Prove that the circumcircles of triangles $A_{1} A_{2} P_{3}$, $A_{1} A_{3} P_{2}$, and $A_{2} A_{3} P_{1}$ intersect at one point lying on the circle of similitude of segments $A_{1} B_{1}$, $A_{2} B_{2}$, and $A_{3} B_{3}$.

b) Let $O_{1}$ be the center of the spiral similarity that maps segment $A_{2} B_{2}$ to segment $A_{3} B_{3}$; points $O_{2}$ and $O_{3}$ are defined similarly. Prove that the lines $P_{1} O_{1}$, $P_{2} O_{2}$, and $P_{3} O_{3}$ intersect at one point lying on the circle of similitude of segments $A_{1} B_{1}$, $A_{2} B_{2}$, and $A_{3} B_{3}$.

Points $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ are called corresponding points of similar figures $F_{1}$ and $F_{2}$ if, under the spiral similarity that maps $F_{1}$ to $F_{2}$, point $A_{1}$ is mapped to $A_{2}$. Corresponding lines and segments are defined similarly.

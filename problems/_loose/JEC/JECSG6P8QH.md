Let's call a chess piece that moves by jumping $\mathrm{m}$ squares vertically or horizontally, and then $\mathrm{n}$ squares in the perpendicular direction, a crocodile. Prove that for any $\mathrm{m}$ and $\mathrm{n}$, it is possible to color an infinite checkerboard in 2 colors (each specific $\mathrm{m}$ and $\mathrm{n}$ has its own coloring), such that any two squares connected by one move of the crocodile will always be of different colors.

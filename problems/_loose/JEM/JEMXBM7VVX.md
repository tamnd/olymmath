The mathematician Gypsy. He shamelessly stole. Of course, he denied it all the way. The count could not figure out how the precious birds, which were fed inside his strictly guarded game preserve, were being reached. But he loved a trick: he wanted to outsmart the thief's mind.

- You, Gypsy... said... the two-week-old barn, and the daily ration of feed.

- I will let you go with 12 chickens, if you can bring me three pheasants from my game preserve.

The Gypsy scratched his ear for a while, then his face suddenly cleared up. He spoke:

- Well, I wish you well, just don't let your noble dogs loose, then I'll try.

The count even encouraged him. Soon he warned his people.

The Gypsy set out on the big task. The gatekeeper at the preserve fence shouted at him.

- Back, Gypsy!
- I must go in, as my stomach is gnawing... I'm starving.
- Go on. But without stealing - I know you won't. So I say: if you give me half of the stolen goods, plus half of one more, I'll cut off your ear. You may go.
- I'm going, I'll throw it over, I'm going, said the Gypsy with a wink. He went, went inside.

Among the trees, he met the ranger. This one also greeted him with the same wish... of course for his own good.

The feeder was just encouraging him, saying he would break his neck if he didn't give him the same share of the loot.

The Gypsy poked the air with his finger for a while... that's so much, and so much. Then he tied a bunch of pheasants and set off back.

Each "official authority" received the desired share "half of the stolen goods plus half of one more." The count also received the three pheasants. The punishment he managed to avoid.

The count told the story in the casino, from the three guards' side, with the "same" conditions... Many and for a long time they argued about it. They are still calculating... how many pheasants did the cunning Gypsy actually steal? ...

They still haven't figured it out.

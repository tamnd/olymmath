A rectangular parallelepiped has vertices: $A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H$. We know the following edge lengths: $A E=B F=C G=200 \mathrm{~mm}$, $A B=D C=126 \mathrm{~mm}, A D=159 \mathrm{~mm}$. The body is cut into two parts by the perpendicular bisector plane of the $A E$ edge, resulting in the bodies $A B C D A_{1} B_{1} C_{1} D_{1}=T^{\prime}$ and $E_{1} F_{1} G_{1} H_{1} E F G H=T^{\prime \prime}$, where the points $A_{1}$ and $E_{1}$ are the midpoints of the $A E$ edge, and the other points with 1 subscript have similar meanings. $T^{\prime \prime}$ is cut by the perpendicular bisector plane of the $E H$ edge, resulting in one part $E F F_{1} E_{1} E^{*} F^{*} F_{1}^{*} E_{1}^{*}=T^{\prime \prime \prime}$, where $E^{*}$ is the midpoint of the $E H$ edge. After removing the other part, $T^{\prime \prime \prime}$ is placed next to $T^{\prime}$ such that $E_{1}$ coincides with $A_{1}$, $E$ lies on the segment $A_{1} B_{1}$, and $E_{1}^{*}$ lies on the line $A A_{1}$. Is it true that in this case, the lines $B E, B_{1} E^{*}$, $D F_{1}, D_{1} F_{1}^{*}, C F$ and $C_{1} F^{*}$ intersect the line $A A_{1}$ at the same point?

Gnomonic Magic Squares. A $3 \times 3$ square is called gnomonic-magic if the sums of the numbers forming the $2 \times 2$ squares that remain after removing one of the four "corners" (gnomons) from the original square are equal to each other. Show that in a gnomonic-magic square of the third order, the sums of the numbers on the two diagonals are equal to each other. Does this property hold for higher orders?

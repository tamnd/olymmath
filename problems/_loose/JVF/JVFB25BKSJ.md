There is a circle with $AB$ as its diameter (called the initial circle), $P$ is a point on $AB$, and circles with $AP$ and $BP$ as diameters are denoted as circle $O$ and circle $O'$ (called the original circles). Construct a circle that is tangent to both original circles and the initial circle (called the 1st circle), and then construct another circle that is tangent to the initial circle, the original circle $O$, and the 1st circle (called the 2nd circle), and so on (note that the $(n+1)$-th circle is on the left side of the $n$-th circle). Prove: The distance from the center of the $n$-th circle to the line $AB$ is equal to $n$ times the diameter of the $n$-th circle.

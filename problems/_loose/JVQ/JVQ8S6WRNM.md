Rémi distributes the integers $0,1, \ldots, 13$ into seven disjoint pairs of integers. For each pair, he writes on the board the sum of its two elements. Prove that Rémi can proceed in such a way that the product of the seven numbers written on the board is a perfect square.

A perfect square is an integer that can be written in the form $n^{2}$, where $n$ is an integer.

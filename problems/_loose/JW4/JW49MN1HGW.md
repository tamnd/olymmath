Let $X=\{1,2, \cdots, n\}$. A family of subsets $A=\left\{A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{1}\right\}$ of $X$ is called completely separable if for any $i, j(1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant n)$, there exist $A_{k}, A_{k} \in \mathscr{A}$ such that $i \in A_{k}-A_{k}, j \in A_{h}-A_{i}$. For any family of subsets $\alpha=\left\{A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{i}\right\}$, define $B_{i}=\left\{k \mid i \in A_{h}\right\}$, generating a family of subsets $\mathscr{A}=\left\{B_{1}, B_{2}, \cdots, B_{n}\right\}$ of $\{1,2, \cdots, t\}$. $A^{*}$ is called the dual of $S$. Prove that $\mathscr{A}$ is completely separable if and only if $\mathscr{N}$ is a family of $S$.

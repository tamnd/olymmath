Supposed that $ f(x)$ has $ f'(x)$ and for any real numbers $ x,\ y$, $ \int_y^{x + y} f(t)\ dt = \int_0^x \{f(y)\cos t + f(t)\cos y\}\ dt$ holds.

(1) Express $ f(x + y)$ in terms of $ f(x),\ f(y).$
(2) Find $ f'\left(x + \frac {\pi}{2}\right)$. Note that $ f\left(\frac {\pi}{2}\right) = 2.$
(3) Find $ f(x).$

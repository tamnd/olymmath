Let $X$ be a random variable with density $f=f(x)$ and characteristic function $\varphi=\varphi(t)$. Prove the Poisson summation formula

$$
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} f(n)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \varphi(2 \pi n)
$$

assuming for simplicity that $X \stackrel{d}{=}-X$ and the series

$$
g(x)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} f(x+n)
$$

defines a function differentiable at zero (this series converges in $L^{1}$ on any finite interval).

By choosing $X$ appropriately, establish the following identity:

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}} \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\frac{n^{2} \pi}{t}}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-n^{2} \pi t}, t>0
$$

which for the Jacobi theta function

$$
\theta(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-n^{2} \pi t}
$$

takes the form $\sqrt{t} \theta(t)=\theta\left(t^{-1}\right), t>0$.

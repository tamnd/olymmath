The 89th question: Given a positive integer $\mathrm{n}$, it is known that the $\mathrm{n}$-degree polynomial with rational coefficients $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{x})$ is irreducible over $\mathrm{Q}[\mathrm{x}]$. If a non-constant polynomial $Q(x)$ with rational coefficients and degree less than $\mathrm{n}$ satisfies $P(x) \mid P(Q(x))$, then $Q(x)$ is called a "magical polynomial". Prove: The number of magical polynomials does not exceed $\mathrm{n}$.

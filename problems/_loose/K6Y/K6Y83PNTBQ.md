Two equal non-intersecting circles are given. On two common internal tangents, take two arbitrary points $F$ and $F^{\prime}$. From both points, one more tangent can be drawn to each circle. Let the tangents drawn from points $F$ and $F^{\prime}$ to one circle meet at point $A$, and to the other at point $B$. It is required to prove that: 1) the line $A B$ is parallel to the line connecting the centers of the circles (in the case of unequal circles, it passes through the point of intersection of the external tangents); 2) the line connecting the midpoints of $F F^{\prime}$ and $A B$ passes through the midpoint of the segment connecting the centers of the circles.

(This problem was proposed to the readers of the journal "Bulletin of Experimental Physics and Elementary Mathematics" by Professor V. Ermakov. This journal was published in Russia in the last century. The problem was published in the 14 (2)-th issue of the journal for 1887. A prize of mathematical literature was promised to the readers for solving the problem.)

A line separating two circles means that the two circles and the line have no common points, and the two circles are on opposite sides of the line. Suppose three pairwise disjoint circles satisfy the following property: any line that separates two of the circles intersects the interior of the third circle. Prove: the sum of the distances between the centers of these three circles does not exceed $2 \sqrt{2}$ times the sum of their radii.

Note: If the constant $2 \sqrt{2}$ in the problem is replaced by another constant $c > 2 \sqrt{2}$, leading to a weaker conclusion, points will be awarded based on the value of the constant $c$.

Does there exist a subset $S$ of the integers such that, given any integer $n$, the equation $n=2 s+s^{\prime}$ has exactly one solution in $\mathrm{S}$ ? For example, if $\mathrm{T}=\{-3,0,1,4$ ), then there are unique solutions $-3=2 \cdot 0-3,-1=2 \cdot 1-3,0$ $=2 \cdot 0+0,1=2 \cdot 0+1,2=0+2 \cdot 1,3=2 \cdot 1+1,4=2 \cdot 0+4,5=2 \cdot-3+1$, but not for $6=2 \cdot 1+4=2 \cdot-3+0$, so $\mathrm{T}$ cannot be a subset of $\mathrm{S}$.

Let the intersection point of the diagonals of rectangle $ABCD$ be denoted by $M$. Let $A', B', C'$ be points on segments $MA, MB, MC$ respectively, and let the intersection of $AB$ and $A'B'$ be denoted by $P$, and the intersection of $BC$ and $B'C'$ by $Q$. Let $PQ$ intersect $CD$ at $R$ and $AD$ at $S$. It is to be proven that the intersection of lines $C'R$ and $A'S$ lies on line $MD$, specifically at a point $D$ such that

$$
\frac{1}{MB'} + \frac{1}{MD'} = \frac{1}{MA'} + \frac{1}{MC'}
$$

(See the 3rd problem of the final round of the 1957 National Mathematical Competition in our previous issue on page 11.)

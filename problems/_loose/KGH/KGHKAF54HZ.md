Let the integer $n \geqslant 3$, and let there be $k$ prime numbers not exceeding $n$. Let $A$ be a subset of the set $\{2,3, \cdots, n\}$, with the number of elements in $A$ being less than $k$, and no element in $A$ is a multiple of another. Prove: There exists a $k$-element subset $B$ of the set $\{2,3, \cdots, n\}$, such that no element in $B$ is a multiple of another, and $B$ contains $A$.

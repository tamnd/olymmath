Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a circle of radius 1, and let $\mathbf{T}$ be a real number. We say that a set of triangles is $\mathbf{T}$-meric if it satisfies the following three conditions:

$\triangleright$ the vertices of each triangle belong to $\mathcal{C}$;

$\triangleright$ the triangles have pairwise disjoint interiors (but two triangles may share a side or a vertex);

$\triangleright$ each triangle has a perimeter strictly greater than $T$.

Find all real numbers $\mathbf{T}$ such that, for any integer $n \geqslant 1$, there exists a $\mathbf{T}$-meric set containing exactly $n$ triangles.

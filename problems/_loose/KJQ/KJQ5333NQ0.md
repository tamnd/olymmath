Let $\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}$, and $\Phi_{3}$ be three arbitrary figures.

a) Prove that the sum of the distances from $\Phi_{1}$ to $\Phi_{2}$ and from $\Phi_{2}$ to $\Phi_{3}$ is not less than the distance from $\Phi_{1}$ to $\Phi_{3}$:

$$
\rho\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right)+\rho\left(\Phi_{2}, \Phi_{3}\right) \geqslant \rho\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{3}\right)
$$

b) Can the inequality

$$
\rho\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right)+\rho\left(\Phi_{2}, \Phi_{3}\right)<\rho\left(\Phi_{3}, \Phi_{1}\right)
$$

hold?

Sometimes it is convenient to characterize the deviation of figure $\Phi_{1}$ from figure $\Phi_{2}$ by the maximum of the previously defined distances from $\Phi_{1}$ to $\Phi_{2}$ and from $\Phi_{2}$ to $\Phi_{1}$. The resulting quantity is called the distance between figures $\Phi_{1}$ and $\Phi_{2}$ and is denoted by $\mathrm{P}\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right)$; thus:

$$
\mathrm{P}\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right)=\max \left[\rho\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right), \rho\left(\Phi_{2}, \Phi_{1}\right)\right]
$$

It is clear that the distance $\mathrm{P}\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right)$ is already symmetric: for any two figures $\Phi_{1}$ and $\Phi_{2}$, it is always

$$
\mathrm{P}\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right)=\mathrm{P}\left(\Phi_{2}, \Phi_{1}\right)
$$

Moreover, $\mathrm{P}\left(\Phi_{1}, \Phi_{2}\right)=0$ in the case when figures $\Phi_{1}$ and $\Phi_{2}$ coincide $\left.{ }^{1}\right)$.

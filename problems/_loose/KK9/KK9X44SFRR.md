Let $\left\langle p_{1}, p_{2}, p_{3}, \cdots, p_{n}, \cdots\right\rangle$ be a sequence of primes defined by $p_{1}=2$ and for $n \geq 1, p_{n+1}$ is the largest prime factor of $p_{1} p_{2} \cdots p_{n}+1$. (Thus $p_{2}=3, p_{3}=7$ ). Prove that $p_{n} \neq 5$ for any $n$.

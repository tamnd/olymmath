Consider a triangle. The "mid-distance" of a line in the same plane from the triangle is understood as the sum of the distances of the vertices from the line if the line does not intersect the triangle; if it does intersect, then the absolute value of the difference between the distance of the vertex on one side or the sum of the distances of the vertices on one side and the distances of the vertices on the other side is called the "mid-distance". - Where do those lines lie for which the mid-distance from the triangle is a given value $d$?

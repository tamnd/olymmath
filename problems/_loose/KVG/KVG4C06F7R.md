We say that a set $ A$ of non-zero vectors from the plane has the property $ \left( \mathcal S \right)$ iff it has at least three elements and for all $ \overrightarrow u \in A$ there are $ \overrightarrow v, \overrightarrow w \in A$ such that $ \overrightarrow v \neq \overrightarrow w$ and $ \overrightarrow u = \overrightarrow v + \overrightarrow w$.

(a) Prove that for all $ n \geq 6$ there is a set of $ n$ non-zero vectors, which has the property $ \left( \mathcal S \right)$.

(b) Prove that every finite set of non-zero vectors, which has the property $ \left( \mathcal S \right)$, has at least $ 6$ elements.

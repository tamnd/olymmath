A Strange Society. One person recounted that once he found himself in a society consisting (including himself) of twelve people, and
a) each of them was not acquainted with six other people, but knew all the others;

b) each belonged to some trio of people who knew each other;

c) among those gathered, it was impossible to select four people who all knew each other;

d) among those gathered, it was also impossible to select four people who were all mutually unacquainted;

e) each of those gathered belonged to a trio of mutually unacquainted people;

f) each could find among the people he did not know someone with whom he had no common acquaintances in this company.

Upon hearing this story, Dr. Sharadek stated that once he was in a society where conditions b), c), d), and e) were met, but each person knew exactly six people and had an acquaintance who could introduce him to everyone else.

Explain these situations.

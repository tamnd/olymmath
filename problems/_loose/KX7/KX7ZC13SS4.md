Consider $2 \mathrm{n}$ strictly positive integers. A pairing is a distribution of these integers into $\mathrm{n}$ pairs of integers that are pairwise disjoint. For example, $\{(1,3),(5,7)\}$ is a pairing of the integers $1,3,5,7$. However, $\{(1,3),(3,7)\}$ is not a pairing of the integers $1,3,5,7$. A pairing is said to be square-free if the product of the two numbers in each pair is never a perfect square. Show that if there exists a square-free pairing, there are at least $n$ such pairings!

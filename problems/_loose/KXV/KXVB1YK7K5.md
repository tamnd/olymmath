For two triangles $\triangle ABC, \triangle A_{1} B_{1} C_{1}$ on the same plane, if the lines connecting corresponding vertices $A A_{1}, B B_{1}, C C_{1}$ intersect at a point $S$, and if the lines containing corresponding sides $BC$ and $B_{1} C_{1}$ intersect at $P$, the lines containing $CA$ and $C_{1} A_{1}$ intersect at $Q$, and the lines containing $AB$ and $A_{1} B_{1}$ intersect at $R$, then $P, Q, R$ are collinear. (Desargues' Theorem. See Example 12 in Chapter 11 of the first volume)

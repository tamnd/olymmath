\section*{Problem 3B - 141043B}

Six students in a mathematics circle are playing a guessing game as a small logic exercise. Peter, Klaus, Monika, Ilona, and Uwe hide five objects:

Compass, eraser, ruler, pencil, and pen in such a way that each of them has exactly one of these objects. Then Dirk is given five statements, of which he is also told that exactly two are false. The statements are:

Uwe: "If Peter does not have the compass, then Klaus does not have the ruler."

Monika: "Uwe has just made a true statement."

Peter: "I have the compass, or Klaus does not have the ruler."

Klaus: "I do not have the ruler, or Uwe has the pencil."

Ilona: "I have the pen, or I have the pencil."

Determine whether all the hiding places of the objects can be uniquely determined according to these rules! What are the hiding places, if this is possible?

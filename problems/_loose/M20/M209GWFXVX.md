Is the equality $\left(a^{4}-1\right)^{6}=\left(a^{6}-1\right)^{4}$ an identity if: 1) $a$ is any number; 2) $a=\{-1 ; 0 ; 1\}$?

There are cases where it is quite difficult to find an example that refutes a general statement. More often, it is easier to prove that such an example exists than to specify it. For instance, if it is known that 100 students received 101 notebooks, and each student received at least one notebook, then it is obvious that someone received two notebooks. The fact that such a student exists is important, and we are not even interested in who exactly received two notebooks. Therefore, the statement "each student received only one notebook" is false. This method of reasoning is often used in solving problems. In a humorous form, it is formulated in the magazine "Kvant" as follows: "It is impossible to put seven rabbits in three cages so that there are no more than two rabbits in each cage."

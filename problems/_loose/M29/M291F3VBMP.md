In math class, the teacher presents the task of determining the side lengths of an isosceles triangle $ABC$ with $AC = BC$, given that one of the medians of this triangle divides the perimeter in such a way that one part is $12 \text{ cm}$ and the other is $9 \text{ cm}$. Individual students express the following opinions:

Achim: "The problem has no solution, because the median of an isosceles triangle is a line of symmetry and can therefore only divide the perimeter into two equal parts."

Birgit: "There is exactly one triangle, up to congruence, that meets the requirements of the problem."

Claudia: "The problem has exactly two (non-congruent) solutions up to congruence."

Dorit: "Since you can draw three medians in a triangle, the problem has at least three non-congruent solutions."

Investigate which of the four students is correct and justify your findings!

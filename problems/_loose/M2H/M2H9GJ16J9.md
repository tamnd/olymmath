Prove that the sum $K_{1}+K_{2}$ of two convex polygons $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$ is itself a convex polygon. If the convex polygons $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$ do not have parallel and identically directed sides (see above, p. 26), then the number of sides of the convex polygon $K_{1}+K_{2}$ is equal to the sum of the number of sides of $K_{1}$ and the number of sides of $K_{2}$; this polygon consists of segments equal and parallel to the sides of the polygons $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$. If the polygons $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$ have parallel and identically directed sides, then the polygon $K_{1}+K_{2}$ has a side parallel to the two parallel sides of the polygons $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$ and equal in length to their sum. In all cases, the perimeter of the polygon $K_{1}+K_{2}$ is equal to the sum of the perimeters of the polygons $K_{1}$ and $K_{2}$.
1) For a rigorous proof, it is sufficient to consider a sequence of polygons inscribed in the curve $K$, the number of whose sides increases, and all sides decrease without bound; form the sum of each such polygon and the circle $O_{\text {r }}^{\text {и }}$ and then pass to the limit.

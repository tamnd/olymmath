Suppose there are several points in space, where no four points are coplanar, and some pairs of points are connected by line segments, thus forming a graph. If the minimum number of colors needed to color these points, such that each point is colored with one of the $n$ colors, and no two points of the same color are connected by a line segment, is $n$, then the graph is called an $n$-color graph. Prove that for any $n \in \mathbf{N}$, there exists an $n$-color graph that does not contain any triangles.
(1993 National Training Team Selection Examination Question)

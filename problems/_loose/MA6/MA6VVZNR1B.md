Two triangles $A B C$ and $A^{\prime} B^{\prime} C^{\prime}$ lie in different planes, such that $(B C) \cap\left(B^{\prime} C^{\prime}\right)=X, (C A) \cap\left(C^{\prime} A^{\prime}\right)=Y, (A B) \cap$ $\cap\left(A^{\prime} B^{\prime}\right)=Z$. Prove that a) $X \in(Y Z)$; b) either $\left(A A^{\prime}\right) \cap\left(B B^{\prime}\right) \cap$ $\cap\left(C C^{\prime}\right)=S$, or $\left(A A^{\prime}\right)\left\|\left(B B^{\prime}\right)\right\|\left(C C^{\prime}\right)$.

Prove the converse statement: if for triangles $A B C$ and $A^{\prime} B^{\prime} C^{\prime}$ we have: $\left(A A^{\prime}\right) \cap\left(B B^{\prime}\right) \cap\left(C C^{\prime}\right)=S$ or $\left(A A^{\prime}\right) \|$ $\left\|\left(B B^{\prime}\right)\right\|\left(C C^{\prime}\right)$ and $(B C) \cap\left(B^{\prime} C^{\prime}\right)=X,(C A) \cap\left(C^{\prime} A^{\prime}\right)=Y,(A B) \cap$ $\cap\left(A^{\prime} B^{\prime}\right)=Z$, then $X \in(Y Z)$. Consider the case when the corresponding sides of the triangles are parallel.

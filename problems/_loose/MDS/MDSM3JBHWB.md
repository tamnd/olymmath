A natural number $N$ is represented in the form $N=a_{1}-a_{2}=b_{1}-$ $-b_{2}=c_{1}-c_{2}=d_{1}-d_{2}$, where $a_{1}$ and $a_{2}$ are squares, $b_{1}$ and $b_{2}$ are cubes, $c_{1}$ and $c_{2}$ are fifth powers, and $d_{1}$ and $d_{2}$ are seventh powers of natural numbers. Is it necessarily true that among the numbers $a_{1}, b_{1}, c_{1}$, and $d_{1}$, there will be two that are equal?

(A.S. Golyanov)

Let $a, b, c$ be integers, and $a>0, a c-b^{2}=p=p_{1} p_{2} \cdots p_{n}$, where $p_{1}, p_{2}, \cdots, p_{n}$ are distinct primes. Let $M(n)$ denote the number of integer solutions to the equation $a x^{2}+2 b x y + c y^{2}=n$.

Prove that $M(n)$ is finite, and for each non-negative integer $k$, $M\left(p^{k} n\right) = M(n)$.
(34th International Mathematical Olympiad Preliminary Problem, 1993)

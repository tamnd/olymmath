(This question is worth 50 points)
Let $S_{n}=1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}, n$ be a positive integer. Prove: For any real numbers $a, b$ satisfying $0 \leqslant a<b \leqslant 1$, the sequence $\left\{S_{n}-\right.$ $\left.[S_{n}]\right\}$ has infinitely many terms in $(a, b)$. Here, $[x]$ denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to the real number $x$.

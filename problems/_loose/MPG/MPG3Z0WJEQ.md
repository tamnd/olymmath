Two subsets of the set of natural numbers are called congruent if one is obtained from the other by a shift by an integer. (For example, the sets of even and odd numbers are congruent.) Is it possible to partition the set of natural numbers into an infinite number of (non-intersecting) infinite congruent subsets?

The Case of the Indistinguishable Twins... This case is more interesting than the previous ones. A robbery has been committed in London. Three suspects - recidivists A, B, and C - have been summoned for interrogation. Suspects A and B are twins and resemble each other so much that few people can tell them apart. The Scotland Yard files contain detailed information about all three, including their character, inclinations, and habits. In particular, it is known that both twins are timid by nature, and neither of them dares to commit a crime without an accomplice. Suspect B, on the other hand, is very bold and hates to go on a job with an accomplice. Moreover,
several witnesses testified that one of the twins was seen in a bar in Dover during the robbery, but it was impossible to determine which of the two twins was meant.

Assuming that no one other than A, B, and C was involved in the robbery, who among them is guilty and who is not?

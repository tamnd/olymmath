Of five visually identical balls, exactly three have the same weight; the other two, which are of equal weight to each other, have a different weight from each of the first-mentioned ones.

Prove that in every case (i.e., with every possible outcome of the weighings), three weighings are sufficient to identify the two last-mentioned balls, if the only tool available is a two-pan balance without weights!

Let $I$ be an open set in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $y=\varphi(x)$ be a function defined on $I$, with values in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$. (If $x=\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}\right) \in I$, then $y=\left(y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n}\right)$, where $y_{i}=\varphi_{i}\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}\right), i=1, \ldots, n$.) It is assumed that all partial derivatives $\frac{\partial \varphi_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}$ exist and are continuous, and that $\left|J_{\varphi}(x)\right|>0$, $x \in I$, where $J_{\varphi}(x)$ is the determinant of the Jacobian of the function $\varphi$:

$$
J_{\varphi}(x)=\operatorname{det}\left\|\frac{\partial \varphi_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|, 1 \leqslant i, j \leqslant n
$$

Show that if $X=\left(X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n}\right)$ is a random vector with values in $I$, having a density $f_{X}(x)$, and $Y=\varphi(X)$, then the density $f_{Y}(y)$ exists and on the set $\varphi(I)=\{y: y=\varphi(x), x \in I\}$ is given by the following formula:

$$
f_{Y}(y)=f_{X}(\psi(y))\left|J_{\psi}(y)\right|,
$$

where $\psi=\varphi^{-1}$ is the inverse function of $\varphi$ (and $\left|J_{\psi}(y)\right|>0$, since $\left|J_{\varphi}(x)\right|>0$).

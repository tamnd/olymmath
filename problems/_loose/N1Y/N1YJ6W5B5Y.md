A mad physicist has discovered a particle called imon. Some pairs of these particles are entangled. The physicist is able to perform the following two operations, one at a time:

(i) If an imon is entangled with an odd number of other imons, then he can delete it.

(ii) He can double the set of imons he possesses, copying each imon. Copies of two entangled imons are themselves entangled, and each imon is entangled with its copy. No other entanglements appear.

Show that regardless of the initial configuration, the physicist can ensure that after a certain number of operations, there are no pairs of imons entangled.

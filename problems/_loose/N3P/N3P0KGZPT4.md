Let $\mathrm{P}, \mathrm{P}^{\prime} \in \Gamma$ be two diametrically opposite points. Prove that the Simson lines of $\mathrm{P}$ and $\mathrm{P}^{\prime}$ intersect at a point on the Euler circle of $\triangle ABC$.

Complete Quadrilaterals We explore here some properties of complete quadrilaterals, which are figures determined by four distinct lines, each intersecting the others, with the intersection of any three of them being empty. More precisely, let $ABC$ be a triangle. Suppose a line $\Delta$ intersects $(BC)$, $(CA)$, and $(AB)$ at $D$, $E$, and $F$ respectively. We refer to this as a complete quadrilateral BCEF.

Theorem 3.29 (Newton Line of a Complete Quadrilateral). Let $M_{1}, M_{2}, M_{3}$ be the midpoints of $[AD]$, $[BE]$, and $[CF]$ respectively. The points $M_{1}, M_{2}, M_{3}$ are collinear on a line called the Newton line of the complete quadrilateral.

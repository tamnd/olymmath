Given a quadratic trinomial $P(x)$, not necessarily with integer coefficients. It is known that for some integers $a$ and $b$, the difference $P(a)-P(b)$ is a square of a natural number. Prove that there are more than a million such pairs of integers $(c, d)$ for which the difference $P(c)-P(d)$ is also a square of a natural number.

(N. Agakhanov)

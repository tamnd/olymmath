The people of an ancient tribe used a language in which words were formed only with the letters $A$ and $B$. Researchers discovered that for any two words of equal length, there are at least three corresponding positions where the letters are different. For example, the words $A B B A A$ and $A A A A B$ differ in positions 2, 3, and 5, i.e., in three positions.

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}, n \geq 3$. Prove that in this language, there cannot be more than $\left[\frac{2^{n}}{n+1}\right]$ words of length $n$ (where $[a]$ is the integer part of the real number $a$).

In one city, there live truth-tellers (who always tell the truth), liars (who always lie), and normals (who sometimes lie and sometimes tell the truth). In a courtroom, a prosecutor, a defense attorney, and an accused have been brought together, and it is known that one of them is a truth-teller, one is a liar, and one is a normal (but it is not known who is who). The court has evidence that the crime was committed by either the accused, the prosecutor, or the defense attorney, and it is also known that the person who committed the crime is not a liar. The three of them made the following statements:

- Accused: "I am innocent."
- Defense Attorney: "The accused is innocent."
- Prosecutor: "The accused is guilty."

Since the court was unable to reach a verdict, an inspector from another city was called in. He decided to not only determine who is guilty but also to identify who among the three is the truth-teller, who is the liar, and who is the normal. First, he asked: "Prosecutor, are you guilty of this crime?" The prosecutor answered, but the inspector still did not have all the desired information, so he asked another question: "Defense Attorney, is the prosecutor guilty of this crime?" The defense attorney answered, and that was enough for the inspector to find out everything he needed. Determine who is guilty of the crime, as well as who is the truth-teller, who is the liar, and who is the normal.

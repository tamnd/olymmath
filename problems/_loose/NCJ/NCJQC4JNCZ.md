A rectangular parallelepiped is cut into 2, then 4, and finally 8 smaller rectangular parallelepipeds with three consecutive cuts. We know that the total surface area of the resulting bodies increases by the same factor at each cut. Show that in this case, the volume of the original rectangular parallelepiped is exactly twice the volume of the largest cube that can be inscribed in it.

We call a number of different numbers beautifully spread if, after sorting them from smallest to largest, consecutive numbers always have the same difference. The numbers 3, 11, and 7, for example, are beautifully spread, because when you sort them from smallest to largest, the difference is always 4.

a) Kees starts with three different numbers. He adds each pair of them together and gets three results. According to Jan, these three results can only be beautifully spread if the numbers Kees starts with are beautifully spread.

Is Jan right? If so, prove it; if not, show with an example that Jan is wrong.

b) Jan starts with four different numbers. He also adds each pair of them together and gets six results. He wants to start with a quartet such that his six results are beautifully spread.

Can he achieve this? If so, give an example; if not, prove that it is not possible.

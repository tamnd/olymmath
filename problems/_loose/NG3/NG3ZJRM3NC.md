(2003-2004 Hungarian Mathematical Olympiad) Let $c, d$ be integers. Prove that there exist infinitely many different integer pairs $\left(x_{n}, y_{n}\right)(n=1,2, \cdots)$, such that $x_{n}$ is a divisor of $c y_{n}+d$ and $y_{n}$ is a divisor of $c x_{n}+d$ if and only if $c$ is a divisor of $d$.

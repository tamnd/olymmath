It is known for a triangle that the transversals connecting the points of tangency of the inscribed circle with the opposite vertices meet at a single point.

To be proven, that the transversals connecting the points of tangency of the inscribed sphere of a tetrahedron with the opposite vertices meet at a single point if and only if the tetrahedron is isogonic, that is, if all edges subtend an angle of $120^{\circ}$ from the (adjacent) points of tangency of the inscribed sphere.

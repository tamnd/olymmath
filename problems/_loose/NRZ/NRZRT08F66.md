In a Thriving State with a Gini index of 0.1, there are only two equally numerous population groups - the poor and the rich. Within each group, residents do not differ in their incomes. After economic reforms in Thriving, a middle class (residents with average income) emerged in the country, the number of which turned out to be equal to the number of the poor and the rich. Within each group, residents still do not differ in their incomes. At the same time, the share of the poor's income in the total income of the country's residents was halved, and the Gini index did not change.

Determine, if possible, by how many times the share of the rich's income in Thriving has changed, or justify why it is impossible to determine this value.

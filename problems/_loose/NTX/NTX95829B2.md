Let $S, T$ be two non-empty sets. If there exists a function $y=f(x)$ from $S$ to $T$ satisfying:
( $i$ ) $T=\{f(x) \mid x \in S\}$; (ii) $\forall x_{1}, x_{2} \in S$, when $x_{1}<x_{2}$, it always holds that $f\left(x_{1}\right)<f\left(x_{2}\right)$.
Then these two sets are said to be "order-isomorphic". Prove:
(1) $A=(0,1), B=\mathbb{R}$ are order-isomorphic;
(2) Determine whether $A=\mathbb{Z}, B=\mathbb{Q}$ are order-isomorphic. If they are, provide a function expression; if not, explain the reason.

The thirty-third question: A fanatical physicist discovered a particle in his laboratory, which he called "imon". This mysterious particle, once it appears, causes some imons in the laboratory to become entangled. Each imon can participate in multiple entanglement relationships. The physicist performs the following two operations on this particle, choosing only one operation at a time:
(1) If an imon is entangled with an odd number of imons, the physicist can eliminate these entanglements and destroy this imon;
(2) At any time, the physicist can double the number of imons in the laboratory by creating a replica I' for each imon I, such that the two replicas I' and J' are entangled if and only if the original I and J are entangled, and each replica I' is entangled with the original imon I.

Prove: The physicist can apply a series of the above operations to some imons, such that in the end, no two remaining imons are entangled.

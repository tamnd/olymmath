We colored the vertices of a tetrahedron blue, red, yellow, and green, respectively, and marked the vertices with the corresponding capital letters of the colors. Then we colored the edges of the tetrahedron so that the color of each edge matches the color of one of its endpoints, and in doing so, we reused all four colors. Prove that there is a vertex from which one of the outgoing edges is blue, another is red, and the third is green, or among the edges connecting the other three points, one is blue, another is red, and the third is green.

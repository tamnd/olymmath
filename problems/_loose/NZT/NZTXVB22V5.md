Al-Kalsadi's Rule. If $\boldsymbol{a}^{2}$ is the greatest square contained in a number, and $r$ is the remainder, then

$$
\sqrt{a^{2}+r}=a+\frac{r}{2 a}
$$ if $r \gtrless a$. If $r > a$, a more accurate value is given:

$$
\sqrt{a^{2}+r}=a+\frac{r+1}{2 a+2}
$$

Determine the path the Arab mathematician followed in establishing this rule.

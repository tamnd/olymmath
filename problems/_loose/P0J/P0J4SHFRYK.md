$12.7 m$ boxes, each containing some balls. Let $n<m$ be a known natural number, and perform the following operation: choose $n$ of these boxes, and place one ball in each of the chosen boxes. Prove:
(1) If $(m, n)=1$, then it is possible to perform a finite number of operations to make the number of balls in all boxes equal.
(2) If $(m, n) \neq 1$, then there exists an initial distribution of the balls in these boxes such that it is impossible to achieve equality through the above operations (any number of times).
(26th International Mathematical Olympiad Candidate Problem, 1985)

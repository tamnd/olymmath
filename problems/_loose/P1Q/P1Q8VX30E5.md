At a scientific congress, $n$ scientists participate, some of whom are honest and some are not. Among the participants, the honest ones are in the majority, and they always tell the truth, while the dishonest ones sometimes say yes and sometimes no. We need to determine which participants are from where. For this purpose, we can choose anyone and ask anyone else whether the chosen one belongs to the honest or dishonest group. Show that for this, $n^{2} / 2$ questions are sufficient.

Would $10 n$ questions also be sufficient?

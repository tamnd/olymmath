Given the ellipse $\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}=1(a>b>0)$ has an eccentricity $e=\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$, and the distance from the origin to the line passing through points $A(0,-b)$ and $B(a, 0)$ is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
(1) Find the equation of the ellipse;
(2) Given a fixed point $E(-1,0)$, the line $y=k x+t$ intersects the ellipse at points $C$ and $D$. Prove that for any $t>0$, there exists a $k$ such that the circle with diameter $C D$ passes through point $E$.

In the infinite sequence $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}, \ldots$ of positive integers, let $f_{n}$ denote the number of occurrences of the natural number $n$, if this value is finite.

The "frequency sequence" of the sequence $a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots$ is then the sequence $f_{1}, f_{2}, \ldots$, if every $f_{i}$ is finite. Similarly, we interpret the above $f_{1}, f_{2}, \ldots$ sequence as the second-order frequency sequence of the sequence $a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots$, if every frequency is finite, and as the third-order frequency sequence of the above, and so on. Is there a sequence $a_{1}, a_{2}, \ldots$ for which the $k$-th order frequency sequence exists for every $k$?

Given two ellipses, their equations are (1) and (2). Let $g$ and $h$ be the lines of two conjugate diameters of (1), and let these lines intersect (2) at the diameters $G_{1} G_{2}$ and $H_{1} H_{2}$, respectively. Prove that the tangents to (2) at these intersection points are the sides of a rectangle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{x^{2}}{a^{4}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{4}}=1 \\
& \frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}=1
\end{aligned}
$$[^0]

[^0]: ${ }^{1} \mathrm{The}$ conjugate of a diameter $d_{1}$ of an ellipse is the diameter $d_{2}$ which is parallel to the tangents at the endpoints of $d_{1}$; then $d_{2}$ is the conjugate of $d_{1}$.

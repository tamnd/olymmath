For an arbitrary tetrahedron, the center of its circumscribed sphere is $O$, and its centroid is $S$. It needs to be proven that the perpendicular planes erected from the midpoints of the edges to the opposite edges intersect the line $OS$ at the reflection of point $O$ in point $S$, denoted as $T$. (The line $OST$ is a generalization of the Euler line $OSM$ of an orthocentric tetrahedron.)

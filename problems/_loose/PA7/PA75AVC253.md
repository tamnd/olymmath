The 37th question: Given positive integers $\mathrm{k}, \mathrm{A}, \mathrm{~B}, \mathrm{C}$ play a game (A on one side, $\mathrm{B}$ and $\mathrm{C}$ on the other side): A first selects $\mathrm{k}$ numbers from the set $\{1,2, \ldots, \mathrm{n}\}$ and gives them to $\mathrm{B}, \mathrm{B}$ then chooses $\mathrm{k}-1$ of these numbers and gives them to $\mathrm{C}$ in order. If $\mathrm{C}$ can determine which number $\mathrm{B}$ did not give to $\mathrm{C}$, then $\mathrm{B}$ and $\mathrm{C}$ win. Find the largest positive integer $\mathrm{n}$ such that $\mathrm{B}$ and $\mathrm{C}$ have a winning strategy.

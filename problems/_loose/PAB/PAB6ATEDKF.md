[7] Two non-intersecting wooden circles of not necessarily the same size - a gray one and a black one - are glued to a plane. An infinite wooden angle is given, one side of which is gray and the other is black. It is moved so that the circles are outside the angle, with the gray side touching the gray circle and the black side touching the black circle (the contact does not occur at the vertex). Prove that inside the angle, a ray can be drawn from the vertex such that for all possible positions of the angle, this ray passes through the same point on the plane.

E. Bakayev, I. Bogdanov, P. Kozhevnikov, V. Rastorguev

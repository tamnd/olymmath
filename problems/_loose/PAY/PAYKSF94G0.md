To assemble products A, B, and C, three types of parts, $A$, $B$, and $C$, are required. Each product A requires 2 $A$ and 2 $B$ parts; each product B requires 1 $B$ and 1 $C$ part; each product C requires 2 $A$ and 1 $C$ part. Using the inventory of $A$, $B$, and $C$ parts, if $p$ pieces of product A, $q$ pieces of product B, and $r$ pieces of product C are assembled, then 2 $A$ and 1 $B$ parts will be left over. However, $C$ parts will be exactly used up. Try to prove: no matter how the quantities of products A, B, and C are changed, it is impossible to use up all the inventory of $A$, $B$, and $C$ parts exactly.

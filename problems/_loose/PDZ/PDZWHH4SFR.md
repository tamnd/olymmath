V a r i a n t A. According to the condition, the function $y=\sin x+a-b x$ turns into zero at exactly two points $x_{1}$ and $x_{2}, x_{1}<x_{2}$.

These points divide the number line into 3 intervals $\left(-\infty, x_{1}\right]$, $\left(x_{1}, x_{2}\right],\left(x_{2},+\infty\right)$. Since $b \neq 0$, and $|\sin x| \leqslant 1$, the function has different signs on the intervals $\left(-\infty, x_{1}\right)$ and $\left(x_{2},+\infty\right)$. Therefore, on some two adjacent intervals $\left(-\infty, x_{1}\right),\left(x_{1}, x_{2}\right)$ or $\left(x_{1}, x_{2}\right),\left(x_{2},+\infty\right)$, the function has the same sign, and then either the point $x_{1}$ or the point $x_{2}$ is an extremum point, and the derivative at this point $y^{\prime}=(\sin x+a-b x)^{\prime}=\cos x-b$ turns into zero.

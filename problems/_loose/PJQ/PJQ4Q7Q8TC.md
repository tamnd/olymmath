In an examination with $n$ questions, a student answered 15 out of the first 20 questions correctly, and for the remaining questions, he answered $\frac{1}{3}$ correctly. All questions have the same score. If this student's score is $50 \%$, then the number of different values of $n$ is
(A) 4.
(B) 3.
(C) 2.
(D) 1.
(E) This problem cannot be solved.
(10th American High School Mathematics Examination, 1959)

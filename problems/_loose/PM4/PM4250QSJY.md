Krosh, having found the roots of the equation $x^{2}-x-1=0$, invented the "Krosh's golden system of numeration," a system of numeration with base $\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$. Losyash noted that, in fact, Krosh finds decompositions of positive numbers into sums of certain different integer powers of the number $\varphi$. For example, in this system of numeration, the record 11,1 corresponds to the number

$$
1+\sqrt{5}=\varphi^{1}+\varphi^{0}+\varphi^{-1}
$$

Losyash also stated that if a number has a decomposition, then it has infinitely many decompositions and it is always possible to do without a decomposition that includes a pair of consecutive powers of $\varphi$; the decomposition itself exists for all positive numbers $\frac{m+n \sqrt{5}}{2}$, where $m, n$ are integers of the same parity. Prove that Losyash is right, for which:

a) Find at least five different decompositions of the number 1. (2 points)

b) Prove that if a number can be decomposed into such a sum, then it can also be decomposed into a sum that does not include a pair of consecutive powers of $\varphi$.

c) Prove that if $a$ is decomposable, then $a+1$ is also decomposable.

2) Prove that the sum and product of decomposable numbers are also decomposable.

d) Prove that if for some integers $m, n$ of the same parity, the number $\frac{m+n \sqrt{5}}{2}$ is positive, then this number is decomposable.

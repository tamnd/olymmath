A point in the coordinate plane where both coordinates $x$ and $y$ are rational numbers is called a rational point. Try to prove that all rational points $(x, y)$ can be divided into two point sets $A$ and $B$, such that set $A$ intersects with any line parallel to the $y$-axis at only a finite number of points, and set $B$ intersects with any line parallel to the $x$-axis at only a finite number of points.
If the number of terms in $|y|$ is greater than the number of terms in $|x|$, then $(x, y) \in B$. It is easy to verify that the $A$ and $B$ defined in this way satisfy all the requirements in the problem.

A subset $\bf{S}$ of the plane is called *convex* if given any two points $x$ and $y$ in $\bf{S}$, the line segment joining $x$ and $y$ is contained in $\bf{S}$. A quadrilateral is called *convex* if the region enclosed by the edges of the quadrilateral is a convex set.

Show that given a convex quadrilateral $Q$ of area $1$, there is a rectangle $R$ of area $2$ such that $Q$ can be drawn inside $R$.

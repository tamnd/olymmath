(2006 Beijing College Entrance Examination Final Question) In the sequence $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$, if $a_{1}, a_{2}$ are positive integers, and $a_{n}=\left|a_{n-1}-a_{n-2}\right|$, $n=3,4,5, \cdots$, then $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$ is called an "absolute difference sequence".
(1) Give an example of an "absolute difference sequence" whose first five terms are not zero (only the first ten terms are required to be written);
(2) If in the "absolute difference sequence" $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$, $a_{20}=3, a_{21}=$ 0, and the sequence $\left\{b_{n}\right\}$ satisfies $b_{n}=a_{n}+a_{n+1}+a_{n+2}, n=1,2$, $3, \cdots$, determine whether the limits of $a_{n}$ and $b_{n}$ exist as $n \rightarrow \infty$. If they exist, find their limit values;
(3) Prove: Any "absolute difference sequence" contains infinitely many zero terms.

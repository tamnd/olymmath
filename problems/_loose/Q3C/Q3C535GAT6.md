There are $n$ balls, divide them into two piles arbitrarily, find the product of the number of balls in these two piles, then choose one of the piles and divide it into two piles arbitrarily, find the product of the number of balls in these two piles, and so on, each time choose one pile arbitrarily, divide this pile into two piles arbitrarily, find the product of the number of balls in these two piles, until no more division is possible.
Prove: Regardless of how the piles are divided, the sum of all products remains constant.

The sum of the squares of two arbitrary natural numbers is to be formed. Then, a "division with remainder" is to be performed, specifically, the above-mentioned sum is to be divided by 4. We want to investigate which numbers can and which cannot appear as remainders in such a division.

a) First, form some examples by choosing two natural numbers each time, dividing the sum of their squares by 4, and noting the remainder that appears! Continue forming such examples until there is only one natural number less than 4 that has not appeared as a remainder in your examples!

b) Now, one can conjecture that this number will never appear as a remainder when the sum of the squares of two arbitrary natural numbers is divided by 4.

Prove this conjecture!

Let $n \geqslant 3$ be an integer. We say that a non-zero natural number is reachable if it is 1 or if it can be obtained from 1 through a sequence of operations such that:

- The first operation is an addition or a multiplication.
- Subsequently, the operations alternate between addition and multiplication.
- At each step, the number obtained from the previous step (initially 1) is subjected to the operation, either with 2 or with $n$.

1. Show that, if $n$ is sufficiently large, there are infinitely many non-reachable integers.
2. Show that, for $\mathrm{n}=3$, all integers except 7 are reachable.

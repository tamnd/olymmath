At the "Come on, monsters!" competition, 15 dragons are standing in a row. The number of heads of neighboring dragons differs by 1. If a dragon has more heads than both of its neighbors, it is considered cunning; if it has fewer heads than both of its neighbors, it is considered strong; the rest (including those standing at the ends) are considered ordinary. In the row, there are exactly four cunning dragons with 4, 6, 7, and 7 heads, and exactly three strong dragons with 3, 3, and 6 heads. The first and last dragons have the same number of heads.

a) Provide an example of how this could be.

[4 points]

b) Prove that the number of heads of the first dragon is the same in all examples. [6 points]

(A.V. Shapovalov, I.V. Yashchenko)

Given the parabola $y^{2}=2 p x(p>0)$. A line $l$ passing through the point $E\left(\frac{p^{2}}{4}, 0\right)$ intersects the parabola at points $A\left(x_{1}, y_{1}\right)$ and $B\left(x_{2}, y_{2}\right)$, satisfying $x_{1}+x_{2}+p=|A B|$. Another line $l^{\prime}$ also passes through point $E$ and is perpendicular to $l$, intersecting the parabola at points $C$ and $D$. Let $C^{\prime}$ and $D^{\prime}$ be the points symmetric to $C$ and $D$ with respect to point $E$, and the slope of line $l$ exists and is greater than 0.
(1) Find the equation of the parabola.
(2) Does there exist an ellipse with the y-axis as its axis of symmetry and an eccentricity of $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ that passes through points $A$, $B$, $C^{\prime}$, and $D^{\prime}$? If it exists, write the standard equation of the ellipse and prove it; if not, explain the reason.

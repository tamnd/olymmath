Let $a$ and $b$ be two integers, $d$ a non-zero natural number, and $a^{\prime}$ and $b^{\prime}$ the two remainders of the Euclidean division of $a$ and $b$ by $d$. Show that $a \equiv b(\bmod d)$ if and only if $a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}$.

The remarkable thing about congruence relations is that they allow, when considering operations of addition, subtraction, and multiplication, to behave like the equality relation.

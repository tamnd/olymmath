Let $a\leqslant b\leqslant c$ be non-negative integers. A triangle on a checkered plane with vertices in the nodes of the grid is called an $(a,b,c)$ *-triangle* if there are exactly $a{}$ nodes on one side of it (not counting vertices), exactly $b{}$ nodes on the second side, and exactly $c{}$ nodes on the third side.

- Does there exist a $(9,10,11)$ -triangle?
- Find all triples of non-negative integers $a\leqslant b\leqslant c$ for which there exists an $(a,b,c)$ -triangle.
- For each such triple, find the minimum possible area of the $(a,b,c)$ -triangle.

*Proposed by P. Kozhevnikov*

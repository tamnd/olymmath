For an arbitrary point $S$ of a convex polyhedron, consider the corresponding polyhedral angles with a common vertex $S$, "resting" on the faces of the polyhedron. These polyhedral angles in their union give the entire space. Taking this into account and using the formulas derived in previous problems, prove Euler's theorem ${ }^{1}$: "The sum of the number of faces and the number of vertices of a convex polyhedron is 2 more than the number of its edges" - in our notation: $f+p-a=2$.

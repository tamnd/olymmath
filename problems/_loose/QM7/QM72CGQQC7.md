On the interval, the left part is defined for $x \in [0 ; \pi / 2]$. Since the inequality does not hold for $x=0$ and $x=\pi / 2$, it is sufficient to consider the case $x \in (0 ; \pi / 2)$ for which the inequalities are satisfied:

$$
\sqrt{\sin x}>\sin ^{2} x, \sqrt{\cos x}>\cos ^{2} x
$$

Thus,

$$
(\sqrt{\sin x}+\sqrt{\cos x})^{7}>\left(\sin ^{2} x+\cos ^{2} x\right)^{7}=1
$$

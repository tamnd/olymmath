Let $N_{1}=A_{1} B_{1} C_{1} \ldots H_{1}$, and $N_{2}=A_{2} B_{2} \ldots G_{2} H_{2}$ be regular octagons where the sides $A_{1} B_{1}$ and $A_{2} B_{2}$ lie on the same line, as do the sides $D_{1} E_{1}$ and $D_{2} E_{2}$, and $G_{2}$ coincides with $C_{1}$, and $A_{2} B_{2}<A_{1} B_{1}$. The regular octagon $N_{i+1}$ is the same image of $N_{i}$ as $N_{2}$ is of $N_{1}$, for $i=2,3, \ldots$.

Show that if the construction of the $N_{i}$ polygons is continued beyond all bounds, the sum $r_{1}+r_{2}+\ldots$ of the radii of the circles circumscribed around the octagons tends to the length of the segment $A_{1} M$, where $M$ is the intersection point of the lines $A_{1} C_{1}$ and $D_{1} O_{2}$, and $O_{2}$ is the center of $N_{2}$.

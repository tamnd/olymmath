In a convex 1994-gon $ M $, 997 diagonals are drawn such that each vertex of $ M $ is the endpoint of exactly one diagonal. Each diagonal divides the perimeter of $ M $ into two parts, and the number of sides in the part with fewer sides is defined as the length of the diagonal. Arrange the lengths of the 997 diagonals in a non-increasing sequence $\left(d_{1}, d_{2}, \cdots, d_{997}\right)$. Can the sequence of diagonal lengths be:
(1) $\left(d_{1}, d_{2}, \cdots, d_{997}\right)=(3,3, \cdots, 3,2,2, \cdots, 2)$, with 991 threes and 6 twos?
(2) $\left(d_{1}, d_{2}, \cdots, d_{997}\right)=(8, \cdots, 8,6, \cdots, 6,3, \cdots, 3)$, with 4 eights, 985 sixes, and 8 threes?

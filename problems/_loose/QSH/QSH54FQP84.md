Alfred and Bernd share $n$ apples by deciding for each individual apple, in turn, through a random decision (e.g., flipping a coin) who will receive this apple.

Such a distribution process is called favorable for Alfred if and only if Alfred never has fewer apples in his possession than Bernd, not only at the end but throughout the entire process.

The probability $w(n)$ that a distribution process is favorable for Alfred is defined as the quotient obtained when the number of all favorable distribution processes for Alfred is divided by the number of all possible distribution processes.

(a) Determine $w(4)$.

(b) Determine $w(n)$ for any natural $n \geq 2$.

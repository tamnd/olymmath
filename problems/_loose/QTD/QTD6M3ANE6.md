If we draw two lines through each vertex of a triangle, which form equal angles with the angle bisector passing through the respective vertex, and if three of the six lines thus obtained intersect at a point, then the other three lines also intersect at a point. If one of these intersection points is the orthocenter of the triangle, then the other is the center of the circle circumscribed around the triangle; if, however, one of the intersection points lies at infinity, that is, three of the six lines are parallel, then the other three lines intersect each other on the circle circumscribed around the triangle.

(Dr. Lipót Klug, university private lecturer.)

Let $ \mathbb{A} \subset \mathbb{N} $ such that all elements in $ \mathbb{A} $ can be representable in the form of $ x^2+2y^2 $ , $ x,y \in \mathbb{N} $, and $ x>y $. Let $ \mathbb{B} \subset \mathbb{N} $ such that all elements in $ \mathbb{B} $ can be representable in the form of $ \frac{a^3+b^3+c^3}{a+b+c} $ , $ a,b,c \in \mathbb{N} $, and $ a,b,c $ are distinct.

a) Prove that $ \mathbb{A} \subset \mathbb{B} $.

b) Prove that there exist infinitely many positive integers $n$ satisfy $ n \in \mathbb{B}$ and $ n \not \in \mathbb{A} $

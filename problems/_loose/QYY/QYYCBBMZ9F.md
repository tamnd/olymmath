One after another, $2^{n-1}$ different sequences of zeros and ones of length $n$ are written down. It is known that for any three of the written sequences, there exists a number $p$ such that in the $p$-th position, all three have a 1. Prove that in some position, all the written sequences have a 1 and such a position is only one.

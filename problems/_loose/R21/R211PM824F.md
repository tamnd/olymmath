On the surface of a spherical planet, there are $2 n$ gas stations, whose positions are symmetric about the center of the sphere (i.e., for each gas station, there is a corresponding gas station at its symmetric point about the center). Each gas station stores a certain amount of fuel, and the fuel is non-renewable. It is known that a car with an initially empty (and infinitely large) fuel tank can start from any gas station, choose an appropriate route, refuel at the gas stations along the way, and reach the gas station symmetric to the starting point. Here, it is assumed that the car consumes a constant amount of fuel per unit length traveled.

Under the above conditions, find the value of $n$ such that the car can definitely traverse all the gas stations on the planet in some way (the starting and ending points are not restricted).

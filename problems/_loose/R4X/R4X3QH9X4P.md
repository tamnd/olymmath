a) Let $f:[0,1] \rightarrow [0, \infty)$ be a continuous function, different from the zero function. Prove that $\int_{0}^{1} f(x) d x > 0$.

b) For any $n \in \mathbb{N}^{*}$, consider the continuous functions $f_{1}, f_{2}, \ldots, f_{n}:[0,1] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ with the property $\int_{0}^{1} f_{k}^{2}(x) d x = \frac{2 k-1}{n}$, for any $k \in \{1,2,3, \ldots, n\}$. Prove that there exists $\alpha \in [0,1]$ such that $f_{1}(\alpha) + f_{2}(\alpha) + \ldots + f_{n}(\alpha) \leq n$.

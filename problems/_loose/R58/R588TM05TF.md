Four people, A, B, C, and D, each make a quadratic equation $x^{2} + p x + q = 0$ with solutions $x_{1}, x_{2}$ according to the following requirements:
A: $p, q, x_{1}, x_{2}$ are all integers that leave a remainder of 1 when divided by 3;
B: $p, q, x_{1}, x_{2}$ are all integers that leave a remainder of 2 when divided by 3;
C: $p, q$ are integers that leave a remainder of 1 when divided by 3, and $x_{1}, x_{2}$ are integers that leave a remainder of 2 when divided by 3;
D: $p, q$ are integers that leave a remainder of 2 when divided by 3, and $x_{1}, x_{2}$ are integers that leave a remainder of 1 when divided by 3.
Question: Can A, B, C, and D each make an equation according to the above requirements? If they can, please write down such an equation. If they cannot, please explain the reason.
(Chinese Beijing Junior High School Grade 2 Mathematics Competition, 1982)

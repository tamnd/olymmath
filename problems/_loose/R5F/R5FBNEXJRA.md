To erect a perpendicular at point $B$ on a given line segment $AB$, Roland performs the following construction:

He chooses a point $C$ between $A$ and $B$. Then he draws circles around $B$ and $C$ with the radius $BC$. One of the intersection points of these circles he calls $D$.

Finally, he draws the line through $C$ and $D$ and marks off a segment of length $CD$ from $D$ on the extension of $CD$. He calls the second endpoint of this segment $E$. Now he claims that the line through $B$ and $E$ is the desired perpendicular.

A deck of cards has: $(a) 36$ cards; $(b) 54$ cards. The magician divides them into several piles and writes a number on each card, which is equal to the number of cards in the pile it is in. Then, the magician uses a certain method to shuffle the cards into one pile, and divides them into several piles again, writing another number on each card to the right of the existing number, which is equal to the number of cards in the pile it is in during the second division. Can the magician achieve: all the pairs of numbers written are different, and for each pair $(m, n)$, there is a pair $(n, m)$?

Divide $\triangle ABC$ into several smaller triangles in any way, where any two small triangles either have no common points, or share only one common point, or share only one common side (not part of a side). All triangle vertices are labeled with the letters $A, B, C$ according to the following method: each vertex on the original triangle's sides is labeled with one of the original endpoints of that side; each vertex inside the triangle is labeled arbitrarily with $A, B, C$. Prove:
(1) There exists a small triangle whose vertices are labeled exactly with $A, B, C$;
(2) There exists a small triangle whose vertices are labeled with $A, B, C$ and whose orientation is the same as that of the original large triangle.

Point $M$ has coordinates $x$ and $y: M=M(x ; y)$. How will these coordinates change if the origin $O$ is moved to point $O^{\prime}$ with coordinates $(a ; b)$?

25*. The vector $\vec{r}=\vec{x} i-\overrightarrow{-}-\vec{y} j$ forms an angle $\varphi$ with the $O x$ axis, which is the angle by which the vector $\vec{i}$ must be rotated in the positive direction to align its direction with the direction of $\vec{r}$. It is known that $\frac{x}{r}:=\cos \varphi$ and $\frac{y}{r}=\sin \varphi$, where $r=|\vec{r}|$. Therefore, $\vec{r}=\overrightarrow{i r} \cos \varphi+\vec{j} r \sin \varphi$. a) Using this formula, establish the relationship between the vectors $\vec{i}$ and $\vec{j}$ and the new unit vectors $\overrightarrow{i^{\prime}}, \vec{j}^{\prime}$, which are obtained by rotating the coordinate axes around the origin $O$ by an angle $\varphi$. b) Point $M$ in the old system had coordinates $(x ; y)$. What formulas relate these coordinates to the new coordinates $x^{\prime}, y^{\prime}$ of the same point $M$?

26*. By rotating the coordinate axes around the origin first by an angle $\alpha$, obtaining the unit vectors $\overrightarrow{i^{\prime}}$ and $\overrightarrow{j^{\prime}}$, and then by an angle $\beta$, obtaining the new unit vectors $\overrightarrow{i^{\prime \prime}}$ and $\overrightarrow{j^{\prime \prime}}$, derive from the obtained dependencies the main trigonometric formulas for $\sin (\alpha+\beta)$ and $\cos (\alpha+\beta)$.

Show that there exists a subset $A$ of $\mathbb{R}^{*}$ such that:

$$
\forall x \in \mathbb{R}^{*}, \exists!(\lambda, a) \in \mathbb{Q} \times A, x=\lambda a
$$

Such a set $A$ is called a set of representatives of the quotient $\mathbb{R}^{*} / \mathbb{Q}$.

Now let's look at some paradoxical puzzles.

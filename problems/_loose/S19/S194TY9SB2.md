Frog On a $10 \times 10$ chessboard, two players, A and B, play a game. The operations are carried out alternately. A starts by placing an $\times$ in an empty cell, and B then places an ○ in an empty cell. When all 100 cells are filled, the two characteristic values $C$ and $Z$ of the table are calculated. $C$ is the number of groups of five consecutive $\times$ in a row, column, or diagonal. If there are six such $\times$, it counts as 2 groups; if there are 7 such $\times$, it counts as 3 groups, and so on. Similarly, $Z$ is calculated in the same way for $\bigcirc$. If $C>Z$, A wins; if $C<Z$, A loses; if $C=Z$, it is a draw. Does A have a strategy to ensure: (1) a draw; (2) a win.

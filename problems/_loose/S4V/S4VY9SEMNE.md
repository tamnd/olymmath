Proportional Representation. When the Crackhams stopped at Mangelton-on-Bliss, they found the townsfolk in a state of excitement due to the local elections. The elections were held on the principle of proportional representation. Each voter was given a ballot with 10 candidates' names.

The voter had to place a No. 1 opposite the candidate for whom they cast their first vote, a No. 2 opposite the candidate for whom they cast their second vote, and so on up to the tenth.

Voters had to place a "check" mark opposite No. 1, and could place "check" marks or not against the other numbers as they wished. George suggested to the other members of the family to find out how many different ways a voter could place "check" marks on their ballot.

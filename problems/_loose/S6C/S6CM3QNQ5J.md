Let $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{100}$ be a permutation of $1,2, \cdots, 100$, and for $1 \leqslant i \leqslant$ 100, let $b_{i}=a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{i}, r_{i}$ be the remainder when $b_{i}$ is divided by 100.
Prove that $r_{1}, r_{2}, \cdots, r_{100}$ take at least 11 different values.
(Canadian Mathematical Olympiad Training Problem, 1991)

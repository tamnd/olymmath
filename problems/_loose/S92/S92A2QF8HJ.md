Let $a, b, c$ be integers and $m$ be a natural number greater than 1. If

$$
a^{n}+b n+c \equiv 0(\bmod m)
$$

for every natural number $n$, prove that

$$
b^{2} \equiv 0(\bmod m)
$$

Must it be that $b \equiv 0(\bmod m)$?

In a country called "Odd Number Country," the face values of stamps are 1 cent, 3 cents, 5 cents, $\cdots \cdots$, all positive odd numbers. The national postal service has the following rule: on an envelope, for any two different face values, the number of stamps with the higher face value cannot exceed the number of stamps with the lower face value.

In another country called "Square Number Country," the face values of stamps are 1 cent, 4 cents, 9 cents, $\cdots \cdots$, all perfect squares, and there are no other restrictions on how stamps can be affixed.

Prove that for every positive integer $n$, there are the same number of ways to affix $n$ cents worth of stamps on an envelope in Odd Number Country and Square Number Country, where rearranging the stamps is considered the same method.

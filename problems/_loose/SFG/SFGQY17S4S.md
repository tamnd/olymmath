Given a point $P_{0}\left(x_{0}, y_{0}\right)$ and the equation of a line $l$ as $a x+b y=0(a \neq 0)$. Let $\mathrm{Q}_{1}$ be the $\lambda$-symmetric point of $P_{0}$ with respect to the $x$-axis, and draw a line through $Q_{1}$ parallel to $l$, intersecting the $x$-axis at $R_{1}$; let $P_{1}$ be the $\mu$-symmetric point of $Q_{1}$ with respect to $R_{1}$. Similarly, from $P_{1}$, we can construct $Q_{2}, P_{2}$; then $Q_{3}, P_{3}; \cdots$ and so on. If the point $P_{n}\left(x_{n}, y_{n}\right)$, under what conditions do $\left\{x_{n}\right\}$ and $\left\{y_{n}\right\}$ converge? What are their limit values when they converge?

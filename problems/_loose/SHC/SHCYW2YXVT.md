Let $a>1$ be a given natural number. The sequence $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{n}, \cdots$ satisfies: $a_{1}=1, a_{2}=a, a_{n+2}=a a_{n+1}-a_{n}$, for $n \geqslant 1$. Prove: There exist infinitely many primes, such that the first of them are divisors of some term in the sequence $\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n \geqslant 1}$.

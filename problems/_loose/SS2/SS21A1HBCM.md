Seven students are each to draw four different lines on their drawing sheet. The first student should draw the lines so that no intersection point occurs, the second so that exactly one intersection point occurs, the third so that exactly 2 intersection points, the fourth so that exactly 3 intersection points, the fifth so that exactly 4 intersection points, the sixth so that exactly 5 intersection points, and the seventh so that exactly 6 intersection points occur. Intersection points that lie outside the drawing sheet are also counted.

After some time, the second, third, and sixth students claim that their task is not solvable.

Determine which of the three students are correct and which are not, and prove your determination!

Through every point $M$ of the plane, two hyperbolas can be drawn, whose equations, referred to the rectangular coordinate axes $O x, O y$, are

$$
a^{2} x y+a y+x=0
$$

where $a$ is a variable parameter; on which curve $(C)$ must the point $M$ lie so that the hyperbolas intersect at right angles at this point?

How many points does the curve $(C)$ intersect the hyperbolas with the equation $a^{2} x y+a y+x=0$. Only those points should be considered which are neither at infinity nor coincide with the origin of the coordinate system.

What relationship must hold between $a$ and $b$ so that the following two hyperbolas, whose equations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
& a^{2} x y+a y+x=0 \\
& b^{2} x y+b y+x=0
\end{aligned}
$$

intersect at right angles at a point different from the origin of the coordinate system. This relationship defines a curve $(C')$; the curve must be constructed. How many finite and non-origin points does the previously defined curve $(C)$ intersect the curve $(C')$?

(Licentiatus stipendium competition written paper, 1894.)

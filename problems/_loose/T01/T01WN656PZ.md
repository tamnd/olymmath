The natural numbers $n$ and $z$ are relatively prime and greater than $1$ . For $k = 0, 1, 2,..., n - 1$ let $s(k) = 1 + z + z^2 + ...+ z^k.$ Prove that:
a) At least one of the numbers $s(k)$ is divisible by $n$ .
b) If $n$ and $z - 1$ are also coprime, then already one of the numbers $s(k)$ with $k = 0,1, 2,..., n- 2$ is divisible by $n$ .

Does there exist 64 disks $C_{2}, C_{3}, \cdots, C_{65}$ (disks include the circumference and the interior) in the plane satisfying the following conditions:
(1) For $2 \leqslant x<y \leqslant 65(x, y \in \mathbf{Z})$, if $x \mid y$, then $C_{x}$ is contained in $C_{y}$;
(2) For $2 \leqslant a, b \leqslant 65$, if $(a, b)=1$, then $C_{a}$ and $C_{b}$ have no common points?

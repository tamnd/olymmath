Let $T$ be an arbitrary set.

(a) Suppose that for each $t \in T$ a probability space $\left(\Omega_{t}, \mathscr{F}_{t}, \mathrm{P}_{t}\right)$ is given. Let

$$
\Omega=\prod_{t \in T} \Omega_{t}, \mathscr{F}=\otimes_{t \in T} \mathscr{F}_{t}
$$

Then

on $(\Omega, \mathscr{F})$ there exists a unique probability measure $\mathrm{P}$ such that

$$
\mathrm{P}\left(\prod_{t \in T} B_{t}\right)=\prod_{t \in T} \mathrm{P}_{t}\left(B_{t}\right)
$$

where $B_{t} \in \mathscr{F}_{t}, t \in T$, and $B_{t}=\Omega_{t}$ for all indices $t \in T$, except possibly for a finite number of them.

Prove this statement for an uncountable set $T$, using the analogous result for a countable $T$ (see Corollary 4 in V1.II.9).

(b) Suppose that for each $t \in T$ a measurable space $\left(E_{t}, \mathscr{E}_{t}\right)$ and a probability measure $\mathrm{P}_{t}$ on it are given. Prove the following Lomnicki-Ulam theorem:

there exists a probability space $(\Omega, \mathscr{F}, \mathrm{P})$ and independent random elements $\left(X_{t}\right)_{t \in T}$ such that $X_{t}$ are $\mathscr{F} / \mathscr{E}_{t}$-measurable and

$$
\mathrm{P}\left(X_{t} \in B\right)=\mathrm{P}_{t}(B), B \in \mathscr{E}_{t}
$$

A well-known pilot told a story: "In one of my take-offs, I started heading north, then after flying $10000 \mathrm{~km}$, I flew east for the same $10000 \mathrm{~km}$. Finally, I turned north again and after another $10000 \mathrm{~km}$ flight, I landed at my starting point."

A colleague replied: "Your story is surprising, yet I believe it. In fact, if you answer my question, I will calculate the latitude from which you took off, and you will calculate the starting latitude of my somewhat more believable story. - Once, I also returned to where I took off after a $30000 \mathrm{~km}$ flight. In each of the 5 consecutive segments of my journey, I flew $6000 \mathrm{~km}$. I started heading east, then turned north, east, south, and finally east again. About $8 \%$ of my journey was already flown over. So, did you fly over already traveled paths during the third part of your journey, or the half of it? Or perhaps even more than that?"

The first one replied: "The half of it."

Let's calculate the geographical latitude of the take-off point of both pilots with an accuracy of $0.1^{\circ}$.

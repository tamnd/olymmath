Does there exist 100 pairwise disjoint subsets of the set of positive integers $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{100}$ such that: for any set $S$ consisting of infinitely many primes, there exists a positive integer $m$ and
$$
a_{1} \in A_{1}, a_{2} \in A_{2}, \cdots, a_{100} \in A_{100},
$$

such that $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{100}$ can all be expressed as the product of $m$ distinct primes from $S$?
(Townhai High School, provided)

Given $n$ real numbers on a circle $(n \geqslant 2)$, assume their sum is 0 and one of the numbers is 1.
(1) Prove that there exist two adjacent numbers, the difference between which is not less than $\frac{4}{n}$;
(2) Prove that there exists a number such that the difference between its two adjacent numbers' arithmetic mean and itself is not less than $\frac{8}{n^{2}}$.
(3) For any $n$, improve the estimate made in (2), i.e., replace the 8 in (2) with a larger number.
(4) When $n=30$, prove that there exists a number on the circle such that the difference between its two adjacent numbers' arithmetic mean and itself is not less than $\frac{2}{113}$. Also, provide an example of 30 real numbers on the circle such that the difference between any number and the arithmetic mean of its two adjacent numbers is not greater than $\frac{2}{113}$.

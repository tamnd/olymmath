Color all the squares of a $3 \times 7$ "chessboard" - in any distribution - either blue or yellow. Then let's look at all the $m \times n$ rectangular parts of this "chessboard", where $2 \leq m \leq 3$ and $2 \leq n \leq 7$. Prove that among these "sub-boards", there is always at least one whose four corners are the same color - no matter how we color our "chessboard".

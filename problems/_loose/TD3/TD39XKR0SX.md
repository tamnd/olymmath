Statement. Let $n$ be a strictly positive integer that is not a perfect square (i.e., it is not the square of an integer). By looking at the decimal representation of $\sqrt{n}$, we observe that the first $k$ decimal places all have the same value $v$ (for example, if $n=48273160$, in $\sqrt{n}=$ $6947.88888800044067 \ldots$, the first 6 decimal places are equal to 8, so $k=6$ and $v=8$).

(a) If $\mathrm{v}=0$ or $\mathrm{v}=9$, prove that $\sqrt{n}>\frac{10^{k}}{2}$.

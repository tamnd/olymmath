Divide the set $S$ into pairwise disjoint subsets $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{n}$, and also into pairwise disjoint subsets $B_{1}, B_{2}, \cdots, B_{n}$. It is known that the union of any two disjoint subsets $A_{i}$ and $B_{j}$, $A_{i} \cup B_{j}$, contains at least $n$ elements, $1 \leqslant i, j \leqslant n$. Prove that the number of elements in $S$ is at least $\frac{n^{2}}{2}$. Can it equal $\frac{n^{2}}{2}$?

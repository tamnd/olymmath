In medieval Florence, fraud in the market was severely punished. A merchant went to the market with 200 gold, and bought 500 chickens for it. (So, 5 chickens for 2 gold.) He divided the chickens into two equal baskets, then from one basket, he sold 3 chickens for 1 gold, and from the other, the prettier ones, 2 chickens for 1 gold. He claimed that he did not raise the prices, since he sold 5 chickens for 2 gold. However, after selling 240 chickens from the first basket for 80 gold, and 240 from the second basket for 120 gold, he got back his 200 gold, and still had 10 + 10 chickens left. Where is the fraud?

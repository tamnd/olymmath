From the sequence of positive integers $1,2,3, \cdots$, delete all terms that are divisible by 4 and all terms that leave a remainder of 1 when divided by 4. Arrange the remaining numbers in ascending order to form the sequence $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3} \cdots$. Then, from the sequence $\left\{a_{n}\right\}$, delete all terms whose indices are divisible by 4 and all terms whose indices leave a remainder of 1 when divided by 4. Arrange the remaining numbers in ascending order to form the sequence $b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3} \cdots$. Prove that every odd square number greater than 1 is the sum of two consecutive terms in the sequence $\left\{b_{n}\right\}$.

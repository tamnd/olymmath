\section*{Task 2 - 221012}

In a discussion about irrational numbers, it was mentioned that $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{5}$ are irrational.

Peter said: "Then $\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{5}$ must also be irrational." "How do you prove that?" asked Katrin. "There is no theorem stating that $x+y$ must be irrational if $x$ and $y$ are irrational." "Yes, but specifically for the irrational numbers $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{5}$, I can prove that their sum $\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{5}$ is also irrational," replied Peter.

a) Confirm Katrin's opinion with an example that there are irrational numbers $x, y$ with a rational sum $x+y$!

b) How could Peter conduct the proof he announced?

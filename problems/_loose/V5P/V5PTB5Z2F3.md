If we draw several secants from a point $P$ to a circle $k$, then the segments determined by their intersection points $Q, Q^{\prime}, R, R^{\prime} \ldots$ satisfy $P Q \cdot P Q^{\prime}=P R \cdot P R^{\prime}=\ldots$ - a product that is independent of the secant and depends only on the point $P$ and the circle $k$. This product is called the power of the point $P$ with respect to the circle $k$.

Prove the following two statements:

a) The power of any point on the line connecting the intersection points of two intersecting circles with respect to both circles is the same,

b) Any point whose power with respect to two circles is the same lies on the line connecting the intersection points of the two circles - the so-called power line of the two circles.

$n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}$ is called a partition of $n$, $(n_{1}, n_{2}, \cdots, n_{k})$ if the order does not matter, it is called an unordered partition of $n$. An unordered partition can be represented by $n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}, n_{1} \geqslant n_{2} \geqslant \cdots \geqslant n_{k}$. Prove that the number of unordered partitions of $n$ into $k$ parts is equal to the number of unordered partitions of $n$ with the largest part $n_{1}=k$.

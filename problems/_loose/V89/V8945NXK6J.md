Let $P$ be a polynomial with real coefficients such that $P(x) \geqslant 0$ for all real $x$. Show that there exist two polynomials $Q, R \in \mathbb{R}[X]$ such that $P=Q^{2}+R^{2}$.

Irreducible Polynomials with Integer or Rational Coefficients In the case of $\mathbb{Q}[X]$, there is no satisfactory characterization of irreducible polynomials (essentially because arithmetic properties of $\mathbb{Z}$ come into play). However, we can provide some methods for finding roots and criteria for irreducibility.

Proposition 3.62. Let $P(x) \in \mathbb{Q}[X]$ and seek its rational roots. By multiplying $P$ by the least common multiple of the denominators of its coefficients, we can assume that $P(x)=a_{n} x^{n}+\cdots+a_{0} \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$. Let $p / q$ be a rational root of $P$. Then $p$ divides $a_{0}$ and $q$ divides $a_{n}$.

Consider a $3 \times 3$ square, in which each cell contains the number 6. A cell is chosen at random, and the number inside it is increased by 2, 3, or 4, depending on how many adjacent cells share a side with it, and one unit is subtracted from the numbers in the adjacent cells. The same operation can also be performed by decreasing the number in the chosen cell by 2, 3, or 4 and increasing by one unit all the numbers in the adjacent cells, according to the model:

| $a$ | $b$ | $c$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $d$ | $e$ | $f$ |
| $g$ | $h$ | $i$ |
| $a-2$ | $b+1$ | $c$ |
| $d+1$ | $e$ | $f$ |
| $g$ | $h$ | $i$ |$\rightarrow$| $a+1$ | $b$ | $c$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $d-3$ | $e+1$ | $f$ |
| $g+1$ | $h$ | $i$ | or | $d-1$ | $e+4$ | $f-1$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $g$ | $h-1$ | $i$ |

All transformations are possible only if they keep natural numbers inside the cells.

a) Find a sequence of transformations that brings the initial square to the form

| 5 | 7 | 4 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 4 | 11 | 7 |
| 5 | 7 | 4 |

b) Prove that the initial square cannot be brought to the form

| 5 | 5 | 5 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 5 | 15 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 | 5 |

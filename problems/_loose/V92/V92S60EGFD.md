Let $n \in \mathbf{Z}_{+}$. If a permutation $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{n}$ of $1,2, \cdots, n$ satisfies that for each positive integer $i(1 \leqslant i \leqslant n-1)$, $a_{i} a_{i+1}+1$ is a perfect square (cube) number, then this permutation is called "square (cube)". Prove:
(1) There are infinitely many positive integers $n$, such that $1,2, \cdots, n$ has at least one square permutation;
(2) There does not exist a positive integer $n$, such that $1,2, \cdots, n$ has a cube permutation. ${ }^{[3]}$

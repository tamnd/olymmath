It is assumed that over $n$ points in space, no four of which lie in a common plane, every tetrahedron formed by four of these $n$ points has a volume not greater than 1.

Prove from this assumption that there then exists a tetrahedron in space with a volume not greater than 27 that contains all $n$ points either in its interior or on its boundary.

Note:

In this problem, for simplicity, units of measurement were omitted when specifying volumes. Proceed similarly in the solution.

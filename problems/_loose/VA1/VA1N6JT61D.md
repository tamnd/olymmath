On a plane, there is an oriented line $t$ and an oriented circle $O$. Through a point $M$ on the line $t$, two tangents $t_{1}$ and $t_{2}$ are drawn to the circle, directed in accordance with the orientation of the circle. Prove that the product

$$
\operatorname{tg} \frac{\alpha}{2} \cdot \operatorname{tg}-\frac{\beta}{2}
$$

does not depend on the position of the point $M$ on the line $t$ ( $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the oriented angles formed by the lines $t_{1}$ and $t_{2}$ with the line $t$ ). Express the obtained constant in terms of the radius of the circle and the distance from the line to the center.

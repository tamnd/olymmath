a) Given a natural number $n \geqslant 2$. Prove that a natural number $d$, for which $x^{d+1} \equiv x(\bmod n)$ for all integers $x$, exists if and only if $n=p_{1} \ldots p_{k}$, where $p_{1}, \ldots, p_{k}$ are pairwise distinct prime numbers.
b) Let $n=p_{1} \ldots p_{k}$, where $p_{1}, \ldots, p_{k}$ are pairwise distinct prime numbers. Prove that the smallest natural number $d$, for which $x^{d+1} \equiv x(\bmod n)$ for all integers $x$, is $\operatorname{LCM}\left(p_{1}-1, \ldots, p_{k}-1\right)$.

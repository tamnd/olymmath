The Case of the Four.

This time, four suspects in a robbery were called for interrogation: A, B, C, and D. Indisputable evidence has proven that at least one of them is guilty and that no one other than A, B, C, and D was involved in the robbery. Additionally, the following facts were established:

1) A is undoubtedly not guilty.
2) If B is guilty, then he had exactly one accomplice.
3) If C is guilty, then he had exactly two accomplices.

Inspector Craig was particularly interested in determining whether D was guilty or not, as D was a dangerous criminal. Fortunately, the facts provided above are sufficient to determine the guilt or innocence of suspect D.

So, is D guilty or not?

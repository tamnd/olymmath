Given the set $M=\left\{x_{1}, x_{2}, \cdots, x_{4 n+3}\right\}$, its $4 n+3$ subsets $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{4 n+3}$ have the following properties:
(1) Every $n+1$ elements in $M$ belong to exactly one subset $A_{i}(1 \leqslant i \leqslant 4 n+3)$;
(2) $\left|A_{i}\right| \geqslant 2 n+1(i=1,2, \cdots, 4 n+3)$.
Prove: Any two subsets $A_{i}, A_{j}(1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant 4 n+3)$ have exactly $n$ common elements.

For a positive integer $n$, the fraction $R_{n}=\frac{n(n+3)}{(n+1)(n+2)}$ is called a "component fraction". Some rational numbers can be expressed as the product of one or more (including one) "component fractions", for example, $\frac{2}{3}=\frac{1 \times 4}{2 \times 3}=R_{1}, \frac{3}{5}=\frac{1 \times 4}{2 \times 3} \times \frac{3 \times 6}{4 \times 5}=R_{1} R_{3}$, $\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1 \times 4}{2 \times 3} \times \frac{2 \times 5}{3 \times 4} \times \frac{3 \times 6}{4 \times 5}=R_{1} R_{2} R_{3}$, and so on. Prove that any positive rational number less than 1 can be expressed as the product of several component fractions.

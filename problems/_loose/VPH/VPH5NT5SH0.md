In a math competition, the sum of the scores of $B$ and $D$ is equal to the sum of the scores of $A$ and $C$. If the scores of $B$ and $C$ are swapped, then the sum of the scores of $A$ and $C$ will exceed the sum of the scores of $B$ and $D$. In addition, it is known that the score of $D$ is more than the sum of the scores of $B$ and $C$. Assuming all scores are non-negative, the order from highest to lowest score is
(A) $D, A, C, B$.
(B) $D, A, B, C$.
(C) $D, C, B, A$.
(D) $A, D, C, B$.
(E) $A, D, B, C$.
(38th American High School Mathematics Examination, 1987)

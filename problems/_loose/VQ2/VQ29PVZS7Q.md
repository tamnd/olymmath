Wouldn't it have been better to remain silent?

On a small island, a person was being tried for a crime. The court knew that the defendant was born and raised on a neighboring island of knights and liars. (Let's recall that knights always tell the truth, while liars always lie.) The defendant was allowed to make only one statement in his defense. After some thought, he declared the following: "The person who actually committed the crime of which I am accused is a liar."

Was it wise for him to make such a statement? Did it help him, or did it only worsen his position? Perhaps it had no impact on the court's decision?

Petya inscribed two squares in a right-angled triangle with sides 3, 4, and 5. One vertex of the first square coincides with the vertex of the right angle, and one side of the second square lies on the hypotenuse. Petya found the sides of each square, represented their ratio as an irreducible fraction, and found the sum of the numerator and the denominator of this fraction. What number did Petya get? (Recall that a quadrilateral is inscribed in a triangle if all its vertices are on the sides or at the vertices of the triangle.)

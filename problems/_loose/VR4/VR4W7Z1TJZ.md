Let $n$ be a positive integer, and $u_{1}, u_{2}, \cdots, u_{n}$ be positive integers not greater than $2^{k}\left(k \geqslant 3, k \in \mathbf{Z}_{+}\right)$. Define a "representation" of a non-negative integer $t$ as a sequence of non-negative integers $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{n}$ such that $t = a_{1} u_{1} + a_{2} u_{2} + \cdots + a_{n} u_{n}$. Prove: If a non-negative integer $t$ has a representation, then there exists a representation of $t$ such that the number of non-zero elements in $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{n}$ is less than $k+1$.

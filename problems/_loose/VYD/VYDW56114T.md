Let $X$ be a non-empty finite set, and $A_{1}$, $A_{2}, \cdots, A_{k}$ be $k$ subsets of $X$, satisfying the following conditions:
(1) $\left|A_{i}\right| \leqslant 3 \ (i=1,2, \cdots, k)$;
(2) Any element in $X$ belongs to at least six of the sets $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{k}$.

Prove: If $m \geqslant \frac{k}{3}$, then it is possible to select $m$ sets from $A_{1}, A_{2}, \cdots, A_{k}$ such that their union is $X$.

(1) Prove: For any $n \equiv 1(\bmod 3)$, the queen bee cannot have a perfect laying process.
(2) When $n=6$, can the queen bee have a perfect laying process? If not, provide the reason; if so, provide a perfect laying process scheme.

In a hexagonal grid (bee brood area) with side length $n$, the hexagon is divided into $6 n^{2}$ equilateral triangles with side length 1. Define a "perfect laying process": a queen bee starts from the center of the hexagon, each time moving along the boundary of the triangles to a vertex, laying an egg at that vertex. The queen bee cannot revisit a vertex where an egg has already been laid, and after laying an egg at a vertex, the queen bee can only continue straight or turn $120^{\circ}$ to the left or right, and must end at a vertex on the boundary of the hexagonal grid (bee brood area) so that the queen bee can leave the brood area smoothly.

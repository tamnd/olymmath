a) Prove that among all convex figures Ф bounded by a line segment of length $a$ and an arc of length $l (l > a)$, the one with the largest area is a circular segment.

b) Prove that among all convex figures Ф bounded by a line segment of arbitrary length and an arc of length $l$, the one with the largest area is a semicircle of radius $\frac{l}{\pi}$.

Problem 65b) (or rather, a slightly more general version of it) is known as the "Dido's Problem." Dido is a mythological Phoenician queen, the founder of the ancient city of Carthage in Africa. According to legend, Dido made a deal with the tribe inhabiting the northern coast of Africa, that she would be given a piece of land "within the limits of an ox hide." However, Dido did not simply cover a small piece of land with the hide, as the landowners intended, but acted more cleverly. She cut the hide into thin strips and tied them together to form one long strip. After that, Dido faced the problem of enclosing the largest possible area with this strip. The question that Dido faced did not simply reduce to an isoperimetric problem, as she could use the seashore and, by enclosing a piece of land adjacent to the sea, obtain a larger territory than if she had chosen a piece of land far from the sea. If we consider the seashore to be straight, we arrive precisely at problem 65b).

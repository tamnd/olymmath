Prove that there exists a projective mapping that transforms three given points on one line into three given points on another line.

Definition. The double ratio of a quadruple of points $A, B, C, D$, lying on the same line, is the number

$$
(A B C D)=\frac{c-a}{c-b}: \frac{d-a}{d-b}
$$

where $a, b, c, d$ are the coordinates of points $A, B, C, D$ respectively. It is easy to verify that the double ratio does not depend on the choice of coordinates on the line. We will also write

$$
(A B C D)=\frac{A C}{B C}: \frac{A D}{B D}
$$

assuming that $A C / B C$ (respectively $A D / B D$) denotes the ratio of the lengths of these segments if the vectors $\overrightarrow{A C}$ and $\overrightarrow{B C}$ (respectively $\overrightarrow{A D}$ and $\overrightarrow{B D}$) are in the same direction, or the ratio of the lengths of the segments, taken with a negative sign, if these vectors are oppositely directed.

Definition. The double ratio of a quadruple of lines $a, b, c, d$, passing through one point, is the number

$$
(a b c d)= \pm \frac{\sin (a, c)}{\sin (b, c)}: \frac{\sin (a, d)}{\sin (b, d)}
$$

the sign of which is chosen as follows: if one of the angles formed by the lines $a$ and $b$ does not intersect either of the lines $c$ or $d$ (in this case, it is said that the pair of lines $a$ and $b$ does not separate the pair of lines $c$ and $d$), then $(a b c d)>0$; otherwise $(a b c d)<0$.

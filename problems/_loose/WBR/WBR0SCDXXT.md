Given the edges of a tetrahedron, its volume can be calculated, as any of its heights can be determined. (The thought process leading to this can start with rotating the other three faces of the tetrahedron into the plane of one of its faces). Let's perform the calculation in the following example: the edge lengths of the tetrahedron $A B C D$ are as follows: $A B=6, A C=7, A D=8, C D=9$, $B D=10, B C=11$ units. (We can choose the base as, for example, the triangle $A B C$.)

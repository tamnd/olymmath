For some natural numbers $n$, a sequence can be constructed in which each of the numbers $1,2,3, \ldots, n$ appears twice, and the second appearance of each number $r$ occurs $r$ places after its first appearance. For example, for $n=4$

$$
4,2,3,2,4,3,1,1
$$

For $n=5$ we have

$$
3,5,2,3,2,4,5,1,1,4
$$

Such an arrangement is impossible for $n=6$ or 7. For $n=8$ we have

$$
8,6,4,2,7,2,4,6,8,3,5,7,3,1,1,5
$$

Prove that such a sequence does not exist if the remainder of the number $n$ when divided by 4 is 2 or 3.

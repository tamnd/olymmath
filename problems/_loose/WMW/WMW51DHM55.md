A flock of 64 sparrows is perched on trees. Then, through a series of flights, the flock changes its arrangement on the trees. If at least half of all the birds end up on one tree, then from it to each of the other trees, exactly as many sparrows fly as are already sitting on that tree (and this counts as one flight). If at some point there are 32 sparrows on two trees, then they fly from one of the trees to the others according to the same rule. Initially, the sparrows were distributed among the trees in such a way that the flock had to make 6 flights, and after that, the flock settled down. Prove that all the sparrows gathered on one tree.

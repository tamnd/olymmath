In a specialist camp for young mathematicians, Dirk presents a puzzle. He places five vessels in a row on a table - from left to right for the audience: a bottle, a jug, a cup, a glass, and a pot. They are, not necessarily in this order, filled with one of the drinks: tea, coffee, milk, lemonade, and cider. The audience does not know which vessel contains which liquid.

Dirk says:

"If I place the pot - while leaving the other vessels unchanged - between two of the other vessels, such that immediately to its left is the vessel with tea and immediately to its right is the vessel with milk, then the milk vessel and the lemonade vessel stand immediately next to each other, and additionally, the vessel with coffee stands as the middle one in the row of five vessels.

Now find out what each of the vessels contains!"

Determine whether it can be deduced from these statements alone which drinks are in each of the five vessels.

Give all possibilities for the distribution of the drinks in the vessels that are consistent with Dirk's statements!

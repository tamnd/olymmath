(2003 IMO Slovenia National Team Selection Test) Let $S$ be a finite non-empty set of positive integers greater than 1, and suppose it has the following property: there exists a number $s \in S$ such that for any positive integer $n$, either $(s, n)=1$ or $(s, n)=s$. Prove: there must exist two numbers $s, t \in S$ (not necessarily distinct), such that $(s, t)$ is a prime number.

a) A circle is divided into $p$ equal sectors, where $p$ is a prime number. In how many different ways can these $p$ sectors be painted using $n$ colors, if it is allowed to paint several (and even all) sectors with the same color, and two colorings are considered different only if they cannot be made to coincide by rotating the circle?

b) Use the result from part a) to prove the following theorem of Fermat: if $p$ is a prime number, then $n^{p}-n$ is divisible by $p$ for any $n$.

For other proofs of Fermat's theorem, see, for example, the books by D. O. Shklyarsky, N. N. Chentsov, I. M. Yaglom, Selected Problems and Theorems of Elementary Mathematics, part 1 (Library of the Mathematical Circle, issue 1) and E. B. Dynkin and V. A. Uspenskii, Mathematical Conversations (Library of the Mathematical Circle, issue 6).

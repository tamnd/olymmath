Given a non-isosceles $\triangle ABC$ with the incircle $\odot I$ touching sides $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$ at points $D$, $E$, and $F$ respectively. The line through point $E$ perpendicular to $BI$ intersects $\odot I$ again at point $K$, and the line through point $F$ perpendicular to $CI$ intersects $\odot I$ again at point $L$. Let $J$ be the midpoint of segment $KL$. Prove:
(1) Points $D$, $I$, and $J$ are collinear;
(2) If $B$ and $C$ are fixed points, and point $A$ satisfies $\frac{AB}{AC} = k$ (where $k$ is a given positive constant), and points $A$, $B$, and $C$ are not collinear, the lines $IE$ and $IF$ intersect $\odot I$ again at points $M$ and $N$ respectively, and the line $MN$ intersects $IB$ and $IC$ at points $P$ and $Q$ respectively, then the perpendicular bisector of segment $PQ$ always passes through a fixed point. ${ }^{[2]}$
(2013, Vietnam Mathematical Olympiad)

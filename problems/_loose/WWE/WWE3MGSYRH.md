Let $O, G, K, S_{1}, S_{2}$ be the circumcenter, centroid, symmedian point, first isogonic center, and second isogonic center of $\triangle ABC$, respectively, and let $R$ be the circumradius. Then
$$
\frac{G S_{1}}{G S_{2}}=\left(\frac{R}{O K}-\sqrt{\frac{R^{2}}{O K^{2}}-1}\right)^{3} .
$$

It is well known that the distance formulas between special points of a triangle are quite complex, and this equation involves the distance relationships between several special points of a triangle, which is very elegant and quite challenging. After the problem was proposed, no public proof has been seen. After careful consideration, the author obtained a complex proof and derived some geometric equations involving the distances between special points of a triangle (these equations will be presented as conclusions in the text). Now, the proof is organized into an article, and it is hoped that readers who are interested can provide better proofs.

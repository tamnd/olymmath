In Daniel Defoe's novel "Robinson Crusoe," upon finding money in one of the chests from the sunken ship, Robinson Crusoe exclaimed: "Worthless trash, what good are you to me now? I would gladly give away a whole pile of gold for any of these cheap knives. I have no use for you. So off to the bottom of the sea with you." Why did Robinson Crusoe speak so disparagingly about money, and what property did money lose in the novel? What other properties should an object have to become money?

On a $10 \times 10$ checkerboard, there is a bacterium in one of the cells. In one move, the bacterium moves to an adjacent cell and splits into two (both remain in the same cell). Then, one of the bacteria sitting on the board moves to an adjacent cell and splits into two, and so on. Can it happen that after several such moves, there are an equal number of bacteria in all cells?

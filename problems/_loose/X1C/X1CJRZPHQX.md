Let there be $2 n$ integers $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{2 n}$, and from these, any one number can be taken out, and the remaining $2 n-1$ numbers can always be divided into two disjoint subsets, such that the sum of the numbers in each subset is equal. Then $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{2 n}$ must all be zero. (Here, "two disjoint subsets" means that for any remaining $a_{i}$, it belongs to exactly one subset).

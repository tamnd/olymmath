Let $X=\left(X_{t}\right)$ be a Borel function on $[0, T]$. Considering $t$ as time, $X$ as the observed process, and $[0, T]$ as the observation period for $X$, P. Lévy proposed the following formula for calculating the time $l_{X}(x, T)$ spent by the process $X$ at level $x \in \mathbb{R}$ over the time interval $[0, T]$:

$$
\lim _{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \frac{1}{2 \varepsilon} \int_{0}^{T} I\left(x-\varepsilon<X_{t}<x+\varepsilon\right) d t
$$

This is known as the local time. Prove that the limit defining $l_{X}(x, T)$ is defined and finite for almost all $x$ (with respect to Lebesgue measure).

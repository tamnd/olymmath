After time $\tau$, the bottom end of the ladder will move away from the corner by a distance $x=a \tau^{2} / 2$ and acquire a speed $v_{1}=a \tau$. At this point, the ladder will be inclined at an angle $\alpha$ to the vertical: $\sin \alpha=a \tau^{2} /(2 l)$. Since the length of the ladder remains constant, the projections of the velocities of its ends onto the direction of the ladder are the same. Denoting the speed of the upper end by $v_{2}$, we get $v_{1} \sin \alpha=v_{2} \cos \alpha$, from which

$$
v_{2}=v_{1} \operatorname{tg} \alpha=\frac{a^{2} \tau^{3}}{\sqrt{4 l^{2}-a^{2} \tau^{4}}}
$$

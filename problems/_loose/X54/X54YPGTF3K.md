The ninety-ninth problem: We call a positive integer $t$ a "good number" if there exist positive integers $x, y$ such that $t=x^{3}+y^{2}$. Prove that for any positive integer $n \geq 2$, there exist infinitely many positive integers $m$ such that the set $\left\{m+1, m+2, \ldots, m+n^{2}\right\}$ contains exactly $\mathrm{n}+1$ good numbers.

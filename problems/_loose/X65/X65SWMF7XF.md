Distinct natural numbers $a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}, \cdots, a_{n}, \cdots$, all of which can be divided by prime numbers no greater than 3, let $S_{n}=\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{a_{n}}$, then the correct conclusion among the following is
(A) $S_{n}$ is a strictly increasing function of $n$, and $S_{n}$ has no upper bound
(B) There exists a natural number $M$, for all $n \in \mathrm{N}$, such that $3 \leqslant S_{n} \leqslant M$
(C) For any $n \in \mathrm{N}$, $S_{n}<3$
(D) There exists $T \in \mathrm{N}$, such that for all $n \in \mathrm{N}$ and $a_{1}, a_{2}, \cdots, a_{n}$, $S_{n} \geqslant T \geqslant 3$

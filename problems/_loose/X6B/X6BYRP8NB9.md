If two adjacent positive odd numbers are both prime numbers, then these two odd numbers are called twin primes. For example, 5 and 7, 11 and 13, 29 and 31, etc., are all twin primes. Given that $p-2$ and $p$ are twin primes, and $k=\left[\frac{2 p-1}{3}\right]$, where $[x]$ represents the greatest integer not exceeding $x$. Prove that $S=1+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{C}_{p-1}^{1}+\frac{1}{3} \mathrm{C}_{p-1}^{2}+$ $\frac{1}{4} \mathrm{C}_{p-1}^{3}+\cdots+\frac{1}{k} \mathrm{C}_{p-1}^{k-1}$ is an integer and is divisible by $p$.

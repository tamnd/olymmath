A bus-train round trip through $n$ cities is a journey that starts in one of these cities, reaches each of the others exactly once, then returns to the starting point, and uses no other means of transportation except bus or train.

Given $n$ cities $S_{1}, \ldots, S_{n}$, it is assumed that between any two of them there is exactly one (bidirectional) connection, and that this is either a bus or a train connection.

Prove for every natural number $n \geq 3$ that there must always be a bus-train round trip through $n$ cities that meet these conditions, where the means of transportation is changed at most once.

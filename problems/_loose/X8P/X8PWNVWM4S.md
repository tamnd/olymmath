Consider a 100-digit number consisting only of the digits 1 and 2. You are allowed to pick any 10 consecutive digits and swap the positions of the first 5 and the last 5 digits. If one 100-digit number can be obtained from another by performing this operation several times, then these two numbers are called congruent. How many 100-digit numbers, consisting only of the digits 1 and 2, can be selected such that no two of them are congruent?

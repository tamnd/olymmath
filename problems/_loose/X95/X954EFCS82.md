$$
\begin{array}{l}
BC: x \cos \theta_{1} + y \sin \theta_{1} - p_{1} = 0, \\
CA: x \cos \theta_{2} + y \sin \theta_{2} - p_{2} = 0, \\
AB: x \cos \theta_{3} + y \sin \theta_{3} - p_{3} = 0.
\end{array}
$$
(1) Prove that the conic section
$$
\begin{array}{l}
a\left(x \cos \theta_{2} + y \sin \theta_{2} - p_{2}\right)\left(x \cos \theta_{3} + y \sin \theta_{3} - p_{3}\right) + b\left(x \cos \theta_{3} \right. \\
\left. + y \sin \theta_{3} - p_{3}\right)\left(x \cos \theta_{1} + y \sin \theta_{1} - p_{1}\right) + c\left(x \cos \theta_{1} + y \sin \theta_{1} \right. \\
\left. - p_{1}\right)\left(x \cos \theta_{2} + y \sin \theta_{2} - p_{2}\right) = 0
\end{array}
$$

must pass through points $A$, $B$, and $C$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are arbitrary constants.
(2) Write the necessary and sufficient condition for this conic section to be a circle, determine the ratio $a : b : c$, and write the equation of the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$.
(3) Based on the geometric meaning of the circumcircle equation of $\triangle ABC$, prove the following theorem: From any point $P$ on the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$, draw perpendiculars to the sides $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$. Then the feet of these perpendiculars $D$, $E$, and $F$ lie on a straight line.

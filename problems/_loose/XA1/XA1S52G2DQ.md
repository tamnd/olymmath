Given $2^{n-1}$ different sequences, each consisting of 0s and 1s, with a length of $n$. It is known that for any 3 sequences, there exists a natural number $m$, such that the $m$-th term of the 3 sequences is 1. Prove that there exists a natural number $k$, such that the $k$-th term of all $2^{n-1}$ sequences is 1, and $k$ is unique.

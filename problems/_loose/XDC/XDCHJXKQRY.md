The three friends José, Pedro, and Daniel are playing a game with eight cards numbered from 2 to 9. In each round, the eight cards are distributed among the three friends, not necessarily in equal quantities, and each one calculates the product of the numbers on their cards. The one with the highest product wins the round. If two have equal and higher results than the third, then the two win. After several rounds, Pedro suspects that one of the three friends will always have a product of at least 72. Unfortunately, Pedro does not know how to prove that this always happens. Let's help him?

(a) Show that if one of the friends takes 4 or more cards, then the product of their cards will certainly be greater than 72.

(b) In one round, Daniel drew three cards, one of which is 9. Knowing that the product of these numbers is less than 72, what are the other two cards of Daniel?

(c) In the same round as the previous item, show that one of the other friends, José or Pedro, will have three or more cards and their product will be greater than 72.

(d) In another round, Daniel took two cards, one of which is 9, but the product is again less than 72. Show that the product of the remaining 6 cards is greater than $72^2$ and conclude that one of the two friends, José or Pedro, has a product of the cards greater than 72.

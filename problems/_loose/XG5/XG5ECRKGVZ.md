On Sunday, as usual, Monsieur Dupont went for a walk. Meanwhile, his wife was trying in vain to decide whether he would remember to buy a pie and whether she would have to go to the bakery herself. In the end, she asked her son: "Do you know if your father intended to buy a pie?"

However, the child, predicting his father's behavior, is wrong one time out of three. Moreover, Madame Dupont believes that if the child is completely deprived of sweets, it will be twice as bad as if he gets two pies at once. How would you advise Madame Dupont to act: go and buy a pie herself regardless of what the son says, or go for the pie only if the boy says that his father did not intend to buy it?

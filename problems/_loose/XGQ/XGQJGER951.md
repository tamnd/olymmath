The second problem of the second round of the Rákosi Mátyás competition read as follows: "On a railway line, a passenger train departs from location $A$ to location $C$. When the train passes through $B$, a freight train departs from $B$ towards $A$. When the freight train arrives at $A$, a fast train departs from $A$ towards $C$, and $\mathrm{s}$ it catches up with the previously mentioned passenger train exactly at the $C$ station. Halfway between $A$ and $B$, a student observes that 15 minutes after the passenger train passes, the freight train passes, and 11 minutes later, the fast train. $B$ is 10 km from $A$. The question is, how many kilometers is $C$ from $B$? (We assume that the trains travel at a constant speed)."

The solution to this problem can be found on page 10 of issue No. 10.

In connection with this problem, we raise the following question: What are the limits within which the hourly speeds of the fast, passenger, and freight trains vary, if we limit the maximum speed of the fast train to 150 km/h and stipulate that the speed of the freight train cannot be greater than the speed of the passenger train?

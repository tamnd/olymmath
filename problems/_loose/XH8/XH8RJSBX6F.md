Numbers $x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{n}$, each equal to 1 or -1, are written around a circle, and the sum of the products of adjacent numbers is zero, and generally for each $k=1,2, \ldots, n-1$ the sum of $n$ products of numbers that are $k$ places apart is zero

(that is, $x_{1} x_{2}+x_{2} x_{3}+\ldots+x_{n} x_{1}=0, x_{1} x_{3}+x_{2} x_{4}+\ldots+x_{n} x_{2}=0, x_{1} x_{4}+x_{2} x_{5}+\ldots+x_{n} x_{3}=0$ and so on; for example, for $n=4$ one of the numbers can be -1, and the other three can be 1).

a) Prove that $n$ is a square of an integer.

b)* Does such a set of numbers exist for $n=16$?

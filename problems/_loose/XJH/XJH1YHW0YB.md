Retail purchases at wholesale prices are called group buying when people cooperate to buy goods from suppliers without a markup.

The practice of group buying became popular in Russia in the mid-2000s and is still used today. Buyers unite on specialized websites or social networks. In each purchase from suppliers, the terms of acquiring goods are specified. For example, delivery times, minimum order amounts, and conditions for returning defective goods are indicated. Those who act as organizers of the purchases negotiate with suppliers, place orders, pay for and sort the goods, and then send the goods by mail or deliver them to a pickup point, where they are received by the customers participating in the group buying. Organizers receive a certain percentage for their services and, based on their experience working with a specific supplier, always inform customers about the risk of receiving goods of the wrong color or size, and as a rule, they do not bear responsibility for the risk of cargo loss in transit, confiscation at customs, etc.

(a) Why, despite the described risks, is the practice of group buying popular in many countries? Explain.

(b) Why is such a practice of group buying not popular, for example, among residents of one building, entrance, or floor when ordering groceries and household items from supermarkets, even though in this case buyers could significantly save on delivery costs? Explain.

Miklós and Péter play the following game of chance: Miklós places 6 identical pieces of string in a box so that the ends of the strings protrude from both sides of the box. Péter ties 3 pairs of the 6 ends protruding from the same side of the box. If, after opening the box, the 6 pieces of string form a single continuous loop, then Péter wins; otherwise, Miklós wins. Which of them has a greater chance of winning? - (How many different ways are there to tie the ends together if we mark the strings, and how many of these are favorable to M. and how many to P.?) - Does our conclusion change if 5 or 7 pieces of string are used instead of 6 (in which case, of course, 1 end will remain unpaired)?

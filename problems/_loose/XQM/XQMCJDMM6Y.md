Prove that for any tetrahedron, there exists a triangle whose side lengths are equal to the products of the lengths of opposite edges of the tetrahedron, and that the area $ S $ of this triangle is equal to $ 6 V R $, where $ V $ is the volume of the tetrahedron, and $ R $ is the radius of its circumscribed sphere (Krein's formula).

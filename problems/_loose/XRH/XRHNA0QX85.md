In a math lesson, the teacher draws exactly ten quadrilaterals on the blackboard and asks the students to make statements about them. He receives the following answers:

Axel: "There are at least two squares on the board."

Beate: "There are exactly twice as many rectangles as squares on the board."

Christa: "There is exactly one parallelogram on the board."

Detlev: "There are exactly twice as many trapezoids as rectangles on the board."

The teacher then informs the class that exactly one of these four statements is false.

a) Who made the false statement?

b) Determine the number of quadrilaterals of each type on the board, to the extent that this number can be derived from the given information!

c) Sketch how the board might have looked based on these statements!

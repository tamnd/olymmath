What is the maximum number of cells that can be crossed

a) by a line segment drawn on a sheet of graph paper of size $m \times n$ cells;

b) by a needle of length 200 (the side of a cell is taken as the unit of length) thrown onto a sheet of graph paper?

[A segment (or "needle") is considered to cross a cell if it passes through its interior point.]

Similarly to the solution of problem 24b), one can also estimate the maximum number of cubes of a spatial ${ }^{1}$ ) cubic grid with side 1 that a "needle" (i.e., a straight line segment) of a given length $L$ can cross. A more complex nature is the estimation of the maximum possible number of squares of a sheet of graph paper (or the number of cubes of a spatial ${ }^{1}$ ) cubic grid) that a line of a given length $L$ can cross (for example, it can be shown that a line of length 200 cannot cross more than 429 cells of a sheet of graph paper, but it can cross 429 cells) - however, this estimate can also be obtained using elementary (school) mathematics (see V. L. Rabinovich [39]).

It is said that a $\sigma$-algebra is countably generated or separable if it is generated by some countable class of subsets.

(a) Show that the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})$ of Borel sets in $\mathbb{R}$ is countably generated.

(b) Provide an example showing that it is possible for two $\sigma$-algebras $\mathscr{F}_{1}$ and $\mathscr{F}_{2}$ to be such that $\mathscr{F}_{1} \subset \mathscr{F}_{2}$, $\mathscr{F}_{2}$ is countably generated, but $\mathscr{F}_{1}$ is not.

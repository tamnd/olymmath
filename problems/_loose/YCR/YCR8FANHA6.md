Prove that if there exists a chain of circles $S_{1}, S_{2}, \ldots, S_{n}$, each of which touches two adjacent ones ( $S_{n}$ touches $S_{n-1}$ and $S_{1}$ ) and two given non-intersecting circles $R_{1}$ and $R_{2}$, then there are infinitely many such chains. Specifically, for any circle $T_{1}$, touching $R_{1}$ and $R_{2}$ (in the same way, if $R_{1}$ and $R_{2}$ do not lie one inside the other, externally and internally in the opposite case), there exists a similar chain of $n$ touching circles $T_{1}, T_{2}, \ldots, T_{n}$ (Steiner's porism).

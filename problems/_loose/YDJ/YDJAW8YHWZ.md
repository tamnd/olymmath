On the blackboard, there are several $0$s, $1$s, and $2$s. You can erase two different numbers and replace them with the number that is different from the erased numbers (for example, replace 0 and 1 with 2, replace 1 and 2 with 0, replace 0 and 2 with 1).

Prove that if after several such operations only one number remains on the blackboard, then this number is independent of the order in which the numbers were erased.
(9th All-Soviet Union Mathematical Olympiad, 1975)

A hundred sages were offered the following trial. They are brought into a hall one by one (in a predetermined order). In the hall, the warden offers the sage a choice of two different numbers from the set $1,2,3$. The sage chooses exactly one of them, reports the chosen number to the warden, and leaves the hall. Before making their choice, the sage has the right to ask the warden which number each of the two previous sages chose (the second sage has the right to know about the first). Any communication between the sages during the trial is prohibited. If at the end the sum of all 100 numbers chosen by the sages equals 200, the sages fail the trial; otherwise, they pass it. Prove that the sages can agree in advance on their actions so as to guarantee passing the trial. (S. Berlov)

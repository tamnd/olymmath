In each field of a square table $2 n \times 2 n (n \in \mathbb{N})$, there is a light bulb that can be turned on or off. In one step, we can choose a row in which at least $n$ light bulbs are on (by "row" we mean any row or column), and change the state of all light bulbs in that row (turn off the ones that are on, and turn on the ones that are off).

(a) Prove that there exists an initial arrangement of light bulbs such that, no matter how we perform the steps, there will always be at least $2 n(n-1)$ light bulbs on.

(b) Prove that it is always possible to achieve, in a finite number of steps, that at most $2 n(n-1)$ light bulbs are on.

(1) Prove: There exists a one-to-one mapping $f: \mathbf{Z}_{+} \times \mathbf{Z}_{+} \rightarrow \mathbf{Z}_{+}$ that satisfies the following conditions:
(i) $f(1,1)=1$;
(ii) For any integer $i>1, f(i, j)(j=1,2, \cdots)$ has a common divisor greater than 1;
(iii) For any integer $j>1, f(i, j)(i=1,2, \cdots)$ has a common divisor greater than 1.
(2) Does there exist a one-to-one mapping $f: \mathbf{Z}_{+} \times \mathbf{Z}_{+} \rightarrow \mathbf{Z}_{+} \backslash\{1\}$ that satisfies the following conditions:
(i) For any positive integer $i, f(i, j)(j=1,2, \cdots)$ has a common divisor greater than 1;
(ii) For any positive integer $j, f(i, j)(i=1,2, \cdots)$ has a common divisor greater than 1?

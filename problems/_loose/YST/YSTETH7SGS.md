For the heights of a tetrahedron to intersect at one point (such a tetrahedron is called orthocentric), it is necessary and sufficient that:

a) the opposite edges of the tetrahedron are perpendicular

b) one height of the tetrahedron passes through the point of intersection of the heights of the base;

c) the sums of the squares of the opposite edges are equal;

d) the segments connecting the midpoints of the opposite edges are equal to each other;

e) the products of the cosines of the opposite dihedral angles are equal;

f) the angles between the opposite edges are equal.

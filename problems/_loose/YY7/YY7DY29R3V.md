a) Points $A_{0}, A_{1}$, and $A_{2}$ lie at the vertices of an equilateral triangle inscribed in circle $O$. An arbitrary point $M$, lying on circle $O$, is connected by segments to points $A_{0}, A_{1}$, and $A_{2}$. Prove that one of these segments is equal to the sum of the other two.

b) Points $A_{0}, A_{1}$, and $A_{2}$ lie at the vertices of an equilateral triangle inscribed in circle $O$. A second circle is drawn tangent to the given circle $O$, and from each point $A_{0}, A_{1}$, and $A_{2}$, a tangent is drawn to this second circle. Prove that the length of one of these tangents is equal to the sum of the lengths of the other two.

c) Three equal circles with centers at $A_{0}$, $A_{1}$, and $A_{2}$ form an equilateral triangle and are tangent to a fourth circle $O$. From an arbitrary point $N$ on circle $O$, a tangent is drawn to each of the three equal circles. Prove that the length of one of these tangents is equal to the sum of the lengths of the other two tangents.

(In problems b) and c), in the case of internal tangency of the circles, it is assumed that circle $O$ has a larger radius-otherwise, it is impossible to draw the tangents mentioned in the problem.)

Let $n$ be a natural number. On a lake, there are $2 n$ lily pads marked with the numbers $1,2, \ldots, 2 n$. Frogs jump from lily pad to lily pad such that each frog starts from the lily pad marked with the number 1, at each jump it jumps to a lily pad with a higher number than the one it is currently on, and ends on the lily pad marked with the number $2 n$. Determine the smallest number of frogs for which it is possible that for every pair $(i, j)$ such that $1 \leqslant i<j \leqslant 2 n$, there is a frog that has jumped directly from the lily pad marked with $i$ to the lily pad marked with $j$.

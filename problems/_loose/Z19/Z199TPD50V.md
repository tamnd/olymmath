After experimenting with the imaginary unit, Kolya Vasin moved on to the complex exponential. Using the formulas from problem $\underline{61115}$, he managed to prove that $\sin x$ is always zero and $\cos x$ is always one:

Where is the mistake in the given equalities?

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \sin x=\frac{e^{i x}-e^{-i x}}{2 i}=\frac{e^{(2 \pi i) \frac{x}{2 \pi}}-e^{(-2 \pi i) \frac{x}{2 \pi}}}{2 i}=\frac{1-1}{2 i}=0 \\
& \cos x=\frac{e^{i x}+e^{-i x}}{2}=\frac{e^{(2 \pi i) \frac{x}{2 \pi}}+e^{(-2 \pi i) \frac{x}{2 \pi}}}{2}=\frac{1+1}{2}=1
\end{aligned}
$$

Letters (from 6th grade. 1 point). At the post office, a courier sends 100 letters. Letters weighing more than 100 g are not accepted. The cost of sending depends on the weight of the letter. The minimum cost (for letters weighing up to 20 g inclusive) is 22 rubles, and for each subsequent full or partial 20 g, an additional 2 rubles 50 kopecks is added (see table).

The first postal clerk ${ }^{1}$ believes that it is necessary to weigh each of the 100 letters separately, find the cost of sending each according to the table, and then add up all the amounts.

| Postal tariffs for simple letters | |
| :---: | :---: |
| Weight, r | Price, $\mathrm{p}$ |
| up to 20 | 22.00 |
| from 21 to 40 | 24.50 |
| from 41 to 60 | 27.00 |
| from 61 to 80 | 29.50 |
| from 81 to 100 | 32.00 |

The second clerk suggests weighing all the letters at once, finding the average weight of a letter, determining the cost of sending according to the table, and multiplying the result by 100. She says this will give the same total, only faster. Is the second clerk right?

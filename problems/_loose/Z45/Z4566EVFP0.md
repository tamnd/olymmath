In the field mathematicus there is an infinite alignment of flowers.

A grasshopper moves from flower to flower. At each jump, it can land on the adjacent flower (if it is on flower $n$ it can jump to flower $n-1$ or $n+1$). Initially, the grasshopper is on flower 0.

In how many ways can it jump $k$ times, knowing that after its $k$ jumps, it must return to flower 0?

In how many ways can it jump $k$ times, knowing that after its $k$ jumps, it must return to flower 0 without ever having landed on a strictly negative flower?

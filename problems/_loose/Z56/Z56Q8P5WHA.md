Let $ABC$ be a triangle and let $A', B'$, and $C'$ be points on the segments $[BC]$, $[CA]$, and $[AB]$ respectively, such that the lines $\left(AA'\right)$, $\left(BB'\right)$, and $\left(CC'\right)$ are concurrent. Let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$. Let $\omega_{A}$ be the circle tangent to the circle $\Gamma$ and tangent to the segment $[BC]$ at point $A'$. Let $P_{A}$ be the point of tangency of the circles $\omega_{A}$ and $\Gamma$. Let $\omega_{B}$ be the circle tangent to the circle $\Gamma$ and tangent to the segment $[CA]$ at point $B'$. Let $P_{B}$ be the point of tangency of the circles $\omega_{B}$ and $\Gamma$. Let $\omega_{C}$ be the circle tangent to the circle $\Gamma$ and tangent to the segment $[AB]$ at point $C'$. Let $P_{C}$ be the point of tangency of the circles $\omega_{C}$ and $\Gamma$. Show that the lines $\left(\mathrm{AP}_{\mathrm{A}}\right)$, $\left(\mathrm{BP}_{\mathrm{B}}\right)$, and $\left(\mathrm{CP}_{\mathrm{C}}\right)$ are concurrent.

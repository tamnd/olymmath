Assume that from $n$ boxes ($n$ being any natural number greater than 0), their dimensions have the following properties:

The first box can fit into the second (if $n \geq 2$); the first two boxes can fit side by side into the third (if $n \geq 3$); the first three boxes can fit side by side into the fourth (if $n \geq 4$); ...; the first $n-1$ boxes can fit side by side into the $n$-th box.

Prove that it is possible to nest such $n$ boxes in a way that the following conditions are met: (1) Each box contains an even number of other boxes inside it (where 0 is also considered an even number).

(2) There are at most two boxes that are not contained in any other box.

(3) If we consider for each box the set of all boxes contained within it, there are at most two boxes in this set that are not contained in any other box in this set.

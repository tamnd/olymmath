The sixteenth question: If integers $a, b$ satisfy $ab+1$ being a perfect square, then $a, b$ are called "square makers". Does there exist a positive integer $\mathrm{n}$ such that the set $\{1,2, \ldots, 2 \mathrm{n}\}$ can be partitioned into $\mathrm{n}$ pairs of square makers? If such $\mathrm{n}$ exists, find all such positive integers $\mathrm{n}$; if not, explain the reason.

In the Gaussian plane, a complex number

$$
z=u+i \cdot v=r(\cos \varphi+i \sin \varphi)
$$

is represented by a point with coordinates $u=r \cos \varphi$ and $v=r \sin \varphi$. Here, the u-axis is referred to as the real axis and the v-axis as the imaginary axis.

Between the line that bisects the angle between the axes in the first quadrant and the positive real axis, there is a set of points whose elements have integer coordinates $u ; v$. These elements have the property that each of them is uniquely associated with a Pythagorean triple; the numbers of the triple correspond to the sides of a right-angled triangle whose vertices are the origin, the point $z^{2}$, and the foot of the perpendicular from $z^{2}$ to the real axis.

Why do the points on the mentioned angle bisector with integer coordinates $u ; v$ not belong to the mentioned set of points?

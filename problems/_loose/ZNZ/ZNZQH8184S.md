Given $\triangle A B C$, let $p=\frac{1}{2}(A B+B C+C A)$, circle $\Gamma_{1}$ is the excircle of angle $A$ (tangent to segment $B C$, and to the rays $A B$ and $A C$), circle $\Gamma$ is tangent to circle $\Gamma_{1}$ and the incircle $\Gamma_{2}$ of $\triangle A B C$ at points $Q$ and $P$ respectively, line $P Q$ intersects the angle bisector of $\angle B A C$ at $R$, and $R T$ is a tangent to circle $\Gamma$. Prove that $R T=\sqrt{p(p-a)}$, where $a=B C$.
(1994 Winter Bulgarian Grade 12 Olympiad)

Prove that for any natural number $k$, the equation

$$
x^{2}=y^{2}+k \cdot z^{2}
$$

has infinitely many "essentially different" solutions in the integers. (We call the solutions $(a, b, c)$ and $\left(a_{1}, b_{1}, c_{1}\right)$ essentially different if there is no rational number $r$ such that $a_{1}=r \cdot a, b_{1}=r \cdot b$, $c_{1}=r \cdot c$.)

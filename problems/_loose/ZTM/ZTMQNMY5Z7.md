Let the intersection points of a line parallel to the side $AB$ of the square $ABCD$ with the extensions of the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ be denoted by $K$ and $L$, respectively. Let the intersection points of the lines $AL$ and $BK$, as well as $CL$ and $DK$, be denoted by $M$ and $N$, respectively. Finally, let the intersection points of the lines through $M$ and $N$ parallel to $AB$ with the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ be denoted by $P$ and $Q$, and $R$ and $S$, respectively. Prove that the points $C, D, N, P, Q$, and the points $A, B, M, R, S$ lie on two parabolas, respectively. - Can the focus of such a parabola be at the midpoint of the side $CD$?

Given an infinite sequence of real numbers $x_0, x_1, x_2, \ldots$ where for $i \ge 0$,
$$x_{i+1} = \frac{4x_i^3 - 1}{6x_i^2 - 3},$$
there are exactly three possible real values, $a < b < c$, that $\lim_{n\to\infty} x_n$ may converge to, depending on $x_0$. A real number $m$ satisfies the condition that, for all $x_0 < m$, the sequence converges to $a$. Find the maximum possible value of $m + a$.

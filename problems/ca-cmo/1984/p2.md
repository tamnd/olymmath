Alice and Bob are in a hardware store. The store sells coloured sleeves that fit over keys to distinguish them. The following conversation takes place:
<span style="color:#0000FF">Alice:</span> Are you going to cover your keys?
<span style="color:#FF0000">Bob:</span> I would like to, but there are only $7$ colours and I have $8$ keys.
<span style="color:#0000FF">Alice:</span> Yes, but you could always distinguish a key by noticing that the red key next to the green key was different from the red key next to the blue key.
<span style="color:#FF0000">Bob:</span> You must be careful what you mean by "*next to*" or "*three keys over from*" since you can turn the key ring over and the keys are arranged in a circle.
<span style="color:#0000FF">Alice:</span> Even so, you don't need $8$ colours.**Problem:** What is the smallest number of colours needed to distinguish $n$ keys if all the keys are to be covered.

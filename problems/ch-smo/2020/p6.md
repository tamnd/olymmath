Let $n\geq 2$ be an integer. Consider the following game: Initially, $k$ stones are distributed among the $n^2$ squares of an $n\times n$ chessboard. A move consists of choosing a square containing at least as many stones as the number of its adjacent squares (two squares are *adjacent* if they share a common edge) and moving one stone from this square to each of its adjacent squares. Determine all positive integers $k$ such that:

a) There is an initial configuration with $k$ stones such that no move is possible.

b) There is an initial configuration with $k$ stones such that an infinite sequence of moves is possible.

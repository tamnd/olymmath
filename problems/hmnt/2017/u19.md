The skeletal structure of coronene, a hydrocarbon with the chemical formula $\mathrm{C}_{24} \mathrm{H}_{12}$, is shown below.
![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/2025_01_24_7c0dacf8fa3bedb48a75g-05.jpg?height=644&width=667&top_left_y=1700&top_left_x=756)

Each line segment between two atoms is at least a single bond. However, since each carbon (C) requires exactly four bonds connected to it and each hydrogen $(\mathrm{H})$ requires exactly one bond, some of the line segments are actually double bonds. How many arrangements of single/double bonds are there such that the above requirements are satisfied?

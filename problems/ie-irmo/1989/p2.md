A $3\times3$ magic square, with magic number $m$, is a $3\times3$ matrix such that the entries on each row, each column and each diagonal sum to $m$. Show that if the square has positive integer entries, then $m$ is divisible by $3$, and each entry of the square is at most $2n-1$, where $m=3n$. [An example of a magic square with $m=6$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
2&1&3\\
3&2&1\\
1&3&2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
]

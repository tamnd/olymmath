Given a complex number $z=x+iy$ ($x,y$ real), we denote by $P(z)$ the corresponding point $(x,y)$ in the plane. Suppose $z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4,z_5,\alpha$ are nonzero complex numbers such that

(a) $P(z_1)$, $P(z_2)$, $P(z_3)$, $P(z_4)$, $P(z_5)$ are the vertices of a convex pentagon $Q$ containing the origin $0$ in its interior and

(b) $P(\alpha z_1)$, $P(\alpha z_2)$, $P(\alpha z_3)$, $P(\alpha z_4)$ and $P(\alpha z_5)$ are all inside $Q$.

If $\alpha=p+iq$, where $p$ and $q$ are real, prove that $p^2+q^2\leq 1$ and that
$$
p+q\tan(\pi/5)\leq 1.
$$

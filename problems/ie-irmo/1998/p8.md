Let $\mathbb{N}$ be the set of all natural numbers (i.e., the positive integers).

(a) Prove that $\mathbb{N}$ can be written as a union of three mutually disjoint sets such that, if $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $|m-n|=2$ or $5$, then $m$ and $n$ are in different sets.

(b) Prove that $\mathbb{N}$ can be written as a union of four mutually disjoint sets such that, if $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $|m-n|=2$, $3$ or $5$, then $m$ and $n$ are in different sets. Show, however, that it is impossible to write $\mathbb{N}$ as a union of three mutually disjoint sets with this property.

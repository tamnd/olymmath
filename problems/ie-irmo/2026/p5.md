For each positive integer $n$, consider $3n$ distinct points on a circle, labelled in clockwise order $1,2,\ldots,3n$. An $n$-triangle configuration is a way to split these $3n$ points into $n$ sets of $3$ points each, and connect the $3$ points in each set to form a triangle, such that no two of the $n$ triangles intersect.

Let $S(n)$ be the number of all possible $n$-triangle configurations. For example, $S(1)=1$ because there are only three points, and $S(2)=3$ corresponding to the three configurations $\{123,456\}$, $\{234,561\}$ and $\{345,612\}$.

Find all positive integers $n$ for which $S(n)$ is not a multiple of $3$.

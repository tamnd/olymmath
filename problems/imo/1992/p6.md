For each positive integer n, S(n) is defined as the greatest integer such that for every positive integer k ≤ S(n), $n^2$ can be written as the sum of k positive squares. (a) Prove that S(n) ≤ $n^2$ - 14 for each n ≥ 4. (b) Find an integer n such that S(n) = $n^2$ - 14. (c) Prove that there are infinitely many integers n such that S(n) = $n^2$ - 14.

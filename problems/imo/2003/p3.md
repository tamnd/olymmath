A3 (GEO) Consider pairs of sequences of positive real numbers $a_{1} \geq$ $a_{2} \geq a_{3} \geq \cdots, b_{1} \geq b_{2} \geq b_{3} \geq \cdots$ and the sums $A_{n}=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}$, $B_{n}=b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}, n=1,2, \ldots$. For any pair define $c_{i}=\min \left\{a_{i}, b_{i}\right\}$ and $C_{n}=c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}, n=1,2, \ldots$ (a) Does there exist a pair $\left(a_{i}\right)_{i \geq 1},\left(b_{i}\right)_{i \geq 1}$ such that the sequences $\left(A_{n}\right)_{n \geq 1}$ and $\left(B_{n}\right)_{n \geq 1}$ are unbounded while the sequence $\left(C_{n}\right)_{n \geq 1}$ is bounded? (b) Does the answer to question (1) change by assuming additionally that $b_{i}=1 / i, i=1,2, \ldots ?$ Justify your answer.

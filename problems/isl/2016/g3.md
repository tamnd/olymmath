Let $B=(-1,0)$ and $C=(1,0)$ be fixed points on the coordinate plane. A nonempty, bounded subset $S$ of the plane is said to be nice if (i) there is a point $T$ in $S$ such that for every point $Q$ in $S$, the segment $T Q$ lies entirely in $S$; and (ii) for any triangle $P_{1} P_{2} P_{3}$, there exists a unique point $A$ in $S$ and a permutation $\sigma$ of the indices $\{1,2,3\}$ for which triangles $A B C$ and $P_{\sigma(1)} P_{\sigma(2)} P_{\sigma(3)}$ are similar. Prove that there exist two distinct nice subsets $S$ and $S^{\prime}$ of the set $\{(x, y): x \geqslant 0, y \geqslant 0\}$ such that if $A \in S$ and $A^{\prime} \in S^{\prime}$ are the unique choices of points in (ii), then the product $B A \cdot B A^{\prime}$ is a constant independent of the triangle $P_{1} P_{2} P_{3}$.

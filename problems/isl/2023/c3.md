Let $n$ be a positive integer. We arrange $1+2+\cdots+n$ circles in a triangle with $n$ rows, such that the $i^{\text {th }}$ row contains exactly $i$ circles. The following figure shows the case $n=6$. ![](https://cdn.mathpix.com/cropped/2025_01_09_9538693ed865d47d2de5g-40.jpg?height=398&width=381&top_left_y=401&top_left_x=843) In this triangle, a ninja-path is a sequence of circles obtained by repeatedly going from a circle to one of the two circles directly below it. In terms of $n$, find the largest value of $k$ such that if one circle from every row is coloured red, we can always find a ninja-path in which at least $k$ of the circles are red. (Netherlands)

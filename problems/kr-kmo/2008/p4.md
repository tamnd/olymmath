We define $A, B, C$ as a *partition* of $\mathbb{N}$ if $A,B,C$ satisfies the following.
(i) $A, B, C \not= \phi$ (ii) $A \cap B = B \cap C = C \cap A = \phi$ (iii) $A \cup B \cup C = \mathbb{N}$ .

Prove that the partition of $\mathbb{N}$ satisfying the following does not exist.
(i) $\forall$ $a \in A, b \in B$ , we have $a+b+2008 \in C$ (ii) $\forall$ $b \in B, c \in C$ , we have $b+c+2008 \in A$ (iii) $\forall$ $c \in C, a \in A$ , we have $c+a+2008 \in B$

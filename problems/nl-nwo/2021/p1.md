Niek has $16$ square cards that are yellow on one side and red on the other. He puts down the cards to form a $4 \times 4$ -square. Some of the cards show their yellow side and some show their red side. For a colour pattern he calculates the *monochromaticity* as follows. For every pair of adjacent cards that share a side he counts $+1$ or $-1$ according to the following rule: $+1$ if the adjacent cards show the same colour, and $-1$ if the adjacent cards show different colours. Adding this all together gives the monochromaticity (which might be negative). For example, if he lays down the cards as below, there are $15$ pairs of adjacent cards showing the same colour, and $9$ such pairs showing different colours.

The monochromaticity of this pattern is thus $15 \cdot (+1) + 9 \cdot (-1) = 6$ . Niek investigates all possible colour patterns and makes a list of all possible numbers that appear at least once as a value of the monochromaticity. That is, Niek makes a list with all numbers such that there exists a colour pattern that has this number as its monochromaticity.

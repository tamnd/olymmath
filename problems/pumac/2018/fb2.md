Aumann, Bill, and Charlie each roll a fair $6$-sided die with sides labeled $1$ through $6$ and look at their individual rolls. Each flips a fair coin and, depending on the outcome, looks at the roll of either the player to his right or the person to his left, without anyone else knowing which die he observed. Then, at the same time, each of the three players states the expected value of the sum of the rolls based on the information he has. After hearing what everyone said, the three players again state the expected value of the sum of the rolls based on the information they have. Then, for the third time, after hearing what everyone said, the three players again state the expected value of the sum of the rolls based on the information they have. Prove that Aumann, Bill, and Charlie say the same number the third time.

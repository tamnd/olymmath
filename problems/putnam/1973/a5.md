A particle moves in $3$ -space according to the equations: $$ \frac{dx}{dt} =yz, \frac{dy}{dt} =xz, \frac{dz}{dt}= xy. $$ Show that:
(a) If two of $x(0), y(0), z(0)$ equal $0,$ then the particle never moves.
(b) If $x(0)=y(0)=1, z(0)=0,$ then the solution is $$ x(t)= \sec t , y(t) =\sec t , z(t)= \tan t; $$ whereas if $x(0)=y(0)=1, z(0)=-1,$ then $$ x(t) =\frac{1}{t+1} , y(t)=\frac{1}{t+1}, z(t)=- \frac{1}{t+1}. $$ (c) If at least two of the values $x(0), y(0), z(0)$ are different from zero, then either the particle
moves to infinity at some finite time in the future, or it came from infinity at some finite
time in the past (a point $(x, y, z)$ in $3$ -space "moves to infinity" if its distance from the
origin approaches infinity).

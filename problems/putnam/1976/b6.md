B-6. As usual, let $ \sigma(N) $ denote the sum of all the (positive integral) divisors of $ N $. (Included among these divisors are $ I $ and $ N $ itself.) For example, if $ p $ is a prime, then $ \sigma(p)=p+1 $. Motivated by the notion of a "perfect" number, a positive integer $ N $ is called "quasiperfect" if $ \sigma(N)=2 N+1 $. Prove that every quasiperfect number is the square of an odd integer.

The number $ d_1d_2\ldots d_9 $ has nine (not necessarily distinct) decimal digits. The number $ e_1e_2\ldots e_9 $ is such that each of the nine 9-digit numbers formed by replacing just one of the digits $ d_i $ is $ d_1d_2\ldots d_9 $ by the corresponding digit $ e_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq 9 $) is divisible by 7. The number $ f_1f_2\ldots f_9 $ is related to $ e_1e_2\ldots e_9 $ in the same way: that is, each of the nine numbers formed by replacing one of the $ e_i $ by the corresponding $ f_i $ is divisible by 7. Show that, for each $ i $, $ d_i - f_i $ is divisible by 7. [For example, if $ d_1d_2\ldots d_9 = 199501996 $, then $ e_6 $ may be 2 or 9, since 199502996 and 199509996 are multiples of 7.]

Let $f : [0, 1]^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ be a continuous function on the closed unit square such that $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ exist and are continuous on the interior $(0, 1)^2$. Let $a = \int_0^1 f(0, y) dy$, $b = \int_0^1 f(1, y) dy$, $c = \int_0^1 f(x, 0) dx$, $d = \int_0^1 f(x, 1) dx$. Prove or disprove: There must be a point $(x_0, y_0)$ in $(0, 1)^2$ such that
$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x_0, y_0) = b - a \text{and} \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x_0, y_0) = d - c.
$$

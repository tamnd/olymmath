Suppose that the function $h:\mathbb{R}^2\to \mathbb{R}$ has continuous partial derivatives and satisfies the equation $$ h(x,y) = a \frac{\partial h}{\partial x}(x,y) + b \frac{\partial h}{\partial y}(x,y) $$ for some constants $a,b$. Prove that if there is a constant $M$ such that $|h(x,y)|\leq M$ for all $(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, then $h$ is identically zero.

Suppose that the function $ h : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R} $ has continuous partial derivatives and satisfies the equation
$$
h(x,y) = a \frac{\partial h}{\partial x}(x,y) + b \frac{\partial h}{\partial y}(x,y)
$$
for some constants $ a, b $. Prove that if there is a constant $ M $ such that $ |h(x,y)| \leq M $ for all $ (x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 $, then $ h $ is identically zero.

Let $F: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ and $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be twice continuously differentiable functions with the following properties:
- $F(u,u) = 0$ for every $u \in \mathbb{R}$;
- for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $g(x) > 0$ and $x^2 g(x) \leq 1$;
- for every $(u,v) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, the vector $\nabla F(u,v)$ is either $\mathbf{0}$ or parallel to the vector $(g(u), -g(v))$.

Prove that there exists a constant $C$ such that for every $n \geq 2$ and any $x_1, \ldots, x_{n+1} \in \mathbb{R}$, we have
$$\min_{i \neq j} |F(x_i, x_j)| \leq \frac{C}{n}.$$

Let $ \mathcal{P} $ be a nonempty collection of subsets of $ \{1, \ldots, n\} $ such that:
(i) if $ S, S' \in \mathcal{P} $, then $ S \cup S' \in \mathcal{P} $ and $ S \cap S' \in \mathcal{P} $, and
(ii) if $ S \in \mathcal{P} $ and $ S \neq \emptyset $, then there is a subset $ T \subset S $ such that $ T \in \mathcal{P} $ and $ T $ contains exactly one fewer element than $ S $.

Suppose that $ f : \mathcal{P} \to \mathbb{R} $ is a function such that $ f(\emptyset) = 0 $ and
$$
f(S \cup S') = f(S) + f(S') - f(S \cap S')
$$
for all $ S, S' \in \mathcal{P} $.

Must there exist real numbers $ f_1, \ldots, f_n $ such that
$$
f(S) = \sum_{i \in S} f_i
$$
for every $ S \in \mathcal{P} $?<|end_of_box|>

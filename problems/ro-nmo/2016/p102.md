Let be a function $ f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R} $ satisfying the conditions: $$ \left\{\begin{matrix} f(x+y) &\le & f(x)+f(y) f(tx+(1-t)y) &\le & t(f(x)) +(1-t)f(y) \end{matrix}\right. , $$ for all real numbers $ x,y,t $ with $ t\in [0,1] . $ Prove that:**a)** $ f(b)+f(c)\le f(a)+f(d) , $ for any real numbers $ a,b,c,d $ such that $ a\le b\le c\le d $ and $ d-c=b-a. $ **b)** for any natural number $ n\ge 3 $ and any $ n $ real numbers $ x_1,x_2,\ldots ,x_n, $ the following inequality holds. $$ f\left( \sum_{1\le i\le n} x_i \right) +(n-2)\sum_{1\le i\le n} f\left( x_i \right)\ge \sum_{1\le i<j\le n} f\left( x_i+x_j \right) $$

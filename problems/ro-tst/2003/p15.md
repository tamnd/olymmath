In a plane we choose a cartesian system of coordinates. A point $A(x,y)$ in the plane is called an integer point if and only if both $x$ and $y$ are integers. An integer point $A$ is called invisible if on the segment $(OA)$ there is at least one integer point.
Prove that for each positive integer $n$ there exists a square of side $n$ in which all the interior integer points are invisible.

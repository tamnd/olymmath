The teacher has assigned grades on a scale from 0 to 100. The academic department can change the upper limit of the scale to any other natural number, recalculating the grades proportionally and rounding to the nearest integers. A non-integer number is rounded to the nearest integer; if the fractional part is 0.5, the academic department can choose any direction of rounding, separately for each grade. (For example, a grade of 37 on a scale of 100, when recalculated to a scale of 40, becomes $37 \cdot 40 / 100 = 14.8$ and will be rounded to 15.)

Students Petya and Vasya received grades $a$ and $b$, different from 0 and 100. Prove that the academic department can perform several recalculations so that Petya's grade becomes $b$, and Vasya's grade becomes $a$ (both grades are recalculated simultaneously).

*(B. Vysokanov, N. Yu. Medved, V. Bragin)*

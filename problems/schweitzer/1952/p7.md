A point $ P$ is performing a random walk on the $ X$ -axis. At the instant $ t=0$ , $ P$ is at a point $ x_0$ ( $ |x_0|\le N$ , where $ x_0$ and $ N$ denote integers, $ N>0$ ). If at an instant $ t$ ( $ t$ being a nonnegative integer), $ P$ is at a point of $ x$ integer abscissa and $ |x|<N$ , then by the instant $ t+1$ it reaches either the point $ x+1$ or the point $ x-1$ , each with probability $ \frac12$ . If at the instant $ t$ , $ P$ is at the point $ x=N$ [ $ x=-N$ ], then by the instant $ t+1$ it is certain to reach the point $ N-1$ [ $ -N+1$ ]. Denote by $ P_k(t)$ the probability of $ P$ being at $ x=k$ at instant $ t$ ( $ k$ is an integer). Find $ \lim_{t\to \infty}P_{k}(2t)$ and $ \lim_{t\to \infty}P_k(2t+1)$ for every fixed $ k$ .

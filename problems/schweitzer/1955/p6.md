For a prime factorisation of a positive integer $N$ let us call the exponent of a prime $p$ the integer $k$ for which $p^{k} \mid N$ but $p^{k+1} \nmid N$ ; let, further, the power $p^{k}$ be called the "contribution" of $p$ to $N$ . Show that for any positive integer $n$ and for any primes $p$ and $q$ the contibution of $p$ to $n!$ is greater than the contribution of $q$ if and only if the exponent of $p$ is greater than that of $q$ .

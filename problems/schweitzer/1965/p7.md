Prove that any uncountable subset of the Euclidean $ n$ -space contains an countable subset with the property that the distances between different pairs of points are different (that is, for any points $ P_1 \not= P_2$ and $ Q_1\not= Q_2$ of this subset, $ \overline{P_1P_2}=\overline{Q_1Q_2}$ implies either $ P_1=Q_1$ and $ P_2=Q_2$ , or $ P_1=Q_2$ and $ P_2=Q_1$ ). Show that a similar statement is not valid if the Euclidean $ n$ -space is replaced with a (separable) Hilbert space.

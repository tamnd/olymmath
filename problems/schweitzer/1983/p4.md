For which cardinalities $ \kappa$ do antimetric spaces of cardinality $ \kappa$ exist?
$ (X,\varrho)$ is called an $ \textit{antimetric space}$ if $ X$ is a nonempty set, $ \varrho : X^2 \rightarrow [0,\infty)$ is a symmetric map, $ \varrho(x,y)=0$ holds iff $ x=y$ , and for any three-element subset $ \{a_1,a_2,a_3 \}$ of $ X$ $$ \varrho(a_{1f},a_{2f})+\varrho(a_{2f},a_{3f}) < \varrho(a_{1f},a_{3f})$$ holds for some permutation $ f$ of $ \{1,2,3 \}$ .

Let $a,b,c$ be real numbers satisfying $a<b<c,a+b+c=6,ab+bc+ac=9$ . Prove that $0<a<1<b<3<c<4$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>Let $abc=k$ , then $a,b,c\ (a<b<c)$ are the roots of cubic equation $x^3-6x^2+9x-k=0\Longleftrightarrow x(x-3)^2=k$ that is to say, $a,b,c\ (a<b<c)$ are the $x$ -coordinates of the interception of points between $y=x(x-3)^2$ and $y=k$ . $y=x(x-3)^2$ have local maximuml value of $4$ at $x=1$ and local minimum value of $0$ at $x=3$ .

Since the $x$ -coordinate of the interception point between $y=x(x-3)^2$ and $y=4$ which is the tangent line at

local maximum point $(1,4)$ is a point $(4,4)$ ,Moving the line $y=k$ so that the two graphs $y=x(x-3)^2$ and $y=k$ have the distinct three interception points,we can find that the range of $a,b,c$ are $0<a<1,1<b<3,3<c<4
$ ,we are done.</details>

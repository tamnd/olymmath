The function $f(x)$ has the property that, for some real positive constant $C$, the expression
$$
\frac{f^{(n)}(x)}{n+x+C}
$$
is independent of $n$ for all nonnegative integers $n$, provided that $n+x+C\ne 0$. Given that $f'(0)=1$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=C+(e-2)$, determine the value of $C$.

Note: $f^{(n)}(x)$ is the $n$-th derivative of $f(x)$, and $f^{(0)}(x)$ is defined to be $f(x)$.

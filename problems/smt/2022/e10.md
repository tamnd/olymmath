You need to bike to class but don’t know where you parked your bike. There are two bike racks, $A$ and $B$. There is a $1/5$ chance for your bike to be at $A$; it takes one minute to walk to $A$ and four minutes to bike from $A$ to class. Then, there is a $4/5$ chance for your bike to be at $B$; it takes three minutes to walk to $B$ and five minutes to bike from $B$ to class. However, if your choice is wrong, you need to walk from your original choice $A$ or $B$ to the other, which takes four minutes, before departing to class from there.
Suppose you only care about getting to class on time. For a some interval of minutes before class, going to bike rack $B$ first gives a strictly higher chance of making it to class on time. How many minutes long is that interval (i.e. an interval of 15 minutes before class to 21 minutes before class has length 6)?

Stanford is building a new dorm for students, and they are looking to offer $2$ room configurations:

- Configuration A: a one-room double, which is a square with side length of $x$;
- Configuration B: a two-room double, which is two connected rooms, each of them squares with a side length of $y$.

To make things fair for everyone, Stanford wants a one-room double (rooms of configuration A) to be exactly $1\text{m}^2$ larger than the total area of a two-room double. Find the number of possible pairs of side lengths $(x,y)$, where $x\in\mathbb{N}$, $y\in\mathbb{N}$, such that $x-y<2022$.

Let $F$ be a set of subsets of $\{1,2,3\}$. $F$ is called distinguishing if each of $1$, $2$, and $3$ are distinguishable from each other—that is, $1$, $2$, and $3$ are each in a distinct set of subsets from each other. For example $F=\{\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}$ is distinguishing because $1$ is in $\{1,3\}$, $2$ is in $\{2,3\}$, and $3$ is in $\{1,3\}$ and $\{2,3\}$. $F=\{\{1,2\},\{2\}\}$ is also distinguishing: $1$ is in $\{1,2\}$, $2$ is in $\{1,2\}$ and $\{2\}$, and $3$ is in none of the subsets.

On the other hand, $F=\{\{1\},\{2,3\}\}$ is not distinguishing. Both $2$ and $3$ are only in $\{2,3\}$, so they cannot be distinguished from each other.

How many distinguishing sets of subsets of $\{1,2,3\}$ are there?

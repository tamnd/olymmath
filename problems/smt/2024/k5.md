Charlie chooses a real number $c>0$. Bob chooses a real number $b$ from the interval $(0,2c)$ uniformly at random. Alice chooses a real number $\tilde{a}$ from the interval $(0,2\sqrt{c})$ uniformly at random. Let $a=\tilde{a}^2$. What is the probability that the roots of $ax^2+bx+c$ have nonzero imaginary parts and have real parts with absolute value greater than $1$?

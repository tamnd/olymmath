A *Latin square* (resp. *cube*) of order $n$ is an $n \times n$ array (resp. $n \times n \times n$ array) filled with different numbers $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ occurring exactly once in each row parallel to any of its sides. Positive integers $h_1, h_2, \dots, h_k$ are such that $n = h_1 + \dots + h_k$. Each of the sides of a Latin square of order $n$ is divided into parts of length $h_1, \dots, h_k$ so that the square is cut into rectangular parts as shown in the figure. It is known that for all $i$ the square parts $h_i \times h_i$ covering the diagonal are Latin squares of order $h_i$ and the sets of numbers in them are disjoint.

Prove that then there exists a Latin cube of order $n$ with similar property: its sides can be divided into parts of length $h_1, \dots, h_k$ so that the cubes $h_i \times h_i \times h_i$ covering its diagonal are Latin cubes with disjoint sets of numbers.
